# 11. PEDAGOGY RULES

- **Always say which cluster:** every command, diagram, and result names the cluster(s) involved; every lab ends with a **per-cluster verification matrix**; teach the habit that a result from one cluster says nothing about the other.
- **Blast radius first:** every fleet-wide mechanism (GitOps fan-out, policy rollout, DNS change, template change, credential) is taught with its blast radius, the ring/gate that bounds it, and the halt/rollback path.
- **Measure the failover, not the switch:** failover is measured end to end (detection, propagation, client tail, warm-up, data safety), never by "the DNS record changed".
- **Name the consistency choice and the anomaly:** every replication design states the guarantee, the lag/RPO, and the user-visible anomaly it permits, demonstrated in a lab.
- **Management plane ≠ data plane:** every unit asks "what keeps working when the hub/controller/DNS/observability hub is down?" and tests it.
- **One running example** (two-region data/ML platform) evolving through the folder: one cluster → deliberately topologized → lifecycle-managed → GitOps fleet with rings → cross-cluster connected (carefully) → globally routed (simulated) → regionally patterned → consistency-explicit → fleet-observed and policy-governed → failed over and failed back with measurements.
- **Show failure on purpose:** every unit has at least one lab where multi-cluster practice fails or surprises (wrong-cluster command, overlapping CIDRs, ApplicationSet cascade delete, bad ring change, stale cross-cluster endpoint, DNS client tail, health-check blackhole, lost acknowledged writes, averaged-away regional outage, non-conformant cluster) and the learner finds and fixes the cause — always preceded by guards, a safety plan, and abort steps.
- **Diagrams** for cluster boundaries and blast radius, fleet registry and CIDR plan, hub-and-spoke vs agent GitOps with credential flows, ring rollout with gates, cross-cluster connectivity options and trust, failover-time anatomy timeline, GSLB/DNS/proxy paths with TTL/caching layers, active-active vs active-passive vs cell designs with capacity bars (N+1), replication topologies and quorum placement, consistency anomalies timeline (stale read, lost write, lost update), fleet observability aggregation tiers and hub-failure behavior, and policy/conformance distribution with rings.
- Plain, precise English; no filler or fear-mongering; define acronyms at first use; consistent terms (*cluster, fleet, hub, spoke, region, cell, ring, wave, blast radius, failure domain, context, CIDR, east-west, GSLB, TTL, RTA, RPA, lag, RPO, quorum, witness, conformance*).
- Honest trade-offs; avoid "always/never" without reasons and exceptions; vendor-neutral and fair across tools; **explain, don't evangelize**; never imply that multi-cluster or multi-region is inherently better — it is a cost justified by requirements.
