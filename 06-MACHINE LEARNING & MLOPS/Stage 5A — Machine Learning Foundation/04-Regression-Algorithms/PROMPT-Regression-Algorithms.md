## Unit 2 — Linear regression and its assumptions

- **L1:** **the model `y = Xβ + ε`** (linear in the parameters; the design matrix may contain transformed features — Unit 4); **ordinary least squares (OLS): choose `β̂` to minimise `RSS(β) = ‖y − Xβ‖²`**; **the normal equations `XᵀXβ̂ = Xᵀy` (recall 4B-14 lesson `02-the-normal-equation-and-closed-form-solution.md`; here with the geometry)**; **the geometry (derived in L3): `ŷ` is the orthogonal projection of `y` onto the column space of `X`; residuals are orthogonal to every column; with an intercept the residuals sum to zero**; **a first worked example by hand: a 5-point simple regression — slope, intercept, fitted values, residuals, `RSS`, `R²` — and the same in NumPy (`lstsq`) and scikit-learn (`LinearRegression`: `coef_`, `intercept_`)**; **the assumptions (stated with plain-language consequences; each is revisited in L2)**: **(A1) linearity in the parameters (the mean of `y` given `x` is `xβ`); (A2) the errors have mean zero given `X` (no omitted systematic effect); (A3) independent errors; (A4) constant variance (homoscedasticity); (A5) no perfect collinearity (`X` has full column rank); (A6) normally distributed errors (needed only for exact small-sample inference)**; **prediction vs inference (two different jobs: assumptions A1–A2 matter for both; A3–A6 mostly for inference and intervals; a model can predict well while its coefficients are uninterpretable)**; **never invert `XᵀX` explicitly (use `lstsq`/QR: why, in L2)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
