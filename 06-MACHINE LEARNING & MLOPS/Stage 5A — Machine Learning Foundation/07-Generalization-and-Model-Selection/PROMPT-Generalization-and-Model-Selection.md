## Unit 1 — Underfitting, overfitting, and generalisation error

- **L1:** **generalisation error (recall 5A-01 Unit 4 and 5A-04 Unit 4)**: **the expected loss on *new* data from the same distribution, `R(h) = E[ℓ(h(X), Y)]`; the training (empirical) error `R̂_S(h)`; the generalisation gap `R(h) − R̂_S(h)`**; **model complexity (capacity): how flexible a model class is (polynomial degree, tree depth, number of neighbours `1/k`, `1/λ` in ridge, `C`, `γ`, number of features/parameters); more capacity can fit more patterns, including noise**; **underfitting (the model is too simple: both training and validation errors are high — close to the baseline or to each other and far above what is achievable)**; **overfitting (the model is too flexible: training error is low, validation error is much higher — it has memorised noise)**; **the irreducible error (noise floor `σ²` or Bayes error: no model can go below it; a validation error equal to the noise floor is not underfitting)**; **a first worked example by hand and in code: a synthetic curved target with known noise `σ = 0.3`; polynomial degrees 1, 3, 9 on 12 training points: training and validation MSE, the noise floor `0.09`, and the diagnosis of each**; **the complexity sweep (the module's first diagnostic): train and validation error across a grid of complexities, with intervals from ≥ 30 resampled training sets; the U-shape of validation error**; **reading numbers, not feelings (a diagnosis table: train error near baseline → underfit or no signal; train ≪ validation → overfit; train ≈ validation ≈ noise floor → good fit; validation ≪ train or implausibly good → suspect leakage)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **the four causes of a disappointing validation score (derived/measured; the module's signature table)**: **(a) overfitting (variance): fix by more data, a simpler model, regularisation, fewer features, early stopping, averaging (bagging: `../08-…`); ## Unit 2 — The bias–variance trade-off in practice

- **L1:** **the idea: a model's error on new data comes from three sources — the noise in the data (irreducible), the model's systematic error (bias: the model class cannot represent the truth), and its sensitivity to the particular training sample (variance: a different training set would give a different fit)**; **a first worked example with a tiny exact model: estimating a mean from `n = 3` Bernoulli(½) draws with the estimators `p̂ = x̄` and a shrunk `p̂ = (3x̄ + 1)/(3 + 2)`: enumerate all eight training sets, compute each estimator's bias, variance, and mean squared error exactly with `Fraction`, and see that the biased estimator can have lower MSE**; **the decomposition for squared error at a point `x` (derived in L3): `E[(Y − f̂(x))²] = σ² + (E f̂(x) − f(x))² + Var f̂(x)`, the expectation over training sets and noise; bias², variance, noise**; **what bias and variance look like (bias: a straight line fitted to a curve is wrong everywhere in the same direction; variance: a degree-9 polynomial fitted to 12 points wiggles differently for each training set — shown by fitting 20 resampled training sets and tabulating predictions at three test points)**; **the trade-off (more flexible models: lower bias, higher variance; the sum is U-shaped in complexity: a seeded sweep with a bias²/variance/noise table and intervals)**; **variance of the model vs noise in the evaluation (two different things: the first is how much the fitted model changes across training sets; the second is the sampling error of a performance estimate: Unit 3)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **estimating bias and variance (the module's toolbox)**: **(1) with a known generator (simulation): draw many training sets, fit, and compute at fixed test points the mean prediction, the bias², and the variance, with `n_rep` and intervals (exact in the limit; the module's main tool because the truth is known); (2) on real data (no known truth): bootstrap the training set and measure prediction variance across bootstrap fits (an estimate of variance only; bias requires a reference; the out-of-bag view: `../08-…`); (3) indirectly: the training–validation gap and the learning curve (Unit 6)**; **what moves each term (each shown by simulation and derived where exact)**: **model complexity (degree, depth, `1/k`, `1/λ`); regularisation (ridge: bias grows and variance shrinks with `λ`: the one-feature closed forms of 5A-04 Unit 5 recalled; the optimum `λ* = σ²/β²` for one feature); training size `n` (variance falls roughly like `1/n` for stable models; bias is unchanged); noisy features (add variance without reducing bias); k-NN (`k` small: low bias, high variance: variance of the neighbour mean is `σ²/k`; `k` large: high bias); ensembles (bagging: averaging `B` predictors with variance `σ_f²` and pairwise correlation `ρ` has variance `ρσ_f² + (1 − ρ)σ_f²/B`: derived; bias unchanged; reduces the variance component only; boosting reduces bias: named; `../08-…`)**; **0–1 loss and classification (the squared-error decomposition does not carry over additively; several decompositions exist (`CITED [VERIFY]` Domingos 2000; James & Hastie): for a classifier the *main prediction* and a "variance" in the sense of disagreement across training sets are still useful diagnostics; the module measures prediction instability (the fraction of test points whose predicted label changes across resampled training sets) as the classification analogue of variance, with intervals, and makes no additive claim)**; **diagnosing by symptoms (high variance: large training–validation gap, unstable predictions/coefficients across resamples, validation error improving with more data; high bias: training error high, small gap, both curves flat; the pair of cheap measurements: the gap and the resample-instability)**; **remedies mapped to terms (high variance → more data, regularise, simplify, bag, select features inside CV; high bias → richer features/model, reduce regularisation; noise-dominated → improve labels/features, accept the floor)**; **the trade-off is not a law (the decomposition is an identity; the claim that bias and variance trade off holds for many classical model families and fails for others (ensembles can reduce both; very flexible models can show non-monotone behaviour: named `CITED [VERIFY]`); the module says "in these families, on these generators")**; **bias–variance and cross-validation (the CV curve of validation error vs complexity is the empirical U; the K in K-fold also trades bias of the *estimate* against its variance: Unit 3)**; **bias–variance for the *procedure* (the hyperparameter search itself adds variance: the selected configuration varies across training sets: a seeded demonstration of selection variance; one reason nested CV targets the procedure: Unit 7)**; **communicating the trade-off to stakeholders (a standard three-number table: baseline, noise floor/target, observed error with its bias/variance reading; no folklore)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **squared-error decomposition: for `Y = f(x) + ε` with `E ε = 0`, `Var ε = σ²`, `ε` independent of the training set, and a predictor `f̂` fitted on an independent training set, `E[(Y − f̂(x))²] = σ² + (E f̂(x) − f(x))² + Var f̂(x)`** — complete (add and subtract `f(x)` and `E f̂(x)`; the cross terms vanish by independence and zero mean; **state where each hypothesis (independence of the new noise and the training set; zero-mean noise) is used**); (b) **estimator MSE decomposition `MSE(θ̂) = Bias² + Var` and the exact enumerated Bernoulli example (the shrunk estimator's MSE is lower for `p` near ½ and higher near 0 or 1: derive the crossing points)** — complete; (c) **averaging: for `B` predictors each with variance `v` and pairwise correlation `ρ`, the variance of their mean is `ρv + (1 − ρ)v/B`; the bias of the mean equals the common bias; the variance is bounded below by `ρv` — so averaging helps only to the extent that errors are decorrelated** — complete (covariance algebra); (d) **k-NN regression at a point with `k` neighbours whose true function values are `f(x_(j))`: `Var(f̂(x)) = σ²/k` (for fixed neighbour locations and independent noise) and the bias is `(1/k)Σf(x_(j)) − f(x)`; bias increases (for a non-linear `f`) as `k` grows because neighbours move away** — complete (conditional on neighbour locations; state the hypothesis); (e) **ridge in one dimension (recalled from 5A-04 Unit 5): with `ŷ = βx` fitted on `n` points with penalty `λ`: bias `= −βλ/(Σx² + λ)·x`, variance `= σ²Σx²x²/(Σx² + λ)²`-type: restate exactly, and the optimal `λ* = σ²/β²` for the stated design; verify numerically** — recalled and verified; (f) **for classification with 0–1 loss, no single additive decomposition into noise + bias² + variance exists with the squared-error properties; one consistent decomposition has a "variance" term that can *reduce* the error at points where the main prediction is wrong (a two-point worked example: `CITED [VERIFY]` Domingos; the learner computes it)** — complete for the worked example, `CITED` for the general statement; **counterexamples**: **a biased estimator with lower MSE (computed)**; **a bagged model with lower variance and the same bias (simulated; intervals)**; **bagging a high-bias model giving no improvement (simulated)**; **variance of a model confused with the noise of its validation estimate (computed)**; **numerical analysis**: **simulation vs closed form for the Bernoulli, ridge, and k-NN cases; bias²/variance estimates' own sampling error vs the number of simulated training sets**; **tool card** for `selectlab.biasvar` (simulation harness: generator, model factory, training-set sampler, fixed test points → bias², variance, noise, total, with intervals; exact enumerator for tiny discrete models; bootstrap variance estimator for real data; averaging-variance calculator; classification instability measure; closed-form ridge/k-NN references; spec-literal references; stable errors; no input mutation).
- **L4:** **bias–variance as a design tool (an MLOps lens)**: **choosing complexity from the bias/variance reading and the cost of data (a worked decision: collect more data vs regularise vs richer features: Unit 6)**; **monitoring instability (the retrain-diff monitor: how much do predictions on a frozen reference set change between retrains? a high value signals variance: a seeded demonstration; links 5A-04 Unit 3 coefficient stability and 5A-05 Unit 2)**; **seed variance in training (run-to-run variation from random initialisation or bagging: report across ≥ 5–30 seeds with intervals; never the best seed: 5A-01 Unit 4; named: 5C)**; **small-data regimes (variance dominates: strong regularisation and simple models: `JUDGEMENT`; Bayesian priors as regularisers: 4C-05)**; **bias from the label definition or the feature set (a systematic error no amount of data removes: `../12-…`; 5A-03 Unit 12)**; **fairness (bias and variance by group: a group with few examples has higher variance: per-group intervals: 5A-06 Unit 8)**; hostile inputs (a generator with `σ = 0`; a single training set; test points outside the training range (extrapolation: bias dominates); a model that fails to converge on some resamples; non-finite predictions); **exhaustive verification** of the decomposition **by exact enumeration over all training sets of size `n ≤ 4` for discrete generators (exact `Fraction`)**, of the averaging-variance formula **over a grid of `(v, ρ, B)` with exact covariance matrices**, and of the Bernoulli estimator crossings **over a grid of `p`** (domains named and sized; `EVIDENCE ONLY`); mutation lab (variance computed over test noise instead of training sets; bias² computed with the noisy target instead of `f`; noise floor omitted; `ddof` mismatch in the variance; training and test points sharing the training sample; averaging variance missing the `ρ` term; the same random seed for every simulated training set) with no surviving mutants; case study: a team that kept adding features to a high-variance model and a team that bagged a high-bias model expecting a gain (original synthetic reproductions with a bias/variance table and instability monitor as fixes).

## Unit 3 — Cross-validation fundamentals

- **L1:** **why cross-validation**: **a single train/validation split uses data inefficiently and gives a noisy estimate; cross-validation (CV) reuses every row for both training and validation, in turns, to estimate how a *procedure* generalises**; **K-fold CV (the algorithm)**: shuffle (or not) and partition the rows into `K` folds; for `k = 1…K` train on the other `K − 1` folds, score on fold `k`; report the aggregate of the `K` scores; **a first worked example by hand: 10 rows, `K = 5`: the fold membership table; each row is in exactly one validation fold; the training sets have 8 rows; a tiny regression with the five fold scores and their mean; the same in `KFold` and in your reference**; **variants (each defined): leave-one-out (LOO: `K = n`), repeated K-fold, shuffle-split (random train/validation partitions: rows may repeat across splits), stratified (Unit 4), grouped (Unit 4), time-series (Unit 4)**; **what CV estimates: the average performance of the *training procedure* applied to `(K − 1)n/K` rows — not the performance of the final model trained on all `n` rows (a slightly pessimistic estimate; Unit 3 L2/L3)**; **the final model is refit on all training rows; CV scores are for evaluation and selection, not a property of that final model**; **`cross_val_score` / `cross_validate` / `cross_val_predict` (documented behaviour `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`; the learner tests: which splitter is used by default for classifiers and regressors; whether the data are shuffled; what is returned; the scoring argument uses 5A-06 metric cards)**; **the test set is separate: CV happens inside the training portion (the frozen test set is never in any fold)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **CV in depth (each derived in L3 or measured)**: **the choice of `K` is a trade-off, not a law (a convention: 5 or 10): small `K` → each training set is smaller (more pessimistic bias) and cheaper; large `K`/LOO → nearly unbiased for the full-data procedure and expensive, and the `K` estimates are highly correlated (their mean can be high-variance: a seeded study across `K ∈ {2, 5, 10, n}` on several model families: the bias and variance of the CV estimate against the known true generalisation error (computed from the generator): `CITED [VERIFY]` Kohavi 1995 and Bengio–Grandvalet 2004 on the difficulty of estimating the variance of CV; the module makes no universal claim about which `K` is best)**; **the uncertainty of a CV estimate (the module's key warning): the `K` fold scores are not independent (training sets overlap, and validation folds are drawn from the same data); the naive standard error `SD/√K` **under**states the uncertainty; there is no unbiased estimator of the variance of the `K`-fold CV estimate (`CITED [VERIFY]` Bengio–Grandvalet); practical reporting: repeated CV (different partitions) to reduce the *partition* variance, the fold-to-fold SD as a *spread*, and, for model comparison, the **corrected resampled `t`** statistic (Nadeau–Bengio, `CITED [VERIFY]`: variance inflated by the factor `1/K_eff + n_test/n_train`-type term; the learner implements it and checks its Type-I error and coverage on a seeded generator against the naive version) or a paired bootstrap on a held-out set**; **paired model comparison (use identical folds for both models; analyse per-fold differences; McNemar on pooled correct/incorrect predictions is *not* valid across folds without care because training sets differ: state the caution; the preferred claims come from a held-out test set (5A-06) or the corrected `t`)**; **aggregating fold metrics (mean of per-fold scores vs a pooled score from out-of-fold predictions (`cross_val_predict`): equal for accuracy with equal fold sizes, different for AUC, F1, and log loss in general (derived and shown); `cross_val_predict` returns predictions produced by *different* models, so a pooled metric is not the score of any one model — use it for diagnostics, error analysis (`../12-…`), and stacking, not as the headline estimate: `[VERIFY]`; the module states the aggregation in the validation card)**; **repeated K-fold and shuffling (`shuffle=True` with a `random_state`; data ordered by time or class must be shuffled for i.i.d. questions and must **not** be shuffled for time questions: Unit 4; a measured effect of unshuffled folds on class-sorted data)**; **LOO and exact shortcuts (for linear models with a hat matrix, LOO predictions are `ŷ_i − ... = y_i − e_i/(1 − h_ii)`: the PRESS identity of 5A-04 Unit 3: no refitting; for k-NN, LOO by excluding the diagonal: 5A-05 Unit 5; `RidgeCV` uses this idea efficiently: `[VERIFY]`)**; **computational cost (`K` fits plus one refit; counted; parallelism `n_jobs` and its seed/ordering effects: results must not depend on `n_jobs`: a test)**; **CV for small data and for big data (small `n`: repeated CV; large `n`: a single holdout is often adequate, because a validation set of `n_val` rows already gives a narrow interval: sized with the Wilson/Hoeffding formulas: 5A-06 Unit 1; `JUDGEMENT`)**; **leakage inside CV (every learned step must be refit per training fold: Unit 7; the simplest violation: scaling or selecting before CV; a measured effect)**; **what CV cannot do (it cannot fix a wrong unit of generalisation: random folds on grouped or time data answer the wrong question: Unit 4; it cannot see shift; it does not replace the test set)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **K-fold partition properties: the validation folds partition the row index set `{1…n}` (each row appears in exactly one validation fold); fold sizes differ by at most 1 under the stated rule (`⌊n/K⌋` or `⌈n/K⌉`); every training set has `n − |F_k|` rows; the number of ordered partitions into folds of given sizes is a multinomial coefficient** — complete (counting); (b) **the pooled CV estimate equals the average of per-row out-of-fold losses; for per-row additive metrics (accuracy, MSE, log loss) with equal fold sizes the mean of fold means equals the pooled mean; with unequal fold sizes they differ unless weighted by fold size; for non-additive metrics (AUC, F1) the fold-mean and the pooled value differ in general (a computed counterexample)** — complete; (c) **the CV estimate is an unbiased estimator of the expected loss of the procedure trained on `n − n/K` rows: `E[CV_K] = E_S[R(A(S_{n(K−1)/K}))]` when the data are i.i.d. and folds are exchangeable (each fold's training set is a random subset of size `n(K − 1)/K`); hence for a learning curve that decreases in training size, CV is pessimistic relative to training on `n` rows** — complete (linearity of expectation; the hypothesis of i.i.d. rows and fold-assignment independent of the data is stated and used); (d) **the variance of the mean of `K` correlated fold estimates: `Var(CV) = (1/K)[σ_f² + (K − 1)ρ_fσ_f²]`, so `Var(CV) ≥ ρ_fσ_f²` and the naive `σ_f²/K` understates it whenever `ρ_f > 0` (the fold-score correlation)** — complete (covariance algebra; the learner estimates `ρ_f` by simulation); (e) **LOO closed form for OLS: the leave-one-out residual of row `i` is `e_i/(1 − h_ii)` and `PRESS = Σ(e_i/(1 − h_ii))²` (Sherman–Morrison; recalled from 5A-04 Unit 3)** — recalled;
