## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, hiring, the "best" search or comparison method, a number of folds or seeds, or a shift threshold is exactly one of: (a) cited research/documentation tagged `[VERIFY]` with limits; (b) practitioner opinion/rule of thumb tagged `[JUDGEMENT]` or `HEURISTIC`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the tree; or (d) omitted. **No general ranking of search methods, validation schemes, or tests; every empirical comparison is stated as evidence about its generator only.** Durations `[ASSUMPTION]`; sizes `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`.

- **Planted-truth convention (the module's central evaluation device).** Every selection or comparison method is evaluated on generators for which **the true deployment performance of every candidate is known** (computed on a very large independent sample, with its own interval), so that **bias, coverage, false-winner rate, regret, and power are measured against truth over ≥ 30 independent generator seeds**, never inferred from the same data the method saw. **The null generator (no signal; all candidates equal) is part of every study.**

## Unit 1 — Nested validation and selection bias

- **L1:** **what cross-validation estimates: the performance of a *fixed* model recipe on held-out folds; when you *choose* among `k` recipes by their CV score, the winner's CV score is biased upward because you picked the luckiest; this is selection bias (the winner's curse)**; **the three roles of data: training (fit), validation (choose), test (report); nested cross-validation makes the roles explicit twice: an *outer* loop holds out test folds; inside each outer training set an *inner* loop selects (tunes) the model; the outer test score then evaluates the *whole procedure "select-then-fit"* on data it never touched**; **a first worked example by hand: six-row, three-candidate score table; plain CV picks candidate B with score 0.83; the nested outer score of the procedure is 0.78 (numbers computed on a stated tiny generator): the difference is the selection bias**; **what nested CV is and is not: it estimates how well the *procedure* will do on new data — it does not give you the final model (refit the procedure on all data) and it does not tell you the final model's true score**; **cost: `K_out × (K_inner × k + 1)` fits (derive and count on the example)**; **reporting in plain words**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **nested validation in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) the selection-bias mechanism, measured** (a seeded study on the null generator: reported best-of-`k` CV score vs the true score as `k` grows, against `σ√(2 ln k)`; on a signal generator with a few good candidates: bias as a function of the gap between candidates and of the noise `σ`; evidence about these generators only); **(2) the nested-CV algorithm** (outer split → inner split → inner selection (search) → refit on the outer training set → score on the outer test fold → aggregate; what must stay inside the inner loop: scaling, encoding, feature selection, resampling, calibration fits (`X_cal`), early-stopping rounds, and the search itself; a leak map table; the 5A pipeline rule recalled: the *whole pipeline* is the inner object); **(3) what the nested estimate estimates** (the expected risk of the procedure trained on `n·(K_out − 1)/K_out` rows — slightly pessimistic relative to training on all `n`; the outer-fold estimate is *not* the risk of the final refit model; the estimator's variance and why no unbiased variance estimator of `K`-fold CV exists `CITED [VERIFY]` (Bengio & Grandvalet 2004), with the practical consequence: intervals are approximate: Unit 6); **(4) alternatives and when each is right** (a single hold-out with a sealed test set and a validation set; repeated CV for variance reduction; the "train/validation/test with a fixed search" protocol of 5A-07; leave-one-group-out for few groups; when nested CV is too expensive and what to do: a smaller outer loop, a single outer split repeated over seeds, or a held-out *selection-evaluation* set; the fit-count trade-offs tabulated); **(5) structure-aware nesting** (grouped nesting: both loops split by group; time-ordered nesting: the outer test block lies after the outer training block, the inner folds are time-ordered inside the outer training block, with a gap/embargo; leakage when the inner loop uses future data; a worked example with a planted temporal leak); **(6) what goes into "the procedure"** (the candidate list, the search method and its random seed, the stopping rule, feature selection, the decision to drop outliers: **every analyst degree of freedom is part of the procedure and must be inside the loop or pre-registered**; the forking-paths link to 5A-11); **(7) repeated nested CV and variance** (repeating the outer split with different seeds; combining repeats; the variance components: outer split, inner split, search randomness, model seed; a crossed-seed design as in 5A-11 Unit 1; a plan for the number of repeats from a pilot); **(8) selection bias beyond hyperparameters** (choosing the metric, the features, the preprocessing, the model family after seeing results; the number of *looks* matters: a ledger); **(9) the sealed test set and the reusable holdout** (one touch; adaptive reuse inflates scores: seeded demonstration; thresholding/noise-addition mechanisms for reuse are `CITED [VERIFY]` (Dwork et al.) and named only); **(10) reporting** (the procedure-level estimate with interval, the fit-count, the number of candidates, the final-model refit protocol, and an explicit statement of what the number means); **(11) sklearn mechanics** (`GridSearchCV` inside `cross_val_score` as the canonical nested pattern; `refit`, `best_score_` (the *biased* inner score), `cv_results_`; `cross_validate` keys; `n_jobs` and seed handling; `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **best-of-`k` bias: for i.i.d. `X_i ~ N(μ, σ²)`, `E[max_i X_i] ≤ μ + σ√(2 ln k)` (the moment-generating-function argument, recalled from 5A-11 Unit 4), and for `k = 2` the exact value `μ + σ/√π`; the winner's *true* score is `μ` when all candidates are equal; hence the reported minus true score is positive with expectation up to `σ√(2 ln k)`; for candidates with true means `μ_i` the bias of the maximum is non-negative: `E[max(μ_i + ε_i)] ≥ max_iμ_i` (Jensen on the max)** — complete (recalled bound; the lower bound is new); (b) **nested CV targets the procedure's risk: let `𝒜` be a selection procedure, `D` a sample of size `m`; let the outer test fold `T` be independent of the outer training set `S` and of the inner randomness; then `E[ℓ(𝒜(S)(x), y)]` over a test point drawn from `Q` equals the procedure's risk at training size `|S|`, and the outer-fold mean is an unbiased estimator of the *average* procedure risk over training sets of size `|S|` (linearity of expectation; the dependence between folds affects the variance only)** — complete (hypotheses: independence of `T` from `S`; identically distributed rows; the procedure does not read `T`); counterexample when the inner loop reads `T` (a computed leak); (c) **the fit-count formula: for `K_out` outer folds, `K_in` inner folds, `k` candidates (a grid of `k` configurations), and `r` repeats, the number of fits is `r·K_out·(K_in·k + 1)`; with a search that evaluates `b` configurations adaptively it is `r·K_out·(K_in·b + 1)`; verify by instrumented counting on tiny cases and by enumeration of the fold structure** — complete; (d) **pessimism from training size: if the learning curve is `R(n) = R_∞ + a·n^{−β}` then the nested estimate at training size `n(K_out − 1)/K_out` exceeds `R(n)` by `a·n^{−β}[((K_out − 1)/K_out)^{−β} − 1]`; for `β = 1/2`, `K_out = 5` it is about `11.8%` of `a·n^{−β}`; a computed table** — complete (algebra; a model, `HEURISTIC` in practice); (e) **non-nested selection bias under the null with correlated candidates: if the `k` candidate scores have common correlation `ρ` the expected maximum scales with `σ√(1 − ρ)·√(2 ln k)` plus a shared component that does not bias the selection (derive via `X_i = √ρ Z + √(1 − ρ) ε_i`: the shared part `Z` is common to all candidates and cancels in the *difference* `max − μ_true` only partially — state exactly: `E[max X_i] − μ = σ√ρ·E[Z] + σ√(1 − ρ)E[max ε_i]`, with `E[Z] = 0`)** — complete; so correlated candidates (similar models) inflate less; (f) **the one-standard-error rule** (choose the simplest candidate within one standard error of the best) as a *bias-reducing heuristic*, not a guarantee: `HEURISTIC` with a seeded measurement of its effect on the null and signal generators; **counterexamples**: **a non-nested CV winner whose reported score exceeds the deployment score by `≈ σ√(2 ln k)` (seeded)**; **a "nested" CV whose inner folds share rows with the outer test fold (computed)**; **feature selection done once before nested CV (leak; seeded)**; **a temporal nested CV with an inner loop that sees the future (seeded)**; **the final refit model's score exceeding the nested estimate by the training-size effect (computed)**; **numerical analysis**: **spec-literal nested CV, optimism simulator, and fit-count calculator vs `GridSearchCV` + `cross_val_score` on tiny and random problems**; **tool card** for `validlab.nested` (fold generators (plain, stratified, grouped, time-ordered with gap) with named-key seeds; nested CV runner with an instrumented fit counter and budget guard; repeated nested CV with variance-component estimation; optimism simulators on planted generators; leak auditor for inner/outer overlap; one-standard-error selector; selection-card validator; spec-literal references; stable errors; no input mutation).
- **L4:** **nested validation in production (an MLOps lens)**: **procedure-level estimates as release evidence (the procedure and its estimate are versioned together; re-estimate when the candidate set, search, or data change); compute budgeting (fit counts as the planning unit; a worksheet from the formula; when a smaller outer loop or one outer split repeated is acceptable and what it costs in variance); retraining policy (the procedure is re-run on new data; the *procedure's* estimate is monitored against realised performance: link 5D `12`, `13`); governance (a candidate-set registry; a look ledger; the sealed-test policy; link 5D `05`)**; hostile inputs (`K_out > n`; groups fewer than folds; a class with one example in an inner fold; a search returning `NaN` scores; a candidate that fails to fit; a time series with unordered timestamps; duplicate rows across folds); **exhaustive verification** of the fit-count formula **over all small `(K_out, K_in, k, r)`**, of fold disjointness and coverage for plain, grouped, and time-ordered nested folds **over all small datasets (≤ 8 rows, ≤ 3 groups)**, of the best-of-2 exact bias `σ/√π` **by `mpmath` quadrature**, and of the nested-estimate unbiasedness for a deterministic procedure **by enumeration of all outer splits for tiny data** (domains named and sized; `EVIDENCE ONLY`); mutation lab (inner folds overlapping the outer test fold; feature selection outside the loop; best inner score reported; fold seeds shared between loops; `refit` score reported as the estimate; time order ignored; fit count unguarded; candidates not counted) with no surviving mutants; case study: a "0.91 AUC" model from a 400-configuration search whose outer estimate was 0.84, and a nested CV that leaked through a shared preprocessing step (original synthetic reproductions with a leak-audited nested loop, a counted candidate set, and a procedure-level interval as fixes).

## Unit 2 — Distribution-aware validation and adversarial validation

- **L1:** **validation is only useful if the validation data look like the data the model will face; if deployment differs from development (new customers, a later period, another region, a changed process), a random split measures the wrong thing**; **kinds of difference, in words and by example: *covariate shift* (the inputs `x` change, the rule `y | x` stays), *label shift* (the class mix changes), *concept drift* (the rule `y | x` changes), *group shift* (new entities), *temporal shift* (the future differs from the past)**; **validation schemes that respect structure: group K-fold, leave-one-group-out, time-ordered splits with a gap, blocked CV; picking one from a description of deployment**; **adversarial validation in plain words: train a classifier to tell *development* rows from *deployment-like* rows; if it succeeds, the two differ; the features that give it away show how; if it fails, the inputs look alike (but the label rule might still differ)**; **a first worked example by hand: two Gaussian clouds with a mean shift `d`; the domain classifier's AUC `= Φ(d/√2)` (derived in L3); a random validation split scores 0.90 while a shift-matched split scores 0.78**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **distribution-aware validation in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) the deployment description** (what changes, how fast, at what unit; a checklist and a decision table mapping description → splitter; the 5A-07 splitters recalled; `[JUDGEMENT]` marks); **(2) leak-safe temporal splits** (`TimeSeriesSplit` with `gap`; **purging and embargo**: when labels or features use a window of length `h`, rows within `h` of the boundary are removed from training so no training label/feature overlaps the test period; a worked example with rolling-window features and a measured leak when the gap is omitted; `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]` for sklearn options); **(3) group and cluster structure** (entities repeated; group K-fold; stratified group K-fold named; few groups → leave-one-group-out with high variance; groups unknown → cluster-based splits as a proxy: `HEURISTIC` with a measured miss rate); **(4) adversarial validation as a diagnostic** (build the domain dataset: development rows labelled 0, deployment-like rows labelled 1; fit a regularised classifier with cross-validation *and* the leak-free rule; the cross-validated AUC with an interval; a permutation null for AUC (labels shuffled) with the `+1` p-value; feature importance and partial dependence of the domain classifier to localise shift; **limits**: it detects *covariate* shift only; AUC `≈ 0.5` does not imply stable `y | x`; AUC depends on model capacity and sample size; high AUC does not mean the shift hurts the model: measure it); **(5) density-ratio estimation and importance weighting** (for covariate shift, `w(x) = q(x)/p(x)`; estimate it from the domain classifier: `w(x) = [π̂(x)/(1 − π̂(x))]·(n_P/n_Q)`; **clipping and normalisation** (self-normalised weights); the **effective sample size** `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σw²` and a rule: report it, and refuse to use weights when `n_eff` is a tiny fraction of `n` (`HEURISTIC`); the **overlap/support** requirement; weighted validation metrics; weighted training is *not* the same as weighted validation; a seeded study of the weighted-validation error against the true deployment score on planted covariate shift, with intervals); **(6) building a validation set that resembles deployment** (choose validation rows by domain-classifier probability: the "most deployment-like" fold; the trade-off between resemblance and size; the leak rule: the selector is fitted on training rows only; a measured effect); **(7) shift tests** (two-sample tests for single features: Kolmogorov–Smirnov and a classifier two-sample test; the population stability index recalled from 5A-02 Unit 8; maximum mean discrepancy named `CITED [VERIFY]`; multiplicity across features: BH); **(8) label shift and concept drift** (estimating class priors under label shift (black-box shift estimation named `CITED [VERIFY]`; a confusion-matrix method implemented on a tiny example); concept drift is not detectable from `x` alone: a monitored label stream or delayed labels; link `../08-…` and 5D `12`); **(9) validation under shift: what can be promised** (with no overlap nothing can be promised; with covariate shift and overlap importance-weighted estimates are consistent; with concept drift only recent labelled data help: honest scoping); **(10) the practical pipeline** (describe deployment → choose the scheme → run the domain classifier → look at `n_eff` and weights → compare random vs matched validation estimates against the planted truth in the lab → record the decision in the selection card); **(11) reporting** (state the scheme, the diagnostics, the weights' `n_eff`, and what the estimate is *not* valid for)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the density-ratio identity: with development and deployment rows pooled and a domain label `D ∈ {0, 1}`, `P(D = 1 | x) = n_Qq(x)/(n_Qq(x) + n_Pp(x))`, so `q(x)/p(x) = [P(D = 1 | x)/P(D = 0 | x)]·(n_P/n_Q)` (Bayes' rule)** — complete; (b) **importance-weighted risk is unbiased under covariate shift: if `q(x, y) = q(x)p(y | x)` and `supp q ⊆ supp p`, then `E_{(x,y)~P}[w(x)ℓ(f(x), y)] = E_{(x,y)~Q}[ℓ(f(x), y)]`; the self-normalised estimator is consistent but biased in finite samples; its variance grows with `E_P[w²]`** — complete (change of measure; hypotheses: support inclusion, covariate shift only); counterexample when `y | x` changes or support fails (computed); (c) **effective sample size: for weights `w_i ≥ 0`, `n_eff = (Σw_i)²/Σw_i² ∈ [1, n]`, `n_eff = n` iff all weights equal, and `n_eff = 1` iff one weight carries all the mass (Cauchy–Schwarz); the variance of the self-normalised estimator for a bounded loss is `≈ σ²_ℓ/n_eff` (delta method; hypotheses stated)** — complete for the bounds, `HEURISTIC` for the variance approximation (checked by simulation); (d) **domain-classifier AUC under a Gaussian mean shift: for `x ~ N(0, 1)` vs `N(d, 1)` in one dimension with the Bayes-optimal (monotone) scorer, the AUC is `Φ(d/√2)`; in `p` independent dimensions with the shift vector `δ` and equal covariance the AUC is `Φ(‖δ‖/√2)`; with no shift it is `½`** — complete (difference of two normals; `AUC = P(S₁ > S₀)`); verified by quadrature and simulation; (e) **AUC under exchangeability: if development and deployment rows are exchangeable, any scorer fitted on a separate training portion has expected test AUC `½` (a symmetry argument: the labels are independent of the features); the permutation null of the AUC is exact for `n` small by enumeration** — complete; (f) **purging removes overlap: if a label at time `t` depends on data in `(t − h, t]` (or a future window `(t, t + h]`), then any training row with timestamp within `h` of a test timestamp shares information with it; excluding the rows within `h` (the embargo) makes training and test windows disjoint; a worked count of rows lost as a function of `h` and block size** — complete (interval overlap argument); (g) **blind spot of adversarial validation: two distributions with identical `p(x)` and different `p(y | x)` give domain-classifier AUC `½` while the model's deployment risk is large; a computed example** — complete; **counterexamples**: **a random split overstating deployment AUC under temporal drift (seeded; intervals)**; **a domain AUC of 0.99 from a leaky ID feature (planted; detected via importance)**; **importance weights with `n_eff = 3` out of 1,000 (computed)**; **domain AUC `½` with concept drift (computed)**; **a purged split vs an unpurged split on overlapping windows (measured leak)**; **numerical analysis**: **spec-literal purged/grouped/time-ordered splitters, domain-classifier AUC, density-ratio weights, `n_eff`, and Gaussian-AUC formulas vs scikit-learn/SciPy and quadrature**; **tool card** for `validlab.shift` (shift-scenario generators with planted truth (covariate, label, concept, group, temporal); splitters with purge/embargo; the domain-dataset builder, classifier, AUC with permutation null and intervals; density-ratio weights with clipping/normalisation and `n_eff`; weighted-validation estimators; deployment-like validation-set selector; two-sample tests with BH; label-shift prior estimator; spec-literal references; stable errors; no input mutation).
- **L4:** **shift-aware validation in production (an MLOps lens)**: **a deployment description as a versioned artifact (what is expected to change, how monitored); the validation scheme recorded in every selection card; a drift watch tied to the validation assumptions (a domain-classifier AUC monitor, PSI, `n_eff` of reference weights: link 5D `12`); a rule for when the validation scheme must be re-derived (a monitored trigger, not a calendar); privacy (domain classifiers can memorise rows: small-sample and leakage cautions; awareness only); fairness awareness (group shifts and subgroup validity)**; hostile inputs (no overlap between domains; a single deployment row; a constant feature; ID-like features dominating the domain classifier; `NaN`; weights with `inf`; negative `gap`; unsorted timestamps; groups with one row); **exhaustive verification** of splitter disjointness, purge/embargo correctness, and coverage **over all small time-indexed datasets (≤ 10 rows) and all `(gap, h)`**, of the density-ratio identity **over all small discrete distributions (exact `Fraction`)**, of importance-weighted unbiasedness **by exact enumeration on tiny discrete problems**, of `n_eff` bounds **over all small weight vectors**, and of the Gaussian-AUC formula **by `mpmath` quadrature** (domains named and sized; `EVIDENCE ONLY`); mutation lab (random split for temporal data; no gap; shuffle across groups; domain classifier fitted on all rows including evaluation; weights unnormalised or unclipped; `n_eff` unreported; AUC without a null; deployment rows used in training; ID feature left in) with no surviving mutants; case study: a model validated at 0.92 on a random split that scored 0.79 next quarter, and an importance-weighted estimate whose `n_eff` was 11 (original synthetic reproductions with a purged time-ordered scheme, a checked domain classifier, and a reported `n_eff` as fixes).

## Unit 3 — Bayesian hyperparameter optimisation

- **L1:** **hyperparameter optimisation (HPO) treats "validation score as a function of the hyperparameters" as an expensive black box and tries to find a good setting with few evaluations**; **grid search tries a lattice; random search samples; Bayesian optimisation (BO) builds a cheap *surrogate model* of the score from past evaluations and uses it to choose the next setting — balancing *exploiting* settings that look good and *exploring* uncertain ones**; **the loop, in plain words: (1) evaluate a few initial settings; (2) fit a surrogate (here, a Gaussian process); (3) compute an *acquisition function* that scores candidate settings by promise and uncertainty; (4) evaluate the best-scoring candidate; (5) repeat until the budget ends**; **a first worked example by hand: a one-dimensional objective with four observations; the GP posterior mean and standard deviation at three candidate points (computed with a stated kernel); the expected improvement of each; which point BO picks and why; the same by the learner's NumPy GP**; **the module's honest framing: BO may or may not beat random search at a given budget; this lesson teaches how it works and how to *test* whether it helped on your problem**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **BO in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) search spaces** (continuous, integer, log-scaled, categorical, conditional spaces; transformations; bounds; why log scale for learning rates and regularisation; encoding categoricals for a GP is awkward: named alternatives (tree-structured Parzen estimators, random-forest surrogates `CITED [VERIFY]`)); **(2) Gaussian-process regression for BO** (a zero-mean GP with a kernel `k(x, x′)` (RBF, Matérn named; length-scales and signal variance); the posterior mean and variance from the observations with noise variance `σ²_n` (the objective is noisy: CV scores vary with seeds); hyperparameters of the GP itself (marginal likelihood maximisation named; fixed hyperparameters in the lab with a sensitivity check); numerical stability (Cholesky with jitter; never an explicit inverse: 4C-07 Unit 12 recalled)); **(3) acquisition functions** (probability of improvement, **expected improvement**, upper/lower confidence bound; the closed forms derived in L3; the role of the exploration parameter; maximising the acquisition (random multistart; a dense grid in low dimension)); **(4) initial designs** (random, Latin hypercube, Sobol named; how many initial points; `HEURISTIC` with measured sensitivity); **(5) noise, repeats, and the incumbent** (with noisy evaluations the best observed score is a biased incumbent; options: the best posterior mean; re-evaluating the incumbent on fresh folds; **the selection bias of Unit 1 applies fully: BO's chosen settings must be evaluated by an outer loop**); **(6) comparing BO with random and grid search honestly** (same fit budget; ≥ 30 independent seeds and generators; the anytime curve (best-so-far score vs fits) with intervals; paired comparisons; the probability of random search hitting the top `p` fraction in `k` draws `1 − (1 − p)^k` as a baseline expectation; evidence about the generators only; **no ranking**); **(7) failure modes** (high dimension; mostly irrelevant parameters (random search's effective-dimension advantage: derived in L3); categorical/conditional spaces; non-stationary or discontinuous objectives; very noisy objectives; flat regions; the surrogate overfitting a few points); **(8) parallel and batch suggestions** (constant-liar, Thompson sampling, batch acquisition named `CITED [VERIFY]`; the cost-benefit under 4 CPU cores); **(9) reproducibility and logging** (every suggestion's surrogate state hashed; named seeds; the full evaluation log; deterministic given seeds; the ledger of evaluated configurations); **(10) using a library** (`optuna`, `scikit-optimize` named as optional; how to confirm they implement what the learner implemented; library-as-tool, not oracle; fallbacks required); **(11) when not to use BO** (tiny budgets; cheap objectives; huge discrete spaces; when random search plus a good space design suffices: `JUDGEMENT`)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **GP posterior: for a GP prior `f ~ GP(0, k)` and noisy observations `y = f(X) + ε`, `ε ~ N(0, σ²_nI)`, the posterior at `x*` is Gaussian with mean `k*ᵀ(K + σ²_nI)^{-1}y` and variance `k(x*, x*) − k*ᵀ(K + σ²_nI)^{-1}k*` (conditioning of a jointly Gaussian vector; the Schur-complement step shown); a worked 3-point example computed with `Fraction` for a simple kernel and checked against the NumPy implementation** — complete; (b) **expected improvement (maximisation): with posterior `f(x) ~ N(μ, σ²)` and incumbent `f*`, `EI(x) = E[max(f(x) − f*, 0)] = (μ − f*)Φ(z) + σφ(z)`, `z = (μ − f*)/σ` (and `EI = max(μ − f*, 0)` when `σ = 0`): integrate against the Gaussian density by parts; properties: `EI ≥ 0`, increasing in `μ` and in `σ`; verified by quadrature** — complete; (c) **probability of improvement `PI = Φ(z)` and the UCB `μ + κσ`; the relationship `∂EI/∂μ = Φ(z)` and `∂EI/∂σ = φ(z)` (derive); consequences for exploration** — complete; (d) **random search hit probability: if a fraction `p` of the space is "good", `k` independent uniform draws contain at least one good point with probability `1 − (1 − p)^k`; to reach probability `1 − δ` requires `k ≥ ln δ/ln(1 − p)`; for `p = 0.05`, `δ = 0.05`, `k = 59` (computed)** — complete; (e) **effective dimensionality: if only `d_e` of `d` hyperparameters matter, a grid with `m` points per axis has `m^d` evaluations but only `m^{d_e}` distinct values on the relevant axes, whereas `m^d` random points give `m^d` distinct values per relevant axis; derive the coverage comparison and a numerical table** — complete (counting); (f) **regret bounds for GP-UCB: the cumulative regret is sublinear for suitable `κ_t` with the rate governed by the maximum information gain `γ_T` of the kernel (Srinivas et al.)** — `CITED [VERIFY]` with a numerical sanity check on a small instance; (g) **noisy incumbents: the maximum of `m` noisy evaluations has the best-of-`m` bias of Unit 1; BO's incumbent by posterior mean shrinks noise: show with a two-point GP example that the posterior mean of an outlier observation is pulled toward the prior mean by the factor `σ²_f/(σ²_f + σ²_n)`** — complete;
