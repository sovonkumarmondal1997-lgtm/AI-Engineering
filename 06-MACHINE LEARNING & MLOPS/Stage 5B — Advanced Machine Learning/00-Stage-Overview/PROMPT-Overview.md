## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, interviews, role requirements, what "an ML engineer needs", or the superiority of a method is exactly one of: (a) cited research tagged `[VERIFY]` with its limits; (b) practitioner opinion tagged `[JUDGEMENT]`; (c) a verifiable statement about code, statistics, or the repository tree; or (d) omitted. **No success rates, no outcome guarantees, no statements about what any named company asks or requires, no invented quotes or anecdotes, no method rankings.** All durations are `[ASSUMPTION]` templates the learner replaces with measured values.
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand/explicit derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation in Python (exact `Fraction`/enumeration where possible; otherwise NumPy/SciPy/scikit-learn with a **stated tolerance and reason**; never float `==`). Statistical statements from experiments carry **`n`, seeds, and an interval or standard error**; agreement on examples is evidence, not proof.
- **Advanced evaluation-protocol convention (the signature habit of this stage; taught in Unit 1, enforced everywhere).** It **extends the eight-item 5A protocol card** (define the question and unit; record the baseline; split first respecting groups and time; fit every learned step on training data only inside a pipeline; select on validation/CV never the test set; touch the test set once; report metrics with intervals and seeds; record versions, data snapshot, and config) **with four items: (ix) the validation scheme matches the deployment distribution (group/time/shift) and is checked; (x) uncertainty is reported and its coverage is checked (and probabilities are calibrated where they feed decisions); (xi) the decision and its costs are explicit, and thresholds/actions follow from them; (xii) the search/compute budget and the number of configurations tried are declared and reported.** The **5B protocol card** records these twelve items.
- **Reproducibility convention.** The 5A run tuple `(code, data, config, environment, seeds)`, reproducibility levels R0–R4, pre-registration, run ledger, and experiment card (5A-11) apply unchanged; **a result without a stated level is not a result**. Randomness only via recorded root seeds and named spawn keys; no hidden global state.
- **Data-handling convention.** Rows are samples, columns are features; `X: (n_samples, n_features)`, `y: (n_samples,)`; the **unit of independence** (row, customer, session, time, graph component) is stated; train/validation/calibration/test are named consistently (**a separate calibration set is named `X_cal`**); the target is never an input; the test set is never an input to any fit; **splits are group-, time-, and graph-aware where the unit of independence requires**.
- **Original-content policy.** All problems, data, and scenarios are original or synthetic. Named results are referred to **by name and own-words statement only**, never by reproducing a textbook's wording, examples, figures, or exercise sets. No downloaded or scraped content.

# 8. UNIT 5 — OVERVIEW PROJECT: ADVANCED EXPERIMENT DESIGN (REQUIREMENTS)

**Target file:** `Projects/01-advanced-experiment-design.md`. This is the **Stage 5B overview project**. Apply the four levels and sections N.0–N.14. It builds **`expdesign`**, a small local Python package and command that **designs, budgets, validates, and dry-runs experiments for Stage 5B work using only Stage 5A methods**, and **proves the learner's workbench and environment are ready for the stage**. It teaches **no 5B module algorithm** (the deep treatments are in Modules 01, 02, 04, 08, and 10, which it previews with one-line links); it teaches **designing evaluation experiments that match deployment, budgeting the search, planning sample size, and measuring the bias and coverage of a design on synthetic problems with known truth**. It reuses `trackbench`/`replab`, `selectlab`, `evallab`, `errbench`, `mlsmoke`, and `env_check` from Stage 5A (**map, do not duplicate**).

**Required components:**
1. **Synthetic problem generators with planted truth (original, seeded, capped).** (a) **iid tabular** (known signal); (b) **grouped** (repeated entities with entity effects); (c) **temporal** (trend, seasonality, drift in the feature distribution and in the label rule); (d) **covariate-shifted** (known density ratio between development and deployment); (e) **rare-event** (prevalence ≤ 1%); (f) **a null generator** (no learnable signal) — each with a manifest of the planted truth (true effect of each candidate, shift schedule, group structure).
2. **The design planner.** Given a candidate list, a metric, and constraints, outputs a **design file** (hashed): the validation scheme chosen by a stated rule from the unit of independence and deployment description (random/stratified/group/time-rolling/blocked), the **nesting structure** (inner/outer folds) and the **fit-count budget** computed from the design (`outer × inner × candidates × repeats`) with a hard cap, the **seed design** (5A-11 `SeedSequence` derivation), the **paired comparison plan** with multiplicity control, the **sample-size/power calculation** for the primary comparison from a pilot variance (paired `t` and bootstrap; derive the formula and verify against simulation), and the exclusion and stopping rules. **The design is validated by a linter** (Tool card): a design that uses a random split for temporal data, tunes and evaluates on the same folds, omits the budget, omits the multiplicity plan, reuses tuning seeds for evaluation, or lacks a unit of independence is rejected with a full issue list.
3. **The dry-run simulator.** Runs a design on the generators with known truth, over ≥ 30 independent generator seeds, and **measures**: (i) the **optimism of the reported score** (non-nested selection vs nested vs an untouched holdout) as a function of search size `k`, against the extreme-value formula `≈ σ√(2 ln k)` with intervals; (ii) the **error of the validation scheme versus the deployment score** under group, temporal, and covariate shift (random split vs the scheme the planner chose); (iii) the **coverage of the reported intervals** (percentile bootstrap, `t`, and a design-aware interval) against the planted truth; (iv) the **false-winner rate on the null generator** with and without multiplicity control; (v) **the achieved power** vs the planned power; each reported with `n`, seeds, and intervals — **evidence about these generators only, never a ranking of methods**.
4. **Pre-registration and run ledger.** The planner writes a pre-registration file (reusing the 5A-11 format, extended with the 5B protocol-card fields) and the simulator logs every run through `replab`; **a deviation checker** compares the executed analysis with the design.
5. **Workbench readiness check.** `expdesign verify` runs the 5A `env_check` with the 5B environment-delta data file, checks the presence (and documents the fallback) of every optional library, runs the 5A `mlsmoke verify` (or reports its absence with a pointer), and prints a PASS/FAIL list; **no module may fail because an optional library is absent**.
6. **Verification.** `pytest` suites with **exhaustive small-domain tests** (e.g., the fit-count formula and nesting structure over all small `(outer, inner, candidates, repeats)`; the validation-scheme selection rule over all small deployment descriptions; the power formula against exact enumeration for tiny discrete difference distributions), **seeded random tests with stated tolerances**, **adversarial inputs** (single candidate; zero variance pilot; one group; unordered timestamps; budget exceeded; NaN), and **property tests** (designs are deterministic given inputs; the linter rejects every planted invalid design and accepts every planted valid one; no design reads evaluation data during planning).
7. **Mutation analysis.** A **mutation lab** (planted: random split chosen for temporal data; tuning and evaluation on the same folds; budget formula off by a factor; multiplicity plan dropped; tuning seeds reused for evaluation; power formula using the wrong variance; interval coverage computed against the sample rather than the truth; deployment shift not simulated; ledger entries missing; pre-registration editable after runs) with **no surviving mutants**, and a **regression guard**.
8. **Reproducibility.** A single command **`expdesign verify`** (supports `--quick`; enforces the disk cap and the declared fit/replicate budgets); a fresh-`venv` reproducibility contract; pinned requirements; **no network after install**; passes without any optional library; R2 on two processes at a stated tolerance for deterministic outputs.
9. **Documentation and quality.** `pyproject.toml` (local editable install only), a package-layout mermaid diagram, an API reference, a `CHANGELOG`, a numerics-and-randomness notes page, and a **library card** (scope, tested domains, tolerances, versions, limits, **what it must not be used for**: a teaching design harness; results hold for the synthetic generators only; not a ranking of methods).
10. **The evidence report template.** A Markdown template the learner fills with **their own results**: environment-delta check output (redacted), designs and hashes, optimism-vs-`k` table, scheme-error table, coverage table, false-winner rates, power check, mutation score, issues found, AI-use disclosure, signed integrity statement. **Ship the blank template and a clearly labelled `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` synthetic example; never fabricated results.**

**Rubric (provide in full):** requirements traceability; correctness (oracle agreement); design validity (the linter catches every planted invalid design and passes valid ones); simulation honesty (`n`, seeds, intervals, agreement with theory); no-ranking discipline; budget enforcement; test and mutation strength; reproducibility; documentation and library card; honesty of the report; integrity statement. Pass conditions use the 80/60 gate. **Stretch goals:** a second validation scheme (blocked/purged time-series) added with its own simulation; a peer exchange (review each other's designs blind to the manifest); a measured fit-count versus optimism trade-off curve.
