## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, hiring, the "best" reduction method, a variance-retention target, a perplexity or neighbour count, or what an embedding "shows" is exactly one of: (a) cited research/documentation tagged `[VERIFY]` with limits; (b) practitioner opinion/rule of thumb tagged `[JUDGEMENT]` or `HEURISTIC`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the tree; or (d) omitted. **No general ranking of reduction methods; no cluster, segment, or "structure" claimed from an embedding without a stability check and a null comparison; every empirical comparison is stated as evidence about its generator only.** Durations `[ASSUMPTION]`; sizes `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`.
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation — **exact (`Fraction`/`sympy`/enumeration/quadrature) on the tiny tables wherever possible**, otherwise `numpy`/`scipy`/scikit-learn with a **stated tolerance and reason**, or simulation with stated `n`, seed, replicates, interval. **Every reduction, measure, and protocol in the module is implemented as a short spec-literal reference and cross-checked against scikit-learn/SciPy on tiny and random inputs, including constant features, duplicate rows, rank-deficient data, `n < p`, ties in distances, and sign/permutation ambiguity of components.** A library function is never the sole oracle for a claim about that function.
- **Proof policy.** Where Section 6 says "proof", write it in full: statement, hypotheses, every step justified, closing "where each hypothesis was used". **Heuristics are labelled `HEURISTIC` with their measured behaviour on a stated generator.** Where a result is `CITED [VERIFY]`, say which and give a numerical sanity check.
- **Identifiability-and-comparison convention (stated once; applied everywhere).** **Components, directions, and embeddings are defined only up to the symmetries of their method: PCA components up to sign (and rotation within equal eigenvalues); ICA components up to sign, scale, and permutation; t-SNE/UMAP embeddings up to rotation, reflection, translation, and (for non-convex objectives) the local optimum reached. Every comparison of two reductions uses a symmetry-invariant measure: principal angles between subspaces, matched correlations (Hungarian matching from 5A-09 Unit 7), and neighbourhood-preservation indices — never raw coordinate or sign comparisons.**
- **Planted-truth convention.** Every reduction method and diagnostic is evaluated on generators for which **the true low-dimensional structure is known** (true subspace and rank; true sources and mixing; true intrinsic coordinates; true cluster labels and relative scales), so that **subspace recovery (principal angles), rank-selection accuracy, source recovery, neighbourhood preservation against the true manifold, false-structure rates on the null generator, and the loss of downstream task information are measured against truth over ≥ 30 independent generator seeds with intervals**, never inferred from the data the method saw. **The null generator is part of every study.**
- **Evaluation-protocol and leak-free convention (inherited from 5A-00 to 5A-13 and the 5B protocol card).** **Every learned reduction — scalers, PCA/SVD, random-projection matrices that depend on data (data-independent ones are seeded), ICA unmixing matrices, t-SNE/UMAP embeddings used as features, component counts, perplexities, neighbour counts — is a learned object fitted on the rows belonging to its role (train; never validation, calibration, or test rows, and never the evaluation fold of a cross-validation); new rows are *transformed* with the stored map (methods without an out-of-sample map — exact t-SNE — are not used as features for unseen rows, and any "transductive" use is labelled as such); the test set is sealed, touched once, and logged; a visualisation of test rows coloured by label is not used for model or hyperparameter choices.** **The unit of independence is stated.** Every example states which items it exercises; **an example that violates one is labelled as a counterexample.**
- **Reduction card (the module's reporting convention; every reduction result is stated in this form).** **the purpose (preprocessing for a model, compression, denoising, visualisation, exploration) and the information that must survive; the data roles and sizes; the preprocessing (centring, scaling, transformations) and why; the method, its parameters (components, whitening, perplexity, neighbours, seed) and how each was chosen (the selection data and criterion); the explained variance/reconstruction error on *held-out* rows with intervals; the task-relevant loss (downstream metric with and without reduction, paired intervals); neighbourhood preservation (trustworthiness/continuity) with a null baseline; stability across seeds/subsamples (principal angles, matched correlations, embedding agreement); the leak checks; the out-of-sample map and its version; the visual-claims audit (what may and may not be inferred from any plot); the seeds, budget, and number of looks.** Reporting a reduction without these fields is a defect; every worked example ends with the filled card.
- **Notation convention.** Data `X ∈ ℝ^{n×p}` (rows are samples; the unit of independence stated); centred data `X_c`; sample covariance `S = X_cᵀX_c/(n − 1)` with eigenpairs `(λ_i, v_i)` sorted descending; SVD `X_c = UΣVᵀ`; a rank-`k` projection `P_k = V_kV_kᵀ`; scores `Z = X_cV_k`; reconstruction `X̂ = ZV_kᵀ + μ`; embeddings `Y ∈ ℝ^{n×d}` (`d = 2` for visualisation); principal angles `θ_1…θ_k` between subspaces; `ln` natural; sums versus means always stated; indices 1-based in text with the 0-based Python translation side by side; plain-text math with Unicode, **no LaTeX**.
- **Estimation policy.** Every reported quantity carries `n`, `p`, the data role, the seeds, and an interval: **explained variance and reconstruction errors on held-out rows with bootstrap intervals (resampling rows at the unit of independence); component-count and subspace estimates with their stability over bootstrap resamples (principal-angle distributions; selection frequencies with Wilson intervals); embedding quality measures (trustworthiness, continuity, neighbourhood overlap) with a null baseline from permuted or isotropic data and intervals over seeds; downstream metrics as paired differences (with vs without reduction) over ≥ 30 seeds or folds with the corrected/permutation tests of 5B-01 Unit 6; and always the number of configurations (components, perplexities, neighbour counts, seeds) tried.** No best-seed, best-perplexity, or best-method reporting.
- **Original-content policy.** All problems, datasets, and scenarios are original/synthetic. ## Unit 2 — Random projection and ICA overview

- **L1:** **two different ways of mapping many features to a few without looking for "directions of most variance"**; **random projection: multiply the data by a random matrix with few columns; surprisingly, with enough columns, *distances between rows are approximately preserved* (the Johnson–Lindenstrauss idea); it is fast, needs no fitting to the data, and works on sparse data**; **independent component analysis (ICA): assume the observed columns are *mixtures* of hidden *independent, non-Gaussian* sources (the cocktail-party picture: microphones recording several speakers) and try to unmix them; unlike PCA the components are chosen for independence, not variance**; **a first worked example by hand: a four-point set in 3-D; a 2 × 3 random matrix with stated entries; the pairwise distances before and after, and their ratios; then a two-source mixture `x = As` with a stated `A`: the whitened data and the recovered unmixing up to sign, scale, and order**; **what to remember: random projection preserves *distances*, not *directions of signal*; ICA needs *non-Gaussian* sources; both are *approximate* and neither knows about your label**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **projection and ICA in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) random projection constructions** (Gaussian matrix with entries `N(0, 1/k)`; **sparse random projections** (entries in `{−√(s/k), 0, +√(s/k)}` with probabilities `{1/2s, 1 − 1/s, 1/2s}`: Achlioptas/Li–Hastie–Church named `CITED [VERIFY]`; density parameter `s`); `GaussianRandomProjection`/`SparseRandomProjection` `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`: `n_components='auto'`, `eps`, `compute_inverse_components`; **named-key seeds make the projection reproducible and independent of the number of components requested**); **(2) distortion and the JL dimension** (the lemma statement: for `n` points and `ε ∈ (0, 1)`, `k ≥ 4 ln n/(ε²/2 − ε³/3)` random dimensions preserve all pairwise squared distances within `1 ± ε` with positive probability `CITED [VERIFY]`; **the bound depends on `n` and `ε`, not on `p`**; numbers: for `n = 10⁶`, `ε = 0.5` the formula gives `≈ 663`; for `ε = 0.1` `≈ 11,841` (computed; check `johnson_lindenstrauss_min_dim` in the installed version) — **the guarantee is conservative and often impractical; speeding up nearest-neighbour or kernel computations; preserving distances for clustering/neighbour tasks: measured; cost `O(nnz·k)`); **what it cannot do** (not data-adaptive: a low-dimensional signal in a few features is blurred with the noise; no denoising; components are not interpretable; downstream accuracy can drop when the signal lives in few directions: a measured comparison with PCA on planted generators — **evidence about the generators only, no ranking**); convergence tolerance; `whiten` default `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`)); **(7) when ICA fails** (Gaussian or nearly Gaussian sources (measured recovery vs kurtosis); dependent sources; more sources than observations; - **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **Gaussian random projection preserves squared norms in expectation: for `R ∈ ℝ^{k×p}` with i.i.d. `N(0, 1/k)` entries and fixed `x`, `E‖Rx‖² = ‖x‖²` and `Var(‖Rx‖²) = 2‖x‖⁴/k`, because `‖Rx‖²/‖x‖² ~ χ²_k/k`** — complete (rotation invariance of the Gaussian; mean and variance of `χ²_k`); (b) **concentration and the union bound (JL, sketch with the standard `χ²` tail bound): `P(|‖Rx‖²/‖x‖² − 1| > ε) ≤ 2exp(−k(ε²/4 − ε³/6))` (the standard bound; `CITED [VERIFY]`), so over `C(n, 2)` pairs the failure probability is at most `n²exp(−k(ε²/4 − ε³/6))`, which is `< 1` once `k ≥ (4 ln n + 2 ln 2)/(ε²/2 − ε³/3)` (the same structure as the Dasgupta–Gupta form `k ≥ 4 ln n/(ε²/2 − ε³/3)`, up to the constant); the learner verifies the single-vector bound numerically against the exact `χ²` tail** — `CITED` for the tail bound, complete for the union-bound structure; (c) **sparse random projections preserve norms in expectation: entries with `E[r] = 0`, `Var(r) = 1/k` give `E‖Rx‖² = ‖x‖²` (variance of each entry times `‖x‖²`, summed over `k` rows); the variance of `‖Rx‖²` is larger by a term proportional to the fourth-moment excess (computed for `s = 3`)** — complete; (d) **whitening yields an orthogonal residual mixing: if `x = As` with `Cov(s) = I` and the whitening matrix `W` satisfies `W Cov(x) Wᵀ = I`, then `(WA)(WA)ᵀ = I`, so `z = Wx = (WA)s` with `WA` orthogonal; hence after whitening, unmixing is a search over rotations** — complete; (e) **non-identifiability for Gaussian sources: if `s ~ N(0, I)` and `Q` is any orthogonal matrix then `Qs ~ N(0, I)` has the same distribution as `s`, so `x = As` and `x = (AQᵀ)(Qs)` are indistinguishable; therefore the mixing matrix is not identifiable when the sources are Gaussian (more generally when more than one source is Gaussian)** — complete (the "at most one Gaussian source" identifiability theorem is `CITED [VERIFY]` (Comon; Darmois–Skitovich)); (f) **kurtosis as a non-Gaussianity contrast: for unit-variance `y = wᵀz` with `‖w‖ = 1` and independent unit-variance sources, `kurt(y) = Σ_iw_i⁴kurt(s_i)` (cumulant additivity with the normalisation `Σw_i² = 1`); the Gaussian has excess kurtosis `0`, the Laplace `3`, the uniform `−6/5`; hence extrema of `|kurt(y)|` over the sphere lie at the coordinate axes when all sources have the same sign of kurtosis (a statement with a short proof for two sources by calculus on `w = (cos φ, sin φ)`)** — complete for two sources; the FastICA convergence theory is `CITED [VERIFY]`; (g) **PCA components are not independent components: for a mixture of two independent uniform sources by a non-orthogonal `A`, the PCA directions are orthogonal by construction and in general differ from the columns of `A`; ## Unit 4 — Reconstruction error and information loss

## Unit 5 — Leakage and visualisation limitations

- **L1:** **two risks that are easy to miss because the methods are "unsupervised": *leakage* (information from rows that should be unseen sneaks into the fitted reduction) and *over-reading pictures* (seeing structure that is not there or meanings the picture does not have)**; **leakage in plain words: if you fit PCA, a scaler, an embedding, or a unmixing matrix on *all* the data and *then* split, the test rows have helped to build the features — the test is no longer a test; for supervised reductions (those that use the label) the leak is severe**; **the rule: split first; fit every reduction on the training rows only; transform the test rows with the stored map**; **visualisation limits in plain words: a 2-D picture of high-dimensional data must distort something; t-SNE/UMAP clusters can be artefacts of settings or of the data's noise; the same data with another seed can look different; colouring a plot by the label and "seeing" separation is not evidence that a model will separate them on new data**; **a first worked example by hand: the six-point table with PCA fitted on all six rows versus on the four training rows: the test scores differ; then a random-label demonstration: with 6 rows and 8 features a supervised linear projection separates *any* labelling perfectly**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
