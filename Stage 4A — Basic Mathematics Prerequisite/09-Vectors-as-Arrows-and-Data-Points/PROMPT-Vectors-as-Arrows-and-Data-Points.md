## Unit 1 — Scalars, vectors, and points versus vectors
- **L1:** **a scalar is a single number (a magnitude with a unit)**; **a vector has a magnitude and a direction and is described by components: `⟨v₁, v₂⟩` in the plane, `⟨v₁, v₂, v₃⟩` in space**; **a point is a location `(x, y)` or `(x, y, z)`**; **the same pair of numbers can mean a point or a vector**; **the position vector of a point `P` is the arrow from the origin to `P`**; **the displacement from `P` to `Q` is the vector `Q − P`**; **equal vectors have equal components — arrows with the same length and direction are the same vector wherever they start (free vectors)**; **the zero vector**; **notation** (bold or arrow for vectors, hat for a *unit* vector — **and `ŷ` in ML means "predicted", a notation clash to name; M15 link**); **vectors in space and the right-handed axis convention (`[VERIFY]`; named, not exploited)**; Python: tuples and lists of numbers; a first `Vector` and `Point`.
- **L2:** **the point–vector type discipline**: **`Point − Point = Vector`, `Point + Vector = Point`, `Vector + Vector = Vector`, `scalar · Vector = Vector`; `Point + Point` and `scalar · Point` are meaningless (a sum of two locations has no geometric meaning) — made executable by two distinct classes whose forbidden operations raise a clear error**; **Chasles' relation `PQ + QR = PR`**; **vectors in physics and in data (displacement and velocity carry units; M01 Unit 8)**; **feature vectors and embeddings: the *same list of numbers* is used as a point in feature space and as a direction — the context decides**; **row vs column vectors and NumPy shapes: `(n,)` is neither a row nor a column, `(n, 1)` is a column, `(1, n)` is a row — and the broadcasting surprises that follow (named; `../15-Mathematical-Notation-Reasoning-and-Math-in-Code/08-translating-formulas-into-python-and-numpy.md`; NumPy is *awareness only*)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **one example's features as a vector; an image flattened to a vector (a list of pixel values; the flattening order is a convention that must be stated)**; **the dimension `n` as the length of the list**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **well-definedness of displacement: `PQ = P′Q′` iff `Q − P = Q′ − P′`, and this does not depend on where the arrow starts**; (b) **Chasles' relation** (`(Q − P) + (R − Q) = R − P`); (c) **the affine rules are consistent: `(P + v) − P = v` and `P + (Q − P) = Q`**; (d) **a parallelogram `PQRS` has `Q − P = R − S` (opposite sides equal and parallel) — a coordinate proof**; (e) **the position vector of `P` has components equal to `P`'s coordinates (so a point and its position vector are in one-to-one correspondence, which is why the confusion is harmless *only when the origin is fixed*)**; **exhaustive verification**: **Chasles' relation for all triples of integer points in `[−3, 3]²` (`49³ = 117,649`) and in `[−2, 2]³` (`125³`, exactly `1,953,125` — run `--quick` on a stated subsample and state the cap)**; the type discipline: every forbidden operation raises, every allowed one returns the right type (an exhaustive operation table over the four classes).
- **L4:** **why a type discipline is worth its code** (the bugs it prevents; the cost; `[JUDGEMENT]`); **dimension mismatches** (a 2D point and a 3D vector — refused); **`n`-dimensional points and vectors up to a stated cap**; **exact components in `int`/`Fraction` (floats accepted only through an explicit flag, with the exact binary value shown)**; **a typed-geometry tool** `vecs` (Tool card: immutable `Point` and `Vector` for dimensions `2 ≤ n ≤ cap` with exact components; the allowed operations; forbidden operations refused with a stable message; conversion between a point and its position vector made **explicit**; shape guards for list/array input; caps; stable errors); hostile inputs (mixed dimensions, `nan`, non-numeric components, huge dimensions — caps); **testing**: the exhaustive operation table and Chasles oracle; **mutation lab** (`Point + Point` allowed; `Q − P` computed as `P − Q`; the dimension check skipped; implicit point↔vector coercion) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 2 — Vector addition, subtraction, and scaling in 2D and 3D
- **L1:** **adding vectors component by component** (`u + w = ⟨u₁ + w₁, u₂ + w₂⟩`); **the tip-to-tail rule and the parallelogram rule** (both pictures); **subtracting vectors** (`u − w = u + (−w)`; the arrow from the tip of `w` to the tip of `u`); **scalar multiplication `c·u = ⟨cu₁, cu₂⟩`**: **stretches by `|c|`, reverses direction if `c < 0`, collapses to the zero vector if `c = 0`**; **vectors in 3D**; **linear combinations `a·u + b·w` (the term only; span and independence are `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/01-Vectors-Dot-Product-and-Norms/05-linear-combinations-span-and-linear-independence.md`)**; Python: elementwise arithmetic with `zip`, and the same with a `Vector` class.
- **L2:** **the algebraic laws** (commutativity and associativity of addition; the zero vector and additive inverses; `c(u + w) = cu + cw`; `(c + d)u = cu + du`; `(cd)u = c(du)`; `1·u = u`) **checked on examples and justified component-wise (they are inherited from the laws of numbers)**; **the midpoint as `(P + Q)/2` computed on position vectors, and the centroid of `n` points as the mean of their coordinates (M02 Unit 10 weighted-average and M12 mean links)**; **affine combinations: `Σ wᵢPᵢ` with `Σ wᵢ = 1` is a point (a weighted average of locations) — the weights summing to 1 is exactly what makes "adding points" meaningful**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the mean vector of a small dataset**, **a parameter update `θ_new = θ − η·g` as vector arithmetic (named; gradients are `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/07-Multivariable-Calculus-and-Gradients`)**, **batch averaging of vectors**; **solving `a·u + b·w = t` for `(a, b)` in 2D (M04 Unit 11/12: a 2×2 system with exact classification)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the parallelogram rule: the diagonal of the parallelogram with sides `u` and `w` from a common vertex is `u + w` (coordinates)**; (b) **all the algebraic laws above, component-wise**; (c) **`−u` is the unique vector with `u + (−u) = 0`**; (d) **every plane vector is a linear combination of the standard basis vectors `⟨1, 0⟩` and `⟨0, 1⟩` (and every 3D vector of `⟨1, 0, 0⟩, ⟨0, 1, 0⟩, ⟨0, 0, 1⟩`) in exactly one way**; (e) **an affine combination of points is independent of the origin chosen (`Σ wᵢ(Pᵢ − O) + O` does not depend on `O` when `Σ wᵢ = 1`)**; (f) **the centroid `G` of points `P₁, …, Pₙ` is the unique point with `Σ(Pᵢ − G) = 0`**; **exhaustive verification**: **every law over all vectors with coordinates in `{−1, 0, 1}³` (`27` vectors; `27³ = 19,683` triples) and scalars in `[−2, 2]`, exactly in `Fraction` arithmetic**; the centroid property over all multisets of 3 points in `[−2, 2]²` (state the domain size); the parallelogram rule over a stated integer grid.

## Unit 3 — Vector length and unit vectors
- **L1:** **the length (magnitude, norm) of a vector** `|v| = √(v₁² + v₂²)` in the plane and `√(v₁² + v₂² + v₃²)` in space (**the Pythagorean theorem, M08 Unit 2; in 3D applied twice**); **the distance between two points is the length of their displacement `|Q − P|` (M08 Unit 3)**; **the length is never negative and is zero only for the zero vector**; **scaling and length: `|c·v| = |c|·|v|`**; **unit vectors (length 1) and normalization `û = v/|v|` for `v ≠ 0`**; **the zero vector cannot be normalized**; Python `math.hypot`, `math.dist`, `math.sqrt`, `Fraction` squared lengths.
- **L2:** **comparing lengths by squared lengths (exact integers; M08 Unit 3)**; **exact normalization**: **when `|v|` is rational (a Pythagorean triple or quadruple — `(3, 4)` with length `5`; `(2, 3, 6)` with length `7`; found by the learner's own search) the unit vector has rational components; otherwise `|v| = √N` and the unit-vector components are `vᵢ/√N = vᵢ√N/N`, all sharing one radical (an exact single-radical vector — M04 Unit 6's `QuadSurd` idea)**; **the dot product defined algebraically `u·w = u₁w₁ + u₂w₂ (+ u₃w₃)` and its relation to length: `v·v = |v|²`** (**the geometric meaning is Unit 4**); **the identity `|u − w|² = |u|² + |w|² − 2u·w` by expansion**; **distance between points via `|Q − P|`**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **L2 normalization of a feature vector so every example has length 1**; **for unit vectors `|u − w|² = 2 − 2u·w` — so ordering neighbors by Euclidean distance and ordering them by dot product give the *same* order (derived, not a claim about which is better)**; **the length of an update step**; **a zero vector arising from missing data and what normalizing it should do (a documented policy: raise)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the 3D length formula from two applications of the Pythagorean theorem**; (b) **`|v| ≥ 0` and `|v| = 0 ⇔ v = 0`**; (c) **`|c·v| = |c|·|v|`**; (d) **`|v/|v|| = 1`**; (e) **`v·v = |v|²` and `|u − w|² = |u|² + |w|² − 2u·w`**; (f) **for unit vectors `|u − w|² = 2 − 2u·w`, hence `|u − w₁| < |u − w₂| ⇔ u·w₁ > u·w₂`**; (g) **for non-negative reals, `a < b ⇔ a² < b²` (squared-length comparison is valid — M08 Unit 3 recall)**; **exhaustive verification**: **for every non-zero integer vector with coordinates in `[−4, 4]³` (`728` vectors) the exact normalization has squared length exactly `1` (exact single-radical arithmetic)**; the unit-vector distance/dot identity over all pairs of unit vectors from Pythagorean triples and quadruples with length `≤ 30` (state the domain size); `v·v = |v|²` and the expansion identity over a stated grid; **float `math.hypot` and `sqrt` of sum of squares compared against exact squared lengths on stated extreme inputs, measured on the learner's machine (`numerical evidence`)**.
- **L4:** **numerical hazards**: **overflow and underflow when squaring (`math.hypot` is documented to avoid intermediate overflow — `[VERIFY]`; measure it)**; **near-zero vectors and the normalization threshold (`HEURISTIC`; the module's tool refuses exactly-zero and flags tiny)**; **`numpy.linalg.norm` is awareness only, never the oracle**; **a norm tool** `norms` (Tool card: exact squared length; exact length as a simplified radical `m√d`; exact normalization (rational or single-radical components); rational-length detection by `math.isqrt`; distance and squared-distance; float fallback labelled `numerical evidence`; zero-vector refusal; stable errors); hostile inputs (zero vector; `nan`; `inf`; huge components — caps); **testing**: exact squared-length oracles and `Decimal` evidence for lengths; **mutation lab** (division by `|v|²` instead of `|v|`; the square root omitted; a zero vector normalized to `nan`; the radical not simplified so equality tests fail) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 4 — Direction, angle, and perpendicularity intuition
- **L1:** **the direction of a vector is its unit vector**; **the angle between two non-zero vectors** `θ ∈ [0°, 180°]` (`[0, π]` radians), measured between their directions; **the dot product and the angle**: **`u·w = |u||w| cos θ`**, so **`cos θ = u·w / (|u||w|)`**; **perpendicular vectors have `u·w = 0`; the sign of `u·w` tells acute (`> 0`), right (`= 0`), obtuse (`< 0`) — the M08 Unit 1 rule, now explained**; **parallel vectors (same direction: `w = c·u`, `c > 0`; opposite: `c < 0`)**; **computing an angle in Python with `math.acos`** (and its domain error); **direction angle of a 2D vector with `math.atan2` (M08 Unit 5)**.
- **L2:** **exact angle classification from integers (the sign of the dot product)**; **exact cosines: `cos θ = (u·w)/√(|u|²|w|²)` is an exact single-radical number**; **the numerically stable 2D angle `θ = atan2(|u₁w₂ − u₂w₁|, u·w)` versus `acos(cos θ)` near `0°` and `180°`**; **parallel in any dimension via `(u·w)² = |u|²|w|²` — an exact integer test, no cross product needed**; **the component of `u` along a unit vector `n̂` is `u·n̂` (named: projection is `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/01-Vectors-Dot-Product-and-Norms/03-projection-onto-a-vector.md`)**; **a linear score as a dot product: `ŷ = w·x + b` (M07 Unit 6's single-feature model, now with several features; named)**; **cosine similarity as the cosine of the angle (the formula only; the topic is `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/04-Similarity-Distance-and-Embedding-Geometry/01-cosine-similarity.md`)**; **the zero-vector problem in cosine similarity (undefined; a policy)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the angle between two direction vectors; "orthogonal" feature directions; a perpendicular test in code**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the law of cosines in the plane of the triangle with sides `|u|`, `|w|`, `|u − w|`: place one side on the `x`-axis and compute (`(c cos θ − b)² + (c sin θ)² = b² + c² − 2bc cos θ`, using `sin² + cos² = 1`, M08 Unit 6)**; (b) **`u·w = |u||w| cos θ` (the expansion in Unit 3 (e) compared with the law of cosines) — a theorem in dimensions 2 and 3; in dimension `n > 3` the angle is *defined* by `cos θ = u·w/(|u||w|)`**; (c) **the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality `|u·w| ≤ |u||w|` in every dimension, by the discriminant argument: `0 ≤ |u − t·w|² = |w|²t² − 2(u·w)t + |u|²` for all real `t` forces the discriminant `4(u·w)² − 4|w|²|u|² ≤ 0` (M04 Unit 7), with equality iff `u = t·w` for some `t`**; (d) **hence `−1 ≤ cos θ ≤ 1` and the definition of the angle in `n` dimensions is meaningful, with `cos θ = ±1` iff the vectors are parallel**; (e) **the triangle inequality `|u + w| ≤ |u| + |w|` and the reverse triangle inequality `||u| − |w|| ≤ |u − w|` (from (c))**; (f) **`u ⊥ w ⇔ u·w = 0` for non-zero vectors (`cos 90° = 0`)**; (g) **in 2D, `|u₁w₂ − u₂w₁| = |u||w| sin θ`, so `atan2(|cross|, dot)` recovers `θ` exactly (from the M08 Unit 6 sum formulas applied to the direction angles)**; **exhaustive verification**: **for all non-zero integer vectors with coordinates in `[−3, 3]³` (`342` vectors; `342² = 116,964` ordered pairs) the squared Cauchy–Schwarz inequality `(u·w)² ≤ |u|²|w|²` holds exactly, with equality exactly for parallel pairs (checked against a direct proportionality test)**; the angle classification by the sign of the dot product vs a float-`acos` evidence value (`numerical evidence`, away from the boundary); the triangle inequality over the same domain in squared form; the stable-vs-unstable 2D angle measured on a stated family of near-parallel pairs.
- **L4:** **why the definition in `n > 3` is a *definition* and why Cauchy–Schwarz is what makes it legitimate**; **`acos` domain and clamping vs exact classification (M08 Unit 5)**; **parallel/perpendicular tests for floats (`HEURISTIC` tolerance)**; **cosines of vectors with large `n` (an exact `Fraction` dot product vs float accumulation; `math.fsum`, `math.sumprod` — 3.12+, `[VERIFY]`)**; **an angle tool** `angles` (Tool card: exact dot product, exact squared lengths, exact classification (acute/right/obtuse), exact parallel test by the Cauchy–Schwarz equality, exact single-radical cosine, the stable 2D numeric angle with its status label, a clamp policy for `acos`, zero-vector refusal; stable errors; SVG angle arcs by the stdlib writer); hostile inputs (zero vectors; mixed dimensions; `nan`; huge dimensions — caps); **testing**: the exhaustive Cauchy–Schwarz and proportionality oracles; **mutation lab** (dot-product sign reversed in the classification; the CS equality test with a non-squared left side; `acos` called without a clamp; zero vector accepted) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 5 — Data points as lists of numbers
- **L1:** **a dataset as a table (rows are examples, columns are features)**; **each row is a *data point* — a vector with one component per feature (`n` features give a point in `n` dimensions)**; **we cannot draw `n > 3`, but length, distance, dot product, and angle are defined by the same formulas (Units 3–4)**; **the label (target) is kept apart from the feature vector**; **the mean vector (centroid) of a dataset (Unit 2)**; **centering: subtract the mean vector from every point**; **distances between data points**; **scales and units matter**; Python: a dataset as a list of lists, columns via `zip(*rows)`; strict numeric parsing from CSV.
- **L2:** **shapes and axes**: **`(n_samples, n_features)` is the convention used throughout; transposition and axis-mixing are the classic bugs (`axis` confusion — a mean over the wrong axis returns a vector of the wrong meaning and length; M15 link; NumPy awareness only)**; **feature scaling: dividing each feature by a positive constant (here the feature's range, an exact rational — min–max scaling) so no feature dominates a distance by its units alone (M12 Unit 6's standardization is the statistical version; its square roots are not needed here)**; **an exact example in which the nearest neighbor of a point changes after rescaling a feature (found by a stated search over small integer points)**; **one-hot vectors for a categorical feature (the standard basis vectors; the distance between two different one-hot vectors is exactly `√2`)**; **binary vectors: the squared Euclidean distance equals the Hamming distance**; **missing values (`nan`) propagate into every dot product and distance — a documented policy: reject by default**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers (all synthetic)**: **a nearest-neighbor query by exact squared distance with a deterministic tie-break (the algorithmic side is S3-19; link, do not re-teach)**; **the mean vector as a class "center" (named; no claim about classifiers)**; **curse-of-dimensionality named and deferred (`../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/04-Similarity-Distance-and-Embedding-Geometry/03-high-dimensional-geometry-and-the-curse-of-dimensionality.md`)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **for points `x₁, …, x_m` with mean `μ`, `Σ|xᵢ − c|² = Σ|xᵢ − μ|² + m·|c − μ|²` for every point `c` (expand; the cross term vanishes because `Σ(xᵢ − μ) = 0`), so the mean is the unique minimizer of the sum of squared distances (the vector form of the M03 Unit 4 variance identity)**; (b) **the mean of the centered data is the zero vector**; (c) **scaling feature `j` by `s_j > 0` changes squared distances to `Σ s_j² Δ_j²`, so it can change the order of neighbors (explicit minimal example)**; (d) **for binary vectors, `|u − w|² = Hamming(u, w)`**; (e) **the distance between two different one-hot vectors is `√2`**; (f) **min–max scaling is an affine map per feature and preserves the order of values within each feature**; **exhaustive verification**: **the minimizer identity over all datasets of three points with coordinates in `[−2, 2]²` (`25³ = 15,625`) and all candidate centers `c` in a stated grid, exactly**; **Hamming vs squared Euclidean for all pairs of binary vectors of length ≤ 8 (`2⁸ × 2⁸ = 65,536` pairs)**; **a search over a stated small integer domain that finds, and reports, an example whose nearest neighbor changes under rescaling**; the scaling-order claim over a stated grid.
