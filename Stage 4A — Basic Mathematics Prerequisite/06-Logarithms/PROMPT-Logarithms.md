## Unit 1 — What logarithms mean: the exponent–log relationship
- **L1:** **a logarithm is an exponent**: `log_b(x) = y` means `bʸ = x`; **the base `b > 0, b ≠ 1`** and **the argument `x > 0`** (why: `1ʸ = 1` for every `y`, so base `1` cannot "reach" other numbers; **a non-positive argument is not a power of a positive base in the reals** — M05 Unit 1's monotonicity and sign facts; **complex logarithms are deferred to `../14-Complex-Numbers-Optional/01-imaginary-unit-and-complex-arithmetic.md`**); **converting between exponential and logarithmic form**; **evaluating simple logarithms** (`log₂ 8 = 3`, `log₁₀ 0.001 = −3`, `log_b 1 = 0`, `log_b b = 1`); **"how many times do I multiply `b` to reach `x`?"** as intuition; **the inverse relationship** `b^{log_b x} = x` and `log_b(bʸ) = y`; Python `math.log`, `math.log2`, `math.log10` and their domain errors (`ValueError`).
- **L2:** **logarithms of fractions and roots** (`log₂(1/8) = −3`, `log₂ √2 = 1/2` — **M05 Units 3–4**); **estimating logarithms by bracketing** (`2⁵ = 32 < 40 < 64 = 2⁶`, so `log₂ 40 ∈ (5, 6)`); **counting digits**: the number of decimal digits of a positive integer `n` is `⌊log₁₀ n⌋ + 1` — **computed exactly by the integer method (`len(str(n))`, or comparison with powers of ten; the `int`↔`str` limit — `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`) and compared with the float formula**; **the connection to orders of magnitude and scientific notation (M05 Unit 5)**: the exponent `k` of `a × 10ᵏ` is `⌊log₁₀ x⌋`; **bits of an integer** (`n.bit_length() = ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1`, M05 Unit 8); **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: the number of bits needed for `N` categories (`⌈log₂ N⌉`), how many orders of magnitude separate two stated quantities.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **for `b > 1` and rational `y`, `bʸ` is strictly increasing in `y`** (from M05 Units 1 and 4); **for real exponents the corresponding facts and the existence of `log_b x` for every `x > 0` are `CITED [VERIFY]` (intermediate value theorem / real exponentials; deferred to analysis and to `../07-Functions-and-Graphs/10-exponential-and-logarithmic-functions-and-graphs.md`)**, **with uniqueness of the exponent following from strict monotonicity**; (b) **`b^{log_b x} = x` and `log_b(bʸ) = y`** (from the definition); (c) **`log_b 1 = 0`, `log_b b = 1`, and the sign of `log_b x` for `b > 1`: positive iff `x > 1`**; (d) **for integers `b, x > 1`, `log_b x` is rational iff the prime-exponent vectors of `b` and `x` are proportional (equivalently, `bᵖ = xᵠ` for some positive integers `p, q`), and then `log_b x = p/q`** (use unique factorization, M01 Unit 6) — and **`log₂ 3` is irrational** (parity argument: `2ᵖ = 3ᵠ` is impossible for positive integers); (e) **the digit-count formula** `⌊log₁₀ n⌋ + 1` for positive integers (from `10^{d−1} ≤ n < 10ᵈ`); **exhaustive verification**: the exact rational-logarithm decision procedure vs a **brute-force search over `p/q` with `p, q ≤ 40`** for all integer pairs `2 ≤ b, x ≤ 200` (state the domain size and the search's blind spot: it finds only rational logs within the stated denominators; the proof in (d) supplies completeness); digit count by integer method vs `len(str(n))` for every `n ∈ [1, 10⁶]`.

## Unit 3 — Logarithm rules
- **L1:** **the product rule** `log_b(xy) = log_b x + log_b y`; **the quotient rule** `log_b(x/y) = log_b x − log_b y`; **the power rule** `log_b(xʳ) = r · log_b x`; **special cases** (`log_b(1/x) = −log_b x`; `log_b √x = ½ log_b x`); **why the rules work** (they are the exponent laws, M05 Unit 2, read backwards); **expanding and condensing** simple expressions; **checking with numbers**; Python checks with `Decimal` evidence and with exact rational logarithms.
- **L2:** **expanding and condensing with several terms and coefficients**; **what the rules do *not* say**: **`log(x + y) ≠ log x + log y`**, `log x · log y ≠ log(xy)`, `(log x)² ≠ log(x²)`, `log(x)/log(y) ≠ log(x/y)` (exact numeric counterexamples); **the domain caution**: the rules hold for **positive** arguments, and applying them can change the domain — **`ln(x²) = 2 ln|x|`, not `2 ln x`** (for negative `x`, `ln x` is undefined while `ln(x²)` is defined); **logarithms of products of many factors** (a sum of logs); **orders of magnitude and the rules**: `log₁₀(a × 10ᵏ) = k + log₁₀ a` (M05 Unit 5 link); **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **a product of `n` probabilities becomes a sum of `n` logarithms** — **the sum is computed in floats while the product underflows (found by a stated computation)**; **the logarithm-error catalogue** (≥ 8 patterns, each with a wrong rule, an exact numeric counterexample, the correct rule, and a rewrite function used to generate wrong options).
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the product rule** (let `a = log_b x`, `c = log_b y`; `bᵃ·bᶜ = bᵃ⁺ᶜ = xy` by the exponent law; uniqueness of the exponent gives the claim); (b) **the quotient rule**; (c) **the power rule for integer `r` (induction from (a)) and for rational `r` (via M05 Unit 4's root facts); for real `r` it is `CITED [VERIFY]`**; (d) **the domain statement for the power rule with even exponents** (`log_b(x²) = 2 log_b|x|` for `x ≠ 0`); (e) **counterexamples** to each misapplied rule; **exhaustive verification**: **exact** verification of each rule on all triples in a family where all logarithms are rational (`b = 2` or `b = 3` with arguments from `{pⁱ : 0 ≤ i ≤ 12}`; state the domain size) using `Fraction`; **`Decimal` evidence at 50 digits** for arbitrary positive integer arguments in a stated range (labelled `EVIDENCE ONLY`); the misapplied rules' failure counts over the same domain (**computed, not remembered**).
- **L4:** **logarithmic expressions as formal linear combinations**: for positive variables, `log_b` of a product/quotient/power of variables and constants expands to `Σ cᵢ log_b(vᵢ)`; **two expressions built from these rules are equal for all positive values iff their coefficient vectors over the "independent" logs agree** (state precisely what is assumed: the variables are independent positive reals, so their logarithms are formally independent symbols; positive integer constants are factored into primes, and **the logarithms of distinct primes are linearly independent over ℚ — proved from unique factorization (M01 Unit 6): a vanishing rational combination `Σ cᵢ log pᵢ = 0` clears denominators to `∏ pᵢ^{nᵢ} = 1` with integers `nᵢ`, forcing every `nᵢ = 0`**); **a rule-engine tool** `logrules` (Tool card: strict parser for a small grammar of `log_b(...)` and `ln(...)` of products, quotients, and powers of positive variables and positive integer constants; **expand** and **condense** into canonical forms; **domain tracking (which variables must be positive; a warning when a rewrite changes the domain)**; equality decision by canonical coefficient vectors; `Decimal` evidence spot-checks at seeded points; caps); hostile inputs (`log(x + y)` is **kept opaque** and not split; unbalanced brackets; huge exponents); **testing**: canonical-form oracle vs `Decimal` evidence over seeded points; **mutation lab** (quotient treated as a product; power rule dropping the absolute value; sum split; domain warning suppressed) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 5 — Solving exponential equations
- **L1:** **the basic equation `bˣ = c`** (`b > 0, b ≠ 1`): **no real solution if `c ≤ 0`; otherwise `x = log_b c`**; **same-base equations** (`2^{x+1} = 8` → `x + 1 = 3`: equal powers of the same base have equal exponents); **solving by taking logarithms** (`3ˣ = 20` → `x = ln 20 / ln 3`); **exact form vs decimal form** (state which; the decimal carries a stated precision); **checking by substituting** (with a stated-precision `Decimal` check, **labelled evidence**); Python `math.log` and `Decimal.ln`.
- **L2:** **isolating the exponential first** (`5 · 2^{3x} − 7 = 33`); **different bases on both sides** (`2ˣ = 3^{x−1}` → take logs of both sides → a linear equation in `x` whose coefficients are logarithms; **M04 Unit 3 linear-solving recall**); **exponential equations as quadratics** (**substitution `u = bˣ`**: `4ˣ − 3·2ˣ − 4 = 0` → `u² − 3u − 4 = 0` → `u = 4` or `u = −1`; **`u = bˣ > 0`, so `u = −1` is rejected — an extraneous candidate, M04 Unit 15**); **growth and decay questions** (**time to double**: `(1 + r)ᵗ = 2` → `t = ln 2 / ln(1 + r)`; **half-life**; **time for a decayed quantity to fall below a threshold**, M05 Unit 6's search now solved exactly in form); **integer answers**: **the smallest integer `t` with `γᵗ ≤ ε` (`0 < γ < 1`) is `⌈ln ε / ln γ⌉` — the inequality flips because `ln γ < 0`**; **but the float computation can be off by one at an exact boundary** (e.g., `γ = 1/2`, `ε = 1/8`): **teach the exact check — find `t` by exact `Fraction` powers (M05 Unit 6's search) and use the logarithm formula only as an estimate**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: the number of epochs until an exponentially decayed learning rate drops below a stated floor; the number of doublings from a stated size to a stated target.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **injectivity: `bˣ = bʸ ⇒ x = y` for `b > 0, b ≠ 1`** (strict monotonicity — Unit 1; real-exponent facts `CITED [VERIFY]`); (b) **`bˣ = c` has exactly one real solution if `c > 0` and none if `c ≤ 0`**; (c) **taking logarithms of both sides is a reversible step when both sides are positive** (injectivity of `log_b`) — **and not valid if a side can be non-positive**; (d) **dividing an inequality by `ln γ < 0` reverses it; the integer-threshold formula `t = ⌈ln ε / ln γ⌉` and the exact integer characterization `γᵗ ≤ ε < γ^{t−1}`**; (e) **the substitution `u = bˣ` maps solutions bijectively onto positive roots `u` of the transformed equation** (hence the rejection of non-positive roots); (f) **doubling time `t = ln 2 / ln(1 + r)` and its relation to the "rule of 70/72" approximations are *not* claimed; the exact formula and a computed comparison table are given instead**; **exhaustive verification**: for all integer `a ∈ [1, 6]`, `b ∈ {2, 3, 5}`, `c ∈ [−3, 60]` the solver's solution set for `a·bˣ = c` vs a **brute-force search over rational `x = p/q` with `q ≤ 6`, `|p| ≤ 40` checked by the exact integer identity `aᵠ · bᵖ = cᵠ` (for `x = p/q` with `p, q` positive; negative `p` handled by inversion — derived in the lesson from raising both sides to the `q`-th power)** — **the search sees only rational solutions; irrational solutions are verified by `Decimal` evidence at 50 digits and labelled so**; the integer-threshold function vs exact `Fraction` power search for all `γ = 1/k` (`k ∈ [2, 12]`) and `ε = 1/m` (`m ∈ [2, 200]`) — **report, by stated computation, every case where the float formula's `ceil` disagrees with the exact answer**.

## Unit 8 — Why logarithms matter in machine learning
- **L1:** **four reasons, each shown with a small exact or bracketed example:** (1) **products become sums** (a product of many probabilities underflows; the sum of their logarithms does not — **Unit 3's example, now with a stated computation**); (2) **multiplicative effects become additive** (Unit 7); (3) **information is measured with logarithms** (`−log₂ p` bits for an event of probability `p`; **named; entropy and cross-entropy as theory are deferred to `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/13-Information-Theory-for-ML`**); (4) **logarithms undo exponentials, and ML uses many exponentials** (softmax and sigmoid; named, **`../07-Functions-and-Graphs/14-activation-shapes-sigmoid-tanh-and-relu.md`**). **No claim is made about how often, or whether, any technique "works better".**

- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **a strictly increasing function preserves the location of a maximum: `argmax f(θ) = argmax g(f(θ))` for strictly increasing `g`** — **so maximizing a likelihood and maximizing its logarithm give the same maximizer (stated for a finite set of candidates, with the general case flagged `CITED [VERIFY]`)**; (b) **`ln ∏ pᵢ = Σ ln pᵢ`** (Unit 3 by induction); (c) **log-sum-exp**: **`m ≤ LSE(x) ≤ m + ln n`** (prove: the sum lies between its largest term and `n` times it) **and the shift identity `LSE(x) = m + LSE(x − m)`** (prove); (d) **softmax is invariant to adding a constant to all inputs, its outputs are positive and sum to 1** (prove); (e) **uniform-guess loss `ln k`**; (f) **underflow condition**: a product of `n` factors each `≤ p` has size `≤ pⁿ`, which underflows when `n·|log₁₀ p|` exceeds the learner-measured float range (**the threshold is computed from `sys.float_info`, not hard-coded**); **exhaustive verification**: **the algebraic identities are verified exactly with `Fraction` weights `wᵢ = e^{xᵢ}` treated as given positive rationals** (`Σ wᵢ = w_max · Σ wᵢ/w_max`; softmax sums to 1; shift invariance) over a stated domain (all weight vectors of length ≤ 4 with entries in `[1, 6]`: `6⁴ = 1296`), **and the logarithmic statements are verified with `Decimal` evidence at 60 digits** (labelled `EVIDENCE ONLY`); the bounds `m ≤ LSE ≤ m + ln n` over the same domain.
