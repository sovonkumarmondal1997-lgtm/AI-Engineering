## Unit 4 — Degrees and radians
- **L1:** **a degree is `1/360` of a full turn; a radian is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc whose length equals the radius**; **a full turn is `360°` or `2π` radians**, so **`180° = π` radians**; **conversions** `radians = degrees × π/180`, `degrees = radians × 180/π`; **common angles in both units** (`30° = π/6`, `45° = π/4`, `60° = π/3`, `90° = π/2` — **generated by the learner's code as exact multiples of π, not as decimals**); **positive angles are counterclockwise (the mathematical convention)**; **Python's trigonometric functions take radians**: `math.radians`, `math.degrees`, `math.pi` — **`math.sin(90)` is not `1`**.
- **L2:** **arc length `s = rθ` and sector area `A = ½r²θ` (with `θ` in radians)**; **why radians make these formulas clean**; **coterminal angles** (differing by whole turns) **and normalizing an angle to `[0°, 360°)` or `[0, 2π)` or `(−π, π]`** (the division algorithm and Python's `%` convention for negatives, **M01 Unit 3 link**); **angles as exact rational multiples of π** (a `Fraction` coefficient: `7π/6 → Fraction(7, 6)`; addition, subtraction, scaling exact); **angular units and "units" of radians (the radian is dimensionless; conventions `[VERIFY]`; angular velocity in rad/s — M01 Unit 8)**; **clockwise vs counterclockwise and the screen's flipped `y` axis (M07 Unit 1)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **a cyclic feature (hour of day) mapped to an angle `2π·h/24`**; **a rotation angle given in degrees by a user and consumed in radians by a function — the classic unit bug**; **`math.pi` is a float approximation of π: `math.sin(math.pi)` is not exactly `0` — find its value on the learner's machine and explain**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **arc length and sector area as the fraction `θ/(2π)` of the circumference `2πr` and of the area `πr²` (the area formula `πr²` is `CITED [VERIFY]` as the standard result; the proportionality argument is the proof)**; (b) **the conversion formulas follow from `180° = π rad`**; (c) **normalization is correct: for any integer or rational angle `θ` and period `P`, `θ − P·⌊θ/P⌋ ∈ [0, P)` and differs from `θ` by a whole number of periods** (division algorithm); (d) **exact multiples of π form a closed system under addition, subtraction, and multiplication by rationals** (so exact angle arithmetic never leaves `Fraction` coefficients); (e) **`π` is irrational and in fact transcendental (`CITED [VERIFY]`), so no finite decimal equals it and `math.pi` is only a nearby float — the error is bounded by half an ulp (derive the statement and measure it with `Decimal` evidence)**; **exhaustive verification**: **normalization of every integer degree in `[−3600, 3600]` to `[0, 360)` vs a brute-force loop adding or subtracting 360**; exact-coefficient arithmetic vs `Fraction` over a stated grid; conversions round-trip exactly in the coefficient domain; the float conversion's error measured vs `Decimal` for a stated seeded sample (`numerical evidence`).

## Unit 5 — Right-triangle trigonometry
- **L1:** **the three ratios for an acute angle `θ` in a right triangle**: **`sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse`, `cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse`, `tan θ = opposite/adjacent`** (the mnemonic SOH-CAH-TOA, named); **why the ratios depend only on the angle, not on the triangle's size (similar triangles)**; **reciprocal ratios `csc`, `sec`, `cot` (named)**; **finding missing sides and angles in a right triangle**; **inverse trigonometric functions `asin`, `acos`, `atan` (named; their ranges; the inverses of Module 07 Unit 12 restricted to make them one-to-one)**; **angles of elevation and depression**; Python `math.sin`, `math.cos`, `math.tan`, `math.asin`, `math.acos`, `math.atan`, `math.atan2`.

## Unit 6 — The unit circle and basic trigonometric identities
