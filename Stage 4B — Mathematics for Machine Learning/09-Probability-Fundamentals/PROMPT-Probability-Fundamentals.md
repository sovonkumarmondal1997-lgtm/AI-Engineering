## Unit 2 — Conditional probability and the multiplication rule

## Unit 3 — The law of total probability and Bayes' theorem

- **L1:** **a partition of the sample space** (disjoint events covering `Ω`); **the law of total probability `P(B) = Σᵢ P(B | Aᵢ)P(Aᵢ)`** (the "weighted average of conditional probabilities" and the tree-diagram reading: **sum over all paths to `B`**); **Bayes' theorem `P(A | B) = P(B | A)P(A)/P(B)`** as the multiplication rule written two ways, with **the standard diagnostic-test example solved first by natural frequencies (out of 10,000 people…) and then by the formula, with exact `Fraction` arithmetic**; **prior, likelihood, evidence (marginal likelihood), and posterior named**; **the base-rate fallacy**: **a test with high sensitivity and specificity can still give a low `P(disease | positive)` when the disease is rare** (an exact worked example); ≥ 3 worked examples.

## Unit 4 — Independence

- **L1:** **independence means that knowing one event does not change the probability of the other**: **`A` and `B` are independent iff `P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B)`** (and, when `P(B) > 0`, iff `P(A | B) = P(A)`); worked examples (two fair coins, a die and a coin, two cards **with and without** replacement — **independent vs dependent**); **independence is not the same as mutually exclusive** (**disjoint events with positive probabilities are *dependent*: knowing one occurred tells you the other did not**); **independent events and complements** (`A ⊥ B ⇒ A ⊥ Bᶜ`); **the multiplication rule for independent events and "at least one in `n` independent trials": `1 − (1 − p)ⁿ`** (the reliability and multiple-attempts formula; **the gambler's fallacy: independence means the past does not change the next flip**); **independent vs identically distributed (i.i.d.)** introduced as the assumption behind sampling a dataset (**named; the formal treatment is Units 5 and 7 and `11-…`**); ≥ 3 worked examples with exact enumeration.
- **L2:** **testing independence on a joint table**: for a `2×2` (or `r×c`) table of probabilities, **check `P(a, b) = P(a)P(b)` for every cell** (exact `Fraction`; **the factorisation test already used on a joint density in S4B-06 Unit 6, now in its discrete form**); **pairwise vs mutual independence**: **an exact example with three events that are pairwise independent but not mutually independent (two fair coins `A`, `B` and `C = {the two coins agree}`)**; **conditional independence `A ⊥ B | C` (`P(A ∩ B | C) = P(A | C)P(B | C)`)** and **how it differs from marginal independence**: **(i) events dependent marginally but independent given a common cause (a confounder `C` that drives both: exact table); (ii) events independent marginally but dependent given a common effect — "explaining away" (two independent causes `A`, `B` and an effect `E = A or B`: exact enumeration of `P(A | E)` vs `P(A | E, B)`)**; **the naive Bayes assumption**: **features independent *given the class*, so `P(x₁, …, x_d | y) = ΠP(xⱼ | y)`** (**the assumption used by Project 02; it is almost always false in detail, and the consequences (overconfident posteriors when features are duplicates) are shown by an exact duplicated-feature example**); **the i.i.d. assumption in machine learning**: **training examples drawn independently from one distribution; what breaks it — duplicated rows, time-ordered data, grouped data (many rows per user), and train/test leakage**; **a leakage demonstration**: a seeded synthetic dataset with near-duplicates split at random — **the measured optimism of the held-out score vs a group-aware split (a finding about this synthetic setup, no real-world claim)**; **independence of random variables previewed through the joint-table test** (formal in Unit 5); **independence and the product of likelihoods**: **for independent observations the likelihood is a product and the log-likelihood a sum** (link S4B-05 Unit 3, 4A-06; used by Project 02); **independence ≠ uncorrelated** previewed (formal in Unit 8: **`X` uniform on `{−1, 0, 1}` and `Y = X²`: uncorrelated, completely dependent — an exact table**).
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the two definitions of independence agree when `P(B) > 0`**; (b) **if `A ⊥ B` then `A ⊥ Bᶜ`, `Aᶜ ⊥ B`, and `Aᶜ ⊥ Bᶜ`** (via the complement rule); (c) **disjoint events with positive probabilities are not independent** (since `P(A ∩ B) = 0 ≠ P(A)P(B)`);
