- **Error-rates-are-the-evidence rule (the module's central verification idea, stated once).** **A procedure is judged by its *measured error rates under stated conditions*: the *coverage* of an interval (the proportion of repeated datasets whose interval contains the truth), the *size* of a test (the rejection rate when the null is true), and its *power* (the rejection rate when a stated alternative is true).** **Every method card reports these rates, computed exactly where possible (coverage by summing over all outcomes; size and power by summing the exact null/alternative pmf over the rejection region) and by seeded simulation with Monte Carlo SE otherwise, on a stated grid of parameters, including the conditions where the method *fails*.** A method whose textbook justification is asymptotic is *not trusted* until its finite-`n` error rates have been measured.
- **Analysis-plan-first rule (stated once).** **Before any data are examined, an analysis plan states: the question and the estimand, the unit of analysis and the unit of randomisation/sampling, the primary metric, the test or interval and its assumptions, `α` and the interval level, the direction (one- or two-sided), the sample size and its justification, the stopping rule, the multiplicity handling, and the exclusion rules.** **Every project begins with a plan and ends with a report that states every deviation from it.** The module demonstrates, by exact computation and simulation, **how much each kind of plan violation (post-hoc test choice, optional stopping, unreported multiplicity, flexible exclusions) inflates the false-positive rate.**
- **What-is-random rule (carried from S4B-11) and the unit-of-analysis rule.** Every statement names what varies between repetitions. **The unit of analysis must match the unit of randomisation/sampling (user, session, item, document, run); analysing correlated rows as independent is invalid (S4B-11 design effect), and the correct unit is stated in every project.**
- **Pinned definitions (stated once; each library convention is a `[VERIFY]` pointer and a test).** **Two-sided p-value for a discrete null distribution: the sum of the null probabilities of all outcomes whose probability is `≤` that of the observed outcome (the "minimum-likelihood" definition), with the doubling-the-smaller-tail alternative named and its difference shown; one-sided p-values state the direction; the permutation p-value is `(1 + #{T* ≥ T_obs})/(B + 1)` (never `0`); the percentile interval uses a stated quantile method (`np.quantile(method=…)`); a confidence level `1 − α` is a two-sided equal-tailed level unless stated; effect direction is always `B − A` (treatment minus control); `ddof` is stated in every call; `Welch` is the default two-sample mean test, with `equal_var` explicit.**

## Unit 2 — Bootstrap resampling

- **L1:** **the idea: if the sample is a good picture of the population, resample *from the sample* to see how much a statistic would vary** — **the empirical distribution (S4B-09 Unit 5: probability `1/n` on each observation) stands in for the population; a *bootstrap sample* is `n` draws with replacement from the data (`rng.integers(0, n, size=n)` indexes the rows); the statistic recomputed on each bootstrap sample gives the *bootstrap distribution***; **a worked tiny example by hand: data `{2, 4, 9}`; list all `3³ = 27` ordered resamples (or the `C(5, 3) = 10` unordered ones with multinomial probabilities, S4B-10 Unit 2) and the exact bootstrap distribution of the mean — its mean (the sample mean) and variance (`σ̂ₙ²/n`, the S4B-11 Unit 4 identity, now proved)**; **a bootstrap standard error and the percentile interval (the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap statistics — with the stated quantile method)**; **what the bootstrap is *not*: it does not create new information, it does not fix a small or biased sample, and it estimates sampling variability *given that the sample is representative*; `B` (the number of resamples) is not the sample size — more resamples reduce *Monte Carlo* error in the bootstrap estimate, not the uncertainty about the population**; **the loop in NumPy with an `(B, n)` index matrix and its memory cost (`B·n` integers; chunking)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **the bootstrap toolkit**: **(i) bootstrap SE (`std(θ*, ddof = 1)`), bias (`mean(θ*) − θ̂`), and the percentile interval; (ii) the basic (reverse-percentile) interval `[2θ̂ − q_{1−α/2}, 2θ̂ − q_{α/2}]` and why it differs from the percentile interval when the bootstrap distribution is skewed; (iii) the bootstrap-`t` (studentised) interval (resample, compute `t* = (θ* − θ̂)/ŜE*`, use its quantiles) and why it tends to have better coverage (`CITED [VERIFY]`) at the cost of an SE per resample; (iv) the BCa interval named and its bias-correction/acceleration constants defined (`CITED [VERIFY]`; implemented by definition as a stretch) — percentile and basic and `t` are the required methods**; **statistics beyond the mean**: **the median, trimmed mean, proportion, difference of two means (resample each group separately, preserving group sizes), a ratio, a correlation (resample *pairs*), the AUC and F1 of a classifier (resample *examples*, preserving the (score, label) pairing), a quantile**; **the paired bootstrap for model comparison (the crucial design rule): *resample the examples once and apply the same indices to both models* so the pairing is preserved; resampling each model's predictions independently destroys the pairing and inflates the variance — shown by a seeded comparison of the two with the exact paired-difference variance**; **bootstrap for clustered data: resample *clusters* (users), not rows — the cluster bootstrap reproduces the design effect (S4B-11 Unit 4: a seeded comparison of the row bootstrap's SE (too small), the cluster bootstrap's SE, and the analytic cluster-robust SE); for time series, the moving-block bootstrap named (block length heuristics `HEURISTIC`) with an AR(1) demonstration**; **the parametric bootstrap**: **fit a model (S4B-10: a normal, Poisson, or exponential fit by MLE), simulate datasets from the fitted model, recompute the statistic — useful when the model is trusted and when the resampling distribution has an exact form; its dependence on the model's correctness**; **the number of resamples `B`**: **the Monte Carlo error of a bootstrap SE is about `SE/√(2B)` for an approximately normal bootstrap distribution (`CITED [VERIFY]`; measured over repeated bootstraps of one dataset), and percentile endpoints need larger `B` than an SE (tail quantiles are noisier — measured); the reproducibility rule: fixed seeds, `B` stated**; **the bootstrap in ML evaluation**: **a CI for an AUC/F1/accuracy/mean loss on a test set, a CI for a paired model difference, a CI for a metric across seeds (resample runs), the SE of a non-linear metric (S4B-11 Unit 4: the "named, deferred to this module")**; **the permutation resampling named (Unit 3)**; **`scipy.stats.bootstrap` named with its defaults (`method`, `n_resamples`, `[VERIFY]`)**; **computational cost accounting (resamples × statistic cost; vectorisation; the cost of a coverage study = outer replications × `B` — capped)**.
- **L3:** **proofs and derivations, written in full:** (a) **the plug-in principle: the bootstrap distribution of a statistic is its sampling distribution *under the empirical distribution `F̂ₙ`* (definition), so every population quantity is replaced by its sample analogue (the mean `μ → x̄`, the variance `σ² → σ̂ₙ²` with divisor `n`)**; (b) **the bootstrap SE of the mean: `Var*(X̄*) = σ̂ₙ²/n` — each bootstrap observation is a draw from `F̂ₙ`, so the S4B-09 Unit 7 variance of the mean applies with `F̂ₙ`'s variance `σ̂ₙ² = (1/n)Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²` — full; so the bootstrap SE is `√((n − 1)/n)` times the usual `s/√n` (a small-`n` downward bias, a measured fact)**; (c) **the resample counts: the vector `(C₁, …, Cₙ)` of the number of times each observation appears is `Multinomial(n; 1/n, …, 1/n)` (S4B-10 Unit 2), so `X̄* = Σ(Cᵢ/n)xᵢ`; the exact bootstrap distribution of any symmetric statistic follows from the multinomial pmf over the `C(2n − 1, n)` count vectors — an exact enumeration for `n ≤ 8` serving as the oracle for Monte Carlo bootstrap**; (d) **each observation is *absent* from a bootstrap sample with probability `(1 − 1/n)ⁿ → e⁻¹ ≈ 0.368` (the S4B-05 limit), so a bootstrap sample contains about `63.2%` of the distinct observations — and the out-of-bag fraction used in evaluation (named; ## Unit 7 — Correlation vs causation, confounding, and sampling bias

- **L1:** **association is not causation**: **two variables can move together because one causes the other, because a third variable causes both (a *confounder*), because of how the sample was selected, or by chance (a spurious correlation among many comparisons — S4B-09 Unit 8: the maximum correlation among many noise features)**; **the S4B-09 exact examples revisited with the causal question made explicit: the confounder table (the association vanishes within each stratum), Simpson's paradox (the aggregate reverses the stratified association), and explaining away/collider bias (two independent causes become dependent given their common effect)**; **the counterfactual definition of a causal effect (potential outcomes): each unit has an outcome `Y(1)` if treated and `Y(0)` if not; the individual effect `Y(1) − Y(0)` is never observed for the same unit (the fundamental problem of causal inference); the average treatment effect `ATE = E[Y(1) − Y(0)]`**; **why a randomised experiment works: random assignment makes the treated and control groups comparable *in expectation* (including on unmeasured variables), so the simple difference in means estimates the ATE — and why an observational comparison does not (the groups differ systematically — selection)**; **a worked tiny example with the potential outcomes of eight units written out, the true ATE, the observed difference under random assignment (exactly averaged over *all* `C(8, 4) = 70` assignments to show unbiasedness), and under a self-selected assignment (biased)**; **sampling bias types, with a one-line synthetic example each (selection, survivorship, self-selection, non-response, convenience sampling, measurement/label bias)**; **a causal-language discipline (a list of phrases: "associated with", "predicts", "is correlated with" for observational data; "causes", "increases" only with a design that supports it)**; ≥ 3 worked examples with exact checks.
