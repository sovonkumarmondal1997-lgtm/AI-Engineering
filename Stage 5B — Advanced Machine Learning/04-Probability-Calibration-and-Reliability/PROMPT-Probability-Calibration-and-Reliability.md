## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, hiring, the "best" calibrator, a number of bins, an ECE cutoff, or a minimum calibration-set size is exactly one of: (a) cited research/documentation tagged `[VERIFY]` with limits; (b) practitioner opinion/rule of thumb tagged `[JUDGEMENT]` or `HEURISTIC`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the tree; or (d) omitted. **No general ranking of calibrators; no ECE value presented as "good" or "bad" without a measured noise floor; every empirical comparison is stated as evidence about its generator only.** Durations `[ASSUMPTION]`; sizes `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`.
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation — **exact (`Fraction`/`sympy`/enumeration/quadrature) on the tiny scored tables and small discrete problems wherever possible**, otherwise `numpy`/`scipy`/scikit-learn with a **stated tolerance and reason**, or simulation with stated `n`, seed, replicates, interval. **Every estimator, calibrator, and protocol in the module is implemented as a short spec-literal reference and cross-checked against scikit-learn/SciPy on tiny and random inputs, including tied scores, empty bins, constant scores, single-class calibration sets, and scores at exactly 0 or 1.** A library function is never the sole oracle for a claim about that function.
- **Proof policy.** Where Section 6 says "proof", write it in full: statement, hypotheses, every step justified, closing "where each hypothesis was used". **Heuristics are labelled `HEURISTIC` with their measured behaviour on a stated generator.** Where a result is `CITED [VERIFY]`, say which and give a numerical sanity check.
- **Calibration vocabulary (stated once; used identically everywhere).** **A probabilistic classifier with output `p̂(x)` is *calibrated* if `P(y = 1 | p̂(x) = p) = p` for all `p` (binary, "confidence" or "top-label" analogues for multi-class are defined where used); *calibration-in-the-large* is `E[p̂] = P(y = 1)`; *calibration slope and intercept* are the coefficients of the logistic regression of `y` on `logit p̂`; *discrimination* is the ability to rank positives above negatives (AUC); *refinement/sharpness* is how far `p̂` is from the base rate. Weak, moderate, and strong calibration levels (calibration-in-the-large and slope; the full calibration curve; the multi-variable analogue) are named `CITED [VERIFY]` and used only where defined.**
- **Planted-truth convention.** Every calibration estimator and calibrator is evaluated on generators for which **the true posterior `P(y = 1 | x)` is known**, so that **the true calibration function `c(p) = E[P(y = 1 | x) | p̂ = p]`, the true calibration error, and the true expected Brier/log loss of any scored model or recalibrated model are computable** (by quadrature or by a very large independent sample with its interval), and **estimator bias and variance, calibrator regret, and false-"well-calibrated" rates are measured against truth over ≥ 30 independent generator seeds**, never inferred from the data the method saw. **The null generator (no signal) and a perfectly calibrated generator are part of every study** (the latter reveals the *noise floor* of each calibration-error estimator).
- **Evaluation-protocol and leak-free convention (inherited from 5A-00 to 5A-13 and the 5B protocol card).** Every learned object — preprocessing, the base model, **the calibration map and its hyperparameters, the number of bins, the temperature, isotonic breakpoints** — is fitted on the rows belonging to its role: **train (base model) / validation (hyperparameters, thresholds) / calibration (`X_cal`: the recalibration map only) / test (calibration *evaluation*, and every other final evaluation)**; **the calibration map is never fitted on rows that trained the base model (unless by cross-fitting, Unit 5), and calibration quality is never evaluated on rows that fitted the map**; the test set is sealed, touched once, and logged. **The unit of independence is stated; with entities or time, calibration and evaluation splits respect it.** Every example states which items it exercises; **an example that violates one is labelled as a counterexample.**
- **Calibration card (the module's reporting convention; every calibration result is stated in this form).** **the decision the probabilities feed and the calibration level required (calibration-in-the-large, slope, full curve, class-wise); the data roles and sizes (including the number of positives in the calibration and evaluation sets); the base model and its score type (probability, logit, margin); the calibrator (none, sigmoid, temperature, isotonic, binning, multi-class family), its parameters, and how it was fitted (cross-fitted or on a dedicated set); the evaluation: reliability table with per-bin counts and intervals, calibration slope and intercept with intervals, ECE/MCE with their estimator, bin scheme, and noise floor, Brier score and its decomposition, log loss, AUC before and after; the prevalence and prior assumptions (sample, deployment, corrected); the leak checks; the drift/monitoring plan; the seeds, budget, and number of looks.** Reporting calibration without these fields is a defect; every worked example ends with the filled card.
- **Notation convention.** Scores `s(x)`; probabilities `p̂(x) = g(s(x))` after a calibration map `g`; labels `y ∈ {0, 1}` (or `{1…K}`); the true posterior `p*(x) = P(y = 1 | x)`; the true calibration function `c(p) = P(y = 1 | p̂ = p)`; bins `B_1…B_M` with counts `n_b`, mean predicted probability `p̄_b`, and observed frequency `ȳ_b`; calibration-error estimators `ECE`, `MCE`; Brier score `BS = mean (p̂ − y)²`; log loss `LL`; `ln` natural; sums versus means always stated; indices 1-based in text with the 0-based Python translation side by side; plain-text math with Unicode, **no LaTeX**.
- **Estimation policy.** Every reported quantity carries `n`, **the number of positives and of rows per bin**, the data role, the seeds, and an interval: **per-bin observed frequencies with Wilson/Clopper–Pearson intervals on the bin's own count; calibration slope/intercept with profile-likelihood or bootstrap intervals; ECE/MCE reported with a bootstrap interval *and* the estimator's noise floor measured on a perfectly calibrated generator at the same `n` and binning; Brier/log-loss differences paired by row with the corrected/permutation tests of 5B-01 Unit 6; calibrator comparisons paired by seed and generator over ≥ 30 independent generator seeds; and always the number of configurations and looks.** No best-seed, best-calibrator, or best-bin-count reporting.

## Unit 1 — Calibration versus discrimination

- **L1:** **two different questions about a model that outputs a probability: (1) *Discrimination*: do the cases that turn out positive get higher scores than the ones that turn out negative? (measured by ROC-AUC, recalled from 5A-06). (2) *Calibration*: when the model says 30%, do about 30% of such cases turn out positive?**; **a model can be good at one and bad at the other: a score can rank perfectly and be wildly over-confident; a forecaster that always says "the base rate" is perfectly calibrated and has no discrimination**; **why calibration matters: whenever a number is *used as a probability* — an expected cost, a threshold `c_FP/(c_FP + c_FN)` (5B-03), an expected loss, a reserve, a promise to a user — it must mean what it says**; **a first worked example by hand: the twelve-row scored table; the AUC (computed exactly); group the rows by score into three groups; compare mean predicted probability with observed frequency in each group; the model ranks well and over-predicts; then apply a monotone transform to the scores and show the AUC does not change while the group averages do**; **what makes models over- or under-confident, in words: strong regularisation pulls probabilities toward the middle; boosted trees and some margin-based models push them to the extremes; naive Bayes multiplies correlated evidence; resampling and class weights shift the prior (5B-03); a prior shift between development and deployment moves everything**; **the one-line summary: *calibration is a property of the numbers; discrimination is a property of the order***; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **calibration versus discrimination in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) definitions and levels** (the vocabulary of Section 6: calibration, calibration-in-the-large, slope and intercept, refinement; **weak/moderate/strong calibration** `CITED [VERIFY]`; the *calibration function* `c(p) = P(y = 1 | p̂ = p)` and the calibration curve as its estimate; calibration of a *score* requires a map from score to probability); **(2) the separation demonstrations** (a seeded set of four forecasters on one generator with known truth: *oracle* (true posterior: calibrated and sharp); *over-confident* (the oracle logit scaled by 2); *under-confident* (scaled by 0.5); *base-rate* (calibrated, no discrimination); table of AUC, Brier, log loss, calibration slope and intercept, ECE with intervals; the AUC is identical for the first three: proved in L3); **(3) calibration slope and intercept** (fit `logit P(y = 1) = a + b·logit p̂`; interpretation: `b < 1` means predictions are too extreme (over-confident), `b > 1` too timid, `a ≠ 0` a base-rate offset; the slope as a *moderate-calibration* summary; its interval by profile likelihood or bootstrap; **the slope is estimated with `m`-dependent noise (few positives → wide intervals: link 5B-03)**); **(4) what decisions need which calibration** (ranking and top-`K` lists need discrimination only (5B-03 Unit 6); a Bayes threshold needs calibration at the *threshold region*; expected-value and expected-loss calculations need calibration-in-the-large and the curve; budgeting and reserving need calibration of the *average*; **(6) the value of recalibration for decisions** (cost at the Bayes threshold before and after calibration on planted-truth generators: the cost regret against the oracle threshold's cost, with intervals; the recalibrated model's decisions and the unchanged ranking; **recalibration cannot add discrimination**); **(7) multi-class definitions** (confidence (top-label) calibration, class-wise calibration, and full-vector calibration `CITED [VERIFY]`; what each needs; which the decision requires; developed in Unit 4); **(8) calibration versus coverage and PIT** (link 5B-02 Unit 1: for regression, calibration of the predictive distribution is PIT uniformity and coverage; for classification, calibration is the reliability of the probability; both are *marginal* statements unless conditioned: the same honesty rules); **(9) calibration under shift** (a perfectly calibrated model under prior shift is miscalibrated in deployment; **(10) reporting** (always report discrimination and calibration *separately*)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **AUC is invariant under strictly increasing transformations of the score: `AUC = P(S₁ > S₀) + ½P(S₁ = S₀)`, and for strictly increasing `h`, `h(S₁) > h(S₀) ⟺ S₁ > S₀` and `h(S₁) = h(S₀) ⟺ S₁ = S₀`; hence `AUC(h ∘ s) = AUC(s)`; for non-strictly increasing `h` (e.g., isotonic) ties are created and AUC can change** — complete; (b) **the unconditional (base-rate) forecaster `p̂ ≡ π` is perfectly calibrated (`P(y = 1 | p̂ = π) = π`) and has `AUC = ½`** — complete; and **a perfectly discriminating score can be arbitrarily miscalibrated: `p̂ = 0.99` for every positive and negative ranked correctly but with all values in `[0.9, 0.99]`: `AUC = 1`, `E[p̂] ≈ 0.95` vs prevalence `π`** — computed counterexample; (c) **recalibration by the conditional mean never hurts a proper score: for any score `s` and any map `h`, with `g*(s) = E[y | s]`, `E[(y − h(s))²] = E[(y − g*(s))²] + E[(g*(s) − h(s))²] ≥ E[(y − g*(s))²]` (Pythagoras for conditional expectation: the cross term vanishes because `y − g*(s)` has conditional mean zero given `s`); for log loss the analogue is `E[ℓ(y, h(s))] − E[ℓ(y, g*(s))] = E[KL(g*(s) ‖ h(s))] ≥ 0`; so the Bayes-optimal recalibration `g*` is calibrated and minimises both scores among functions of `s`** — complete (note: `g*` is not available in practice — it is estimated); (d) **a calibrated forecaster is calibration-in-the-large: `E[p̂] = E[E[y | p̂]] = E[y]` (tower property)** — complete; the converse fails; (e) **the Brier decomposition for a forecaster taking finitely many values `p_b` with frequencies `n_b/N`, conditional event frequencies `ȳ_b`, and overall base rate `ȳ`: `BS = (1/N)Σn_b(p_b − ȳ_b)² − (1/N)Σn_b(ȳ_b − ȳ)² + ȳ(1 − ȳ)` (reliability − resolution + uncertainty); exact when forecasts take exactly the distinct values `p_b`; for continuous forecasts binned into groups there is an additional non-negative within-bin variance term** — complete (expand `(p − y)²` within each group; verify exactly on the twelve-row table with `Fraction`); (f) **the calibration slope identity: if `P(y = 1 | p̂) = σ(a + b·logit p̂)` is the true calibration relationship, a forecaster with `b < 1` has over-dispersed logits: for the oracle logit `z` scaled to `λz`, the true relationship is `logit P(y = 1 | p̂) = z = (1/λ)·logit p̂`, i.e., slope `1/λ`: `λ = 2` gives slope `0.5` (over-confident), `λ = 0.5` gives slope `2` (under-confident)** — complete (algebra for a logistic truth); (g) **calibration versus decisions: for costs `c_FP`, `c_FN` and a forecaster `p̂` with true calibration function `c(p̂)`, the expected cost of thresholding at the Bayes threshold of `p̂` exceeds the cost of thresholding at the Bayes threshold of `c(p̂)` by a non-negative amount that is zero iff the two decisions coincide a.s. (a Bayes-decision argument with the conditional probability `c(p̂)`)** — complete; **counterexamples**: **AUC unchanged under `p ↦ p²` while Brier worsens (computed)**; **a calibrated, useless forecaster (computed)**; **a perfectly ranking, badly calibrated forecaster (computed)**; **an over-confident forecaster with slope `0.5` and a Bayes-threshold cost regret of `X` (measured on the generator, with interval)**; **numerical analysis**: **spec-literal AUC (with ties), calibration function estimators, slope/intercept fits, Brier decomposition, and decision-cost calculators vs scikit-learn/SciPy and exact enumeration on the twelve-row table**; **tool card** for `calibrlab.core` (planted-truth generators with known posteriors and base-model "miscalibrators" (scaling, clipping, prior shift); AUC with ties; calibration-in-the-large; calibration slope/intercept with intervals; Brier and log-loss decompositions; Bayes-threshold cost evaluators against truth; separation demonstrators; calibration-card validator; spec-literal references; stable errors; no input mutation).

## Unit 2 — Reliability diagrams and the Brier score

- **L1:** **the reliability diagram is the picture of calibration: group the predictions into bins by predicted probability; for each bin plot the *average predicted probability* against the *observed frequency* of the event; a perfectly calibrated model lies on the diagonal**; **the reliability *table* (the same thing as numbers, with a text alternative): bin range, number of cases, mean prediction, observed frequency, and an interval for the observed frequency**; **binning choices: *equal-width* bins (`[0, 0.1), …`) can leave most bins nearly empty when events are rare; *equal-mass* (quantile) bins give each bin the same number of cases**; **summaries: *expected calibration error* (ECE): the bin-weighted average of `|observed − predicted|`; *maximum calibration error*: the worst bin; the **Brier score**: the mean squared difference between predicted probability and the 0/1 outcome (recalled from 5A-06), which rewards both calibration and sharpness**; **a first worked example by hand: the twelve-row table in three equal-mass bins: the reliability table, the ECE, the Brier score, and the exact decomposition into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty**; **the warning: with few cases per bin the observed frequencies are noisy; a "bad-looking" diagram can be noise and a "good" ECE can be luck**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **reliability analysis in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) binning schemes** (equal-width, equal-mass, adaptive/merged bins that guarantee a minimum count; the number of bins as a bias–variance trade-off: few bins hide miscalibration, many bins add noise; `calibration_curve(strategy=…, n_bins=…)` `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`; a measured study of ECE vs the number of bins on perfectly calibrated and miscalibrated generators, with intervals); **(2) intervals per bin** (Wilson/Clopper–Pearson on the bin's own count `n_b`; the diagram with its intervals as a table; **bins with fewer than a stated number of events are flagged, not plotted as if reliable**); **(3) the noise floor of ECE** (even a *perfectly calibrated* forecaster has positive expected binned ECE: `E[ECE] ≈ √(2/π)·(1/N)Σ_b√(n_b p_b(1 − p_b))` (derived in L3); at `N = 1,000`, ten equal-mass bins, `p ≈ 0.5`: `≈ 0.040`; it shrinks like `1/√N` and grows with the number of bins; **always report the noise floor measured on a perfectly calibrated generator at the same `N` and binning**; **ECE comparisons across different `N` or bin schemes are invalid**); **(4) ECE variants** (L1 vs L2 versions; *top-label* ECE for multi-class; classwise ECE; **debiased/bias-corrected ECE and kernel/smoothed ECE named `CITED [VERIFY]`**; the learner implements a simple debiasing by subtracting the expected noise floor and tests it against the generator; **ECE is not a proper scoring rule**: the base-rate forecaster scores `0` (L3)); **(5) the Brier score and its decomposition** (reliability, resolution, uncertainty; the **Brier skill score** versus the base-rate forecaster `1 − BS/BS_ref`; interpretation in terms of calibration and refinement; the decomposition is exact only for exactly-discrete forecasts: the within-bin term for binned continuous forecasts; implemented and checked on discrete and continuous forecasters); **(6) log loss and its decomposition** (the calibration–refinement decomposition of log loss as expected KL terms: `LL = E[KL(c(p̂) ‖ p̂)] + H(y | p̂)` for the true calibration function `c` (proved in L3); log loss is sensitive to tail miscalibration; clipping and its bias); **(7) hypothesis tests for calibration** (calibration-in-the-large: a z-test on `Σ(y − p̂)` with variance `Σp̂(1 − p̂)` under calibration; Hosmer–Lemeshow-type chi-square over bins named `CITED [VERIFY]` with its known sensitivity to the grouping; the likelihood-ratio test of `(a, b) = (0, 1)` in the slope–intercept model; power depends on `N` and on how far from calibrated; **a non-significant test is not evidence of calibration**: an equivalence view with a margin chosen in advance (5A-11 Unit 4)); **(8) rare events** (few positives per bin; equal-mass bins in the lowest decile contain almost no events; log-odds-scale reliability plots (as tables); the calibration slope/intercept as the stable summary; link 5B-03 Unit 1); **(9) conditional reliability** (calibration by subgroup or slice with multiplicity control and small-cell suppression (5A-12 Unit 3 recalled); marginal calibration does not imply subgroup calibration: a planted example); **(10) comparing models' calibration** (paired bootstrap on row-level squared/log-loss differences; the corrected tests of 5B-01 Unit 6; no ranking); **(11) reporting** (reliability table with counts and intervals, ECE with the noise floor, slope/intercept, Brier decomposition, tests with their power caveat)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the Brier decomposition (recalled from Unit 1(e), applied): for each distinct forecast value `p_b`, `Σ_{i ∈ b}(p_b − y_i)² = n_b(p_b − ȳ_b)² + n_bȳ_b(1 − ȳ_b)`, and `Σ_bn_bȳ_b(1 − ȳ_b) = Nȳ(1 − ȳ) − Σ_bn_b(ȳ_b − ȳ)²`; summing and dividing by `N` gives the decomposition** — complete; (b) **the noise floor of binned ECE under perfect calibration: in bin `b` with `n_b` cases each an independent Bernoulli(`p_b`) (calibration with a common forecast value), `ȳ_b − p_b` has mean `0` and variance `p_b(1 − p_b)/n_b`; by the central limit theorem `E|ȳ_b − p_b| ≈ √(2/π)√(p_b(1 − p_b)/n_b)`; hence `E[ECE] ≈ Σ_b(n_b/N)√(2/π)√(p_b(1 − p_b)/n_b) = (√(2/π)/N)Σ_b√(n_bp_b(1 − p_b))`; for `N = 1,000`, ten bins, `n_b = 100`, `p_b = 0.5`: `0.7979 × 50/1000 = 0.0399`; for `p_b = 0.1`: `0.7979 × 30/1000 = 0.0239`; the exact finite-`n_b` value from the binomial distribution is computed and compared** — complete (CLT approximation with the exact check; hypotheses: calibration within bins, independent outcomes, constant `p_b` within a bin); (c) **ECE is not a proper scoring rule: the base-rate forecaster `p̂ ≡ ȳ` has ECE `= 0` while having zero resolution, and a strictly better forecaster (higher resolution, same calibration) has the same ECE `≈ 0`; more strongly, a forecaster that sacrifices a little calibration to gain a lot of discrimination is *rejected* by ECE and *preferred* by the Brier score (a computed example)** — complete (counterexample); (d) **the calibration–refinement decomposition of log loss: with `c(p̂) = P(y = 1 | p̂)`, `E[ℓ(y, p̂)] = E[KL(Ber(c(p̂)) ‖ Ber(p̂))] + E[H(Ber(c(p̂)))]` where the first term is the calibration loss and the second the refinement loss (condition on `p̂`; the expected log loss of a Bernoulli(`c`) outcome under forecast `p` equals `H(c) + KL(c ‖ p)`)** — complete; (e) **calibration-in-the-large test: under calibration `E[Σ(y_i − p̂_i)] = 0` and `Var = Σp̂_i(1 − p̂_i)` (independent Bernoulli outcomes), so `z = Σ(y_i − p̂_i)/√(Σp̂_i(1 − p̂_i))` is approximately standard normal; the test's power against a shift `δ` in the intercept (computed)** — complete; (f) **the per-bin Wilson interval half-width at `p = 0.5` is `≈ 1.96/(2√n_b)`: `n_b = 25` gives `≈ 0.19`; so differences of `0.1` between predicted and observed frequencies cannot be resolved with 25 cases per bin; the planning formula `n_b ≈ z²p(1 − p)/δ²` (`≈ 384` for `δ = 0.05`, `p = 0.5`)** — complete; (g) **ECE depends on the binning: for a forecaster whose true calibration curve is non-linear, coarser bins average out miscalibration of opposite sign: `ECE_coarse ≤ ECE_fine` when each coarse bin is a union of fine bins (triangle inequality: `|Σ_jw_j(ȳ_j − p̄_j)| ≤ Σ_jw_j|ȳ_j − p̄_j|`)** — complete; **counterexamples**: **an ECE of 0.02 that is pure noise at `N = 500` (computed noise floor)**; **ECE `0` for the base-rate forecaster (computed)**; **opposite-sign miscalibration cancelling in two coarse bins (computed)**; **a significant calibration-in-the-large test at `N = 10^6` for a trivial offset `0.001` (computed power)**; **numerical analysis**: **spec-literal reliability tables (equal-width, equal-mass, min-count merging), ECE/MCE variants, Brier and log-loss decompositions, noise-floor calculators, and calibration tests vs `calibration_curve`, SciPy, and exact enumeration (agreement conditions: bin edges, last-bin closure, `pos_label`)**; **tool card** for `calibrlab.reliability` (binning schemes with min-count merging; reliability tables with Wilson/Clopper–Pearson intervals and low-count flags; ECE/MCE (L1/L2/top-label/classwise); noise-floor calculators and simulators on perfectly calibrated generators; debiased ECE; Brier/log-loss decompositions; calibration-in-the-large, Hosmer–Lemeshow-type, and likelihood-ratio tests with power calculators; subgroup reliability with multiplicity control; planted-truth studies; spec-literal references; stable errors; no input mutation).

## Unit 3 — Platt scaling and temperature scaling

- **L1:** **recalibration in plain words: keep the model, and *learn a small correction* that maps its scores to better probabilities, using a set of examples the model was not trained on (the *calibration set*)**; **Platt scaling: fit a logistic regression with a single input — the model's score — so `p = 1/(1 + exp(−(a·s + b)))`; two parameters `a` (stretch) and `b` (shift)**; **temperature scaling (for models that output *logits*, especially multi-class): divide all logits by a single number `T` before the softmax; `T > 1` makes the probabilities less extreme (softer); `T < 1` more extreme; it does not change which class has the highest probability**; **what these fix and do not fix: they are monotone, so ranking (AUC) is unchanged; they can fix over/under-confidence and offsets that are *smooth and monotone in the score*; they cannot fix a calibration curve with a complicated shape (Unit 4)**; **a first worked example by hand: the twelve-row table of margin scores; fit `a` and `b` by a few Newton steps (with `Fraction`/NumPy), the recalibrated probabilities, the Brier score and log loss before and after, and the unchanged AUC; then a three-class logit row with temperatures `T = 0.5, 1, 2`**; **warning: the map must be fitted on rows that did *not* train the model (Unit 5)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.

- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **convexity of the Platt negative log-likelihood: `NLL(a, b) = Σ_i[ln(1 + e^{z_i}) − t_iz_i]` with `z_i = as_i + b` is convex in `(a, b)` because `ln(1 + e^z)` is convex in `z` and `z_i` is affine in `(a, b)`; ## Unit 4 — Isotonic regression and multi-class calibration

- **L1:** **sometimes the relationship between a model's score and the true probability is not a smooth S-curve — it has bumps and plateaus. *Isotonic regression* learns a step-shaped, *non-decreasing* mapping from score to probability, with no assumed shape except "higher score never means lower probability"**; **how it works, in plain words (the pool-adjacent-violators idea): sort the calibration examples by score; start with each example's 0/1 label as its own value; whenever a higher-scored example has a lower value than the one before it (a *violation*), pool the two and replace both by their average; repeat until the values never decrease**; **a first worked example by hand: eight calibration rows; the pooling steps; the final step function; the probabilities assigned to three new scores; the reliability table on the calibration rows (exactly calibrated in-sample: the warning)**; **what you gain and lose: flexibility (can fix irregular curves) against variance (needs more data; steps can be jumpy; **ties**: many scores get the *same* probability, so some ranking resolution is lost)**; **multi-class calibration: *one-vs-rest* (calibrate each class's probability against "all others" and renormalise so the probabilities sum to 1), *temperature/vector scaling* on logits (Unit 3), and richer families (Dirichlet calibration) named**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
