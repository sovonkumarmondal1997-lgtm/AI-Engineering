- **The evidence-label convention (reused from `../09-…`, restated once).** Every statement about a system's behaviour carries exactly one label: **`MEASURED`** (observed on this machine, for this workload and configuration, with repetitions and an interval), **`SIMULATED`** (a seeded model or planted-scenario replay with stated assumptions; validated against `MEASURED` data where any exists), **`DOCUMENTED`** (stated in named official documentation for a stated version and date; not independently verified), or **`NOT ASSESSED`**. **An SLO attainment, detection rate, attack-success rate, disparity, or recovery time without a label is a defect. Results on planted scenarios and synthetic models are `SIMULATED` evidence about the planted family only — never statements about real systems, real populations, or real adversaries.**
- **The SLO-card convention (defined once; extends the monitoring-signal card of `../12-…`; a course convention).** **Every service-level objective is specified by an *SLO card* with: (1) *the user journey or consumer* it protects; (2) *the SLI* — a precise definition as `good events / valid events`, stating exactly what counts as *valid* (excluded: client errors, abstentions by design — a stated decision) and *good* (including how *degraded* and *fallback* responses count — a stated decision, `../12-…` Unit 7); (3) *the target* and *the compliance window* (rolling or calendar, length) with the reasoning; (4) *the measurement source* with its *evidence label*, *sampling*, and *interval* (a point estimate without an interval is incomplete); (5) *the error budget* `1 − target`, its unit (events or minutes), and the *error-budget policy* (what happens at stated consumption levels — alerts, freezes, reductions of the autonomy level of `../13-…`, required reviews); (6) *the burn-rate alert design* with its derivation (windows, thresholds, expected detection time, expected false-alert rate); (7) *dependencies* and the composition of their objectives; (8) *label-delay treatment* for quality SLOs (the maturity cutoff and the lag of the measurement); (9) *owner and review cadence*; (10) *evidence of testing* (replay on planted incidents and clean traces). An SLO without a complete card is a defect.**
- **The degradation-ladder convention (defined once; a course convention).** **Every ML service declares an ordered *degradation ladder*: the sequence of behaviours it falls back through as capabilities fail — for example *full model → cached recent score → simpler/rule-based model → conservative default → abstain/route to human review → refuse*. Each rung specifies: its *trigger* (the failure it handles and the breaker/health signal that selects it); its *quality accounting* (expected excess loss relative to the full path, `MEASURED` or `SIMULATED`); its *safety check* (the harm analysis of the default — *which direction is safe* for this decision, and for whom); its *disclosure* (the response states the rung used, the model version, and `degraded: true`; `../09-…` Unit 2 service contract); its *SLI accounting* (how it counts in the SLO card); its *recovery rule* (how and when the service climbs back); and its *evidence* (fault-injection test results). **No rung is silent; no default is chosen for being cheap or convenient.****
- **The data-handling convention (defined once; a course convention, not a legal one).** **Every data element in the ML system is recorded in a *data inventory* with: *classification* (the course's classes — public, internal, personal, sensitive-personal, secret — after Stage 2D `10` lesson `02`), *source and purpose* (why it is held and for which uses), *location* (store, region), *access* (principals and policies, `../11-…` Unit 2), *retention* (a period or a condition, with the reasoning) and *deletion behaviour*, *derivations* (every dataset, feature, snapshot, training set, model, log, cache, and backup it flows into — via lineage), and *minimisation* (why each field is needed). **A deletion or retention rule is *complete* only if it propagates along every derivation edge it can; edges it cannot clean (a trained model, an immutable backup, a legal hold) are recorded with their compensating control.****
- **The accountability-evidence convention (defined once; a course convention).** **A *card* (model, dataset, system) is a *generated, versioned artifact bound to a model or dataset hash*: every quantitative or categorical claim in it carries a reference to the evidence artifact (an evidence-bundle hash, a run record, a lineage edge) it was computed from; a card whose bound hash differs from the deployed artifact is *stale* and invalid. An *audit trail* is an append-only, hash-chained, periodically anchored log of decisions, lifecycle events, approvals, and access events, written with pseudonymous identifiers and a stated retention; tampering, truncation, or rollback beyond the last anchor is *detectable*. A *decision is reconstructable* iff, from its id, the record of the model version, feature values or their references, threshold, policy, and approvals can be retrieved and verified.**
- **The fairness-evidence convention (defined once; a course convention).** **Every group-level metric states: the *definition* (rate, parity, calibration — with its formula); the *denominator* (all cases, positives only, flagged only — a frequent source of error); the *group definition* and *source of the attribute* (self-reported, inferred, proxy, unavailable — and the limits of each); the *sample size per group* and the *number of positives and negatives per group*; an *interval* (Wilson/Newcombe-type for rate differences, with the method); the *multiplicity accounting* across groups, metrics, intersections, and windows; the *label-delay maturity* and any *group-dependent delay or noise*; the *selective-label status* (is the measured population the whole population?); and the *decision context* (the harm analysis that motivated the metric). **No single metric is declared "the fairness metric"; no verdict "fair"/"unfair" is issued without the harm analysis and the intervals; the course teaches measurement discipline and documents the choice of definitions as a *recorded human decision*.****
- **The incident convention (defined once; a course convention).** **An ML incident record states: *detection* (which signal, when, by whom or what); *severity* derived from *impact* (affected decisions × harm per decision × duration), not from the size of a statistic; *roles* (command, operations, communications, scribe — after Stage 2E `10` lesson `04`); a *timeline* with the two clocks (when it began, when it was detected, when mitigated, when resolved); the *affected-decision set* (identified by querying the prediction and decision logs for the model version and window — `../08-…` Unit 4, `../12-…` Unit 1); the *remediation plan* (re-score with a good model, reverse or review affected decisions, notify as policy requires); *mitigations* and their reversibility; and a *blameless review* with action items, each with an owner and a due date.**
- **The risk-and-threat convention (defined once; a course convention).** **A *threat model* records *assets*, *trust boundaries*, *adversaries* (capability: knowledge of the system, access, query and compute budget, goal), *attack surfaces*, *threats* (each mapped to the surface), *mitigations* (each with *evidence* of effect and *cost*), and *residual risk*. A *risk register* entry records: an id; the description; cause; impact; *likelihood with its basis* (an interval from data, or an ordinal level defined in the register's own legend — never an unexplained number); *detectability*; owner; mitigation; residual risk; and review date. **Risk-priority numbers (severity × occurrence × detectability) are a *heuristic ordering* with known pathologies (an ordinal product treated as a ratio scale) — `HEURISTIC` — and the course reports the underlying components alongside any product.** Attack simulations are run **only against the learner's own synthetic models**, framed defensively, and never against any system the learner does not own and control.**
