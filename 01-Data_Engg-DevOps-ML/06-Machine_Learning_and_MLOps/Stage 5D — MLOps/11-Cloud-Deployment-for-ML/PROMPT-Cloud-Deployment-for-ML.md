## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, role requirements, provider/service popularity, "industry standard" status, service maintenance, durability, availability, SLAs, limits, quotas, prices, free-tier terms, regions, performance, or superiority is exactly one of: (a) cited from a T1/T2 source tagged `[VERIFY]` with its limits; (b) practitioner opinion tagged `[JUDGEMENT]`; (c) a verifiable statement about code, a derivation, a measurement on the emulator, a simulation, or the repository tree; or (d) omitted. **No success rates, no outcome guarantees, no statements about what any named company does, no invented quotes or anecdotes, no rankings, no real prices, no provider performance or reliability figures.** All durations are `[ASSUMPTION]` templates; **all prices, rates, sizes, and traffic values in examples are `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` parameters or are measured on the learner's machine and labelled as such**.
- **The evidence-label convention (reused from `../09-…`/`../10-…`, restated once).** Every statement about a system's behaviour carries exactly one label: **`MEASURED`** (observed on this machine, for this workload and configuration, with repetitions and an interval — and, for cloud-shaped behaviour, **only against the emulator, stated as such**), **`SIMULATED`** (a seeded model with stated assumptions; validated against `MEASURED` data where any exists), **`DOCUMENTED`** (stated in named official documentation for a stated provider, service, version, and date; **not independently verified**), or **`NOT ASSESSED`**. **A cloud behaviour, limit, price, or figure without a label is a defect. Anything a real provider does is `DOCUMENTED` or `NOT ASSESSED`, never `MEASURED`.**
- **The cloudsim-fidelity convention (defined once; a course convention).** **The module's local emulator ("`cloudsim`") implements *course-defined* semantics for each cloud-shaped component — object store, identity and policy evaluation, job lifecycle, billing accrual, infrastructure plan/apply. Each emulated behaviour has a *fidelity row*: the behaviour; the course's modelled rule; the provider documentation (service, page, version/date) it was derived from or `none (course design)`; the fidelity (`modelled`, `simplified`, `absent`); and the known provider variation. Results obtained on the emulator are `MEASURED (emulator)` or `SIMULATED`; they establish properties of the *protocol and the model* (for example, that a manifest-last commit never exposes a partial artifact *under the modelled consistency rules*), and **never establish what a provider does**. Where provider behaviour matters (for example, whether a listing is immediately consistent), the lesson teaches the *defensive design that holds under the weakest documented behaviour* and tests it with the emulator's fault-injection mode.**
- **The cloud-readiness-card convention (CRC; defined once; a course convention, `JUDGEMENT`, an extension of the PRC).** **A cloud deployment is *ready* when ten items are present or explicitly recorded `unavailable` with a reason: (1) *identity* — every workload and human has a named identity with least privilege and no long-lived keys; (2) *exposure* — every public endpoint is intended, authenticated, and rate-limited; no data store is publicly reachable; (3) *data location* — where data and artifacts reside, residency and encryption stated; (4) *artifact integrity* — artifacts are content-hashed, committed manifest-last, verified before load, and referenced through a pointer with a revision counter; (5) *environment boundary* — environments are separated by a hard boundary and the same artifact is promoted, not rebuilt; (6) *configuration* — config is layered, schema-checked, and secrets are referenced, not embedded; (7) *cost controls* — budgets sized from alert latency, tags and expiry on every resource, quotas known; (8) *cleanup* — an inventory, a reaper with protections, and teardown that leaves nothing; (9) *recovery* — RPO/RTO stated for artifacts and metadata, restore tested (`../14-…/06-…`, Stage 2E `13`); (10) *observability hooks* — resource tags, request ids, audit logs, and cost allocation data exist for `../12-…` and `../14-…`.** The CRC is not an industry standard.
- **The destructive-action convention (defined once; a course convention).** **Any operation that deletes, stops, overwrites, or expires a resource follows: *inventory → classify → plan (dry-run, with the reason for each item) → protection check (protect tags, registry/lineage references, retention and legal holds, production approval) → grace (stop or quarantine first) → execute (idempotent) → audit log → verify. The default is *keep* when any check is unknown.**
- **The artifact-commit convention (defined once; extends `../03-…`/`../05-…`).** **An artifact is a set of immutable content-addressed blobs plus a *manifest* (names, sizes, SHA-256, parent, signature/HMAC where applicable). Blobs are written first; the manifest is written last and is the commit marker; readers trust only manifests, verify every blob hash before use, and never trust a listing or an ETag as proof of content. A *pointer* (alias) to a manifest is the only mutable object and is moved by compare-and-swap with a revision counter.**
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand/explicit derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation or check (exact `Fraction`/enumeration where possible; a spec-literal brute-force reference; a closed form checked by simulation; an optional real tool as a differential oracle where installed; never importing the function under test).
