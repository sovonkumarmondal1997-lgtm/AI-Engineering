## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, role requirements, tool/approach popularity, "industry standard" status, retraining cadence, window length, drift or decay prevalence, tool maintenance, or superiority is exactly one of: (a) cited from a T1/T2 source tagged `[VERIFY]` with its limits; (b) practitioner opinion tagged `[JUDGEMENT]`; (c) a verifiable statement about code, a derivation, a measurement, a simulation, or the repository tree; or (d) omitted. **No success rates, no outcome guarantees, no statements about what any named company does, no invented quotes or anecdotes, no rankings, no "retrain every N days" rules, no decay-prevalence figures.** All durations are `[ASSUMPTION]` templates; **all costs, rates, drift sizes, label delays, and traffic in examples are `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` parameters or planted values labelled as such**.
- **The evidence-label convention (reused from `../09-…`, restated once).** Every statement about a system's behaviour carries exactly one label: **`MEASURED`** (observed on this machine, for this workload and configuration, with repetitions and an interval), **`SIMULATED`** (a seeded model or planted-scenario replay with stated assumptions; validated against `MEASURED` data where any exists), **`DOCUMENTED`** (stated in named official documentation for a stated version and date; not independently verified), or **`NOT ASSESSED`**. **A policy's performance, a validator's error rate, or a cost figure without a label is a defect. Results on planted scenarios are `SIMULATED` evidence about the planted family only — never real-world performance of a retraining policy.**
- **The retraining-policy-card convention (RPC; defined once; a course convention).** **Every retraining policy is specified by a *policy card* with: (1) *scope* (which model, which decision); (2) *responses* (the set of actions the policy may choose — `no_action`, `alert_only`, `recalibrate`, `threshold_update`, `retrain_candidate`, `fix_data_block`, `rollback_review` — each with its reversibility and its gate); (3) *triggers* (which monitoring-signal records, from `../12-…`, with the thresholds and the rationale — effect size, false-alarm budget, evidence class); (4) *guards* (cooldown, minimum new matured labels, retraining budget per period, concurrency limit, data-quality precondition, reference-refresh rule); (5) *decision rule* (an expected-cost or evidence-posterior rule, with its cost parameters labelled); (6) *autonomy level* (the autonomy ladder below) and *approvals*; (7) *owner and on-call*; (8) *measured operating characteristics* on planted scenarios (retrains per period, false-trigger rate, wasted retrains, reaction delay, time-with-degraded-model, regret versus an oracle policy), with intervals and evidence labels. A policy without a complete card is a defect.**
- **The autonomy-ladder convention (defined once; a course convention, `JUDGEMENT`).** **L0 — *manual*: humans decide and run everything. L1 — *auto-train, manual promote*: the system retrains and validates automatically, a human promotes. L2 — *auto-release to a limited exposure*: the system may promote a challenger that passes the gate to shadow or canary exposure automatically; full promotion requires a human or an explicit second gate. L3 — *auto-promote with auto-rollback*: the system promotes fully and rolls back on a guard breach. **The course implements and verifies up to L2; L3 is analysed (the conditions under which it is defensible) and never defaulted.** The level is a recorded property of each policy, raised only by a recorded decision with evidence from the planted-scenario record and drills.**
- **The training-data-cut convention (defined once; extends `../03-…`/`../12-…`).** **Every training run records its *cut*: the *trigger id* and policy id; the *as-of time* `t_cut`; the *data snapshot id* (a content hash); the *window specification* (type, length, recency weighting, seasonal alignment); the *label-maturity cutoff* `a*` and the *delay model* it was derived from; the *label coverage* of the included cohorts; the *exclusions* (quarantined data, incident periods, feedback-contaminated records) with reasons; the *time-ordered split* with its *embargo*; the *class balance and sample weights*; and the *selection mechanism* of every label source (the feedback-loop convention). **Two rules are enforced by tools: no feature value may use information after its prediction time (`../03-…` Unit 4), and no label may be used before its maturity cutoff or outside its permitted use.** A run without a recorded cut is not reproducible and is rejected.**
- **The champion–challenger-evidence convention (CCE; defined once; extends the gate evidence of `../08-…` Unit 2).** **A promotion decision rests on an *evidence bundle* (content-hashed) containing: the *challenger and champion hashes*; the *training cut* of the challenger; the *validation cohort* (time range, size, **age relative to serving time**, label maturity, disjointness from training and from threshold-fitting data by snapshot id); the *paired comparison* with a stated non-inferiority/superiority margin and interval, the *discordant-pair counts* for classification, and the *matched-operating-point comparison*; the *seed-noise floor* (spread across `k` retrains with different seeds); *slice results with the multiplicity accounting*; *calibration and threshold-derivation evidence* (the threshold fitted on a split disjoint from the judging data); the *churn analysis* (decision-flip rate and its impact); the *holdout-reuse ledger entry* (how many comparisons this holdout has already served); and the *gate verdict* from the `../08-…` engine. A promotion without a complete bundle is a defect.**
- **The feedback-loop convention (FLC; defined once; a course convention).** **Every label or feedback source declares: its *selection mechanism* (who gets labelled and why), whether *propensities are logged* (or recorded `unknown`, which marks the source *biased for evaluation*), its *noise model* and quality-control evidence, its *annotator provenance*, whether *model scores or decisions were visible to the labeller* (and the measured anchoring effect), the *delay distribution*, and its *permitted uses* (training, evaluation, monitoring — each with the required weighting). **An unweighted selective sample is never used for evaluation; a sample drawn by active learning is never used for evaluation unless weighted by recorded inclusion probabilities; an unbiased random *audit sample* of stated rate is always maintained for evaluation and monitoring.****
- **The holdout-reuse-ledger convention (defined once).** **Every validation or test set used for champion–challenger decisions has a *ledger*: each comparison made against it is recorded (challenger id, date, outcome). The ledger enforces a *look budget* per holdout; beyond it, the holdout is retired and replaced by a fresh matured cohort. A comparison against an exhausted holdout is not valid evidence.**
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand/explicit derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation or check (exact `Fraction`/enumeration where possible; SciPy or a spec-literal brute-force reference; a closed form checked by simulation; never importing the function under test). Measurements and simulations carry **`n`, seeds, replicates, machine description, and an interval or spread; one run is an anecdote**.
- **The Production-Readiness Card (PRC), the units convention, the test-level vocabulary, the two-clock and scheduling-time conventions, the run-record, model-package, registry, determinism-level, resource-budget-card, checkpoint, container-spec, change-axes, release-vocabulary, rollback-readiness, gate-evidence, service-contract, model-version-consistency, load-test-validity, optimisation-claim, parity-level, derived-artifact, cloud-readiness-card, destructive-action, artifact-commit, monitoring-signal-card, reference-window, label-delay, alert-budget, telemetry-privacy, and statistical-validity conventions** are those of the Stage 5D overview and `../01-…`–`../12-…` (link; restate the twelve PRC item names once: (1) decision, consumers, serving mode; (2) data contract and quality gates; (3) point-in-time correctness and training-serving parity; (4) reproducible build; (5) tracked runs, registry, lineage; (6) tests at four levels; (7) release gate and rehearsed rollback; (8) serving contract and budget; (9) monitoring and runbook; (10) retraining policy; (11) security, privacy, retention, model card; (12) ADR and failure-mode review). The PRC is a **course convention (`JUDGEMENT`)**, not an industry standard. The **ADR** template (context, options, decision, consequences, status) is used in every unit.
- **Numerical-assertion convention.** Every floating-point comparison states its tolerance, its semantics, and why; no `==` on floats unless bit-exactness is guaranteed and tested; **money is `Decimal` or integer minor units with a labelled source or `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` (`../11-…` Unit 5)**; probabilities and rates state their denominators; every interval states its method and `B`/`n`; percentile definition and estimator stated; time units always stated (days, hours) with the clock (event, prediction, label-available, wall) named.

- **Randomness and time policy.** Every randomized component takes an explicit `Generator`/seed derived from a recorded root seed with named spawn keys (**stream generation, drift events, label delays, annotators, exploration, training seeds, bootstrap, and shuffles use distinct named keys**); tools never read the system clock inside logic (inject a clock); schedules, cooldowns, run lifecycles, and label arrivals run on a virtual clock; no test sleeps; **logical (data-interval) time and wall time are distinct (`../04-…`)**; **retraining is deterministic given its cut and seed (`../06-…` determinism levels), and nondeterministic components (thread scheduling, solver order) are stated**; wall-clock timing appears only at the CLI edge; simulated time is data.

- **Integrity and ethics policy.** Work is the learner's own; AI assistance is allowed for explanation and review, **must be disclosed in the evidence log**, and AI-generated policies, thresholds, windows, validation procedures, and tool claims are **untrusted hypotheses verified against a check** (a generated policy is replayed on planted scenarios and clean traces; a generated window is checked against the leakage and maturity rules; a generated validation is run on planted challenger families; a generated tool claim is traced to documentation or marked `NOT ASSESSED`).
