## Conventions stated once, used everywhere (state them in Unit 0, apply in all units)

- **Sequence and shape conventions (a table, stated once; mixing is a defect).** Sequence batches are **`(B, T, d)` (batch-first)** with an integer **`lengths: (B,)`** tensor and a boolean **padding mask `(B, T)`** (`True` = valid); the time index is `t = 1..T`; hidden states are `(B, H)` in the from-scratch code and `(num_layers · num_directions, B, H)` in `torch.nn` (`[VERIFY]` against the docs; the mapping is an explicit, tested function); **the from-scratch cell's `W_x` is `(d, H)` and `W_h` is `(H, H)` with `a_t = x_t W_x + h_{t−1} W_h + b`; `torch.nn` stores `weight_ih` as `(gates·H, d)` and `weight_hh` as `(gates·H, H)` with two bias vectors — the transposes, the gate order, and the double bias are mapped explicitly and tested** `[VERIFY]`. Tabular batches are `(B, d_num)` floats plus `(B, d_cat)` `int64` category indices with a reserved **unknown/padding index**. **State the convention for every gate equation, including GRU's update gate (`h_t = (1 − z_t) ⊙ h̃_t + z_t ⊙ h_{t−1}` versus the reverse), and map it to the library's.**
- **Loss and masking convention (inherited from Module 03 and made explicit).** Per-token losses are **masked** and **token-weighted**: the batch loss is `Σ_{valid tokens} ℓ / (number of valid tokens)`; **padding never contributes to the loss, the metric, or the normalisation**; the same rule applies to validation aggregation (weighted by token count, never a mean of means). A test that **adds padding to a batch and checks that the loss, gradients, and metric are unchanged** is mandatory.
- **State-handling convention.** The learner states, for every training and evaluation loop, **whether the hidden state is reset per sequence, carried across batches (stateful), or truncated (`detach`) every `k` steps**, why, and tests it. **Hidden state never leaks across unrelated sequences or across the train/validation boundary.**
- **Time-order and leakage convention (extends 5B-08's rolling-origin discipline).** Time-ordered data are split **by time, never randomly**; the **gap between the end of training and the start of validation is at least the forecast horizon**; **windows are cut after the split** (no window straddles the boundary); **scalers, encoders, and imputers are fitted on training data only, inside a pipeline**; per-series scaling uses statistics from the training span only; **multi-series data are split by series (cold-start) and by time (warm-start) and the scheme is stated**; entity-level data are split **by entity** where the unit of independence is the entity.
