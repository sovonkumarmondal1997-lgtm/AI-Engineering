**Design decisions to state in Unit 0 and Unit 7 (deliberate, `JUDGEMENT`):**

1. **Synthetic exercise papers with planted truth replace downloaded real papers for every graded activity.** A reading-and-reproduction module cannot honestly grade against real papers offline: the papers cannot be fetched, quoting them is a copyright and accuracy risk, and no answer key exists for a real paper's flaws. **The syllabus therefore authors a small pack of original exercise papers (each ≤ 2 pages, labelled `[SYNTHETIC EXERCISE PAPER — FICTIONAL]` in the title and every page header, with no real names), each with an answer key, a data-generating process, and a reference implementation.** **The learner applies the same protocols to real papers from their own access; those readings are logged but never graded, and no claim about any real paper's content enters the syllabus beyond a `[VERIFY]`-tagged conceptual pointer.**
2. **Reading, reproducing, and summarising are measured skills.** Flaw detection is scored as **precision and recall against the planted flaw list** (with severity weights); reproduction is scored by **whether the learner's estimate of the true effect, with a pre-registered tolerance and interval, covers the planted truth and whether their verdict (reproduced / not reproduced / inconclusive) matches the key**; summaries are scored by a **claim-fidelity checklist** (every claim's scope, hedge, and evidence status preserved; no claim strengthened; no invented reference). **No score is a certificate; thresholds are `HEURISTIC`.**
3. **The essential-paper list is a map, not a canon.** It is an annotated, `[VERIFY]`-flagged index that links well-known papers to this stage's modules and to the reading and reproduction skills they train; it contains **only items you are confident exist, with title/author/topic and a conceptual one-line purpose (never results, numbers, page numbers, DOIs, or URLs)**; it is **explicitly `JUDGEMENT`**, states its selection criteria and its blind spots, and teaches the learner to build and maintain their own list.
4. **Reproduction is a statistical procedure.** A claim becomes a hypothesis with an effect, a tolerance, an interval, and a decision rule fixed **before** running; unknowns in the paper become entries in an **assumption ledger** that are varied, not guessed silently; every component of a re-implementation is checked against an independent oracle before the end-to-end number is trusted; and "did not reproduce" is distinguished from "reproduced a different effect" and from "inconclusive".

**Priority:** Unit 3 (reproducing results — the statistics-and-procedure unit), Unit 1 (how to read a paper — the evidence-evaluation unit), Unit 7 (the project) get the deepest treatment; Units 2 and 4 must be complete and rigorous; Units 0, 5, 6 meet their stated requirements.

## Conventions stated once, used everywhere (state them in Unit 0, apply in all units)

- **The synthetic-exercise-paper convention (signature habit).** **Every graded reading, reproduction, or summary exercise uses an original synthetic exercise paper authored in this syllabus.** Each such paper: **is titled and headed `[SYNTHETIC EXERCISE PAPER — FICTIONAL]`; uses fictional method names, fictional authors (initials only, e.g., "A. Fictional"), and no real venue; is ≤ 2 pages; states one headline claim with its scope; describes the method, the setup, the data, the baselines, the metrics, the seeds, and the results in a structured Markdown/YAML form that `replab.claims` parses; and ships with (i) an `answer_key.yaml` (the true effect size, the planted-flaw list with ids and severities, the verdict a correct reproduction should reach), (ii) a `generator` (the data-generating process and the reference implementation of the *true* method), and (iii) a `hash` of the key so that the learner can commit their analysis hash before opening it.** **Keys are printed once, in a clearly marked section (`ANSWER KEY — DO NOT READ BEFORE ATTEMPTING`), and the syllabus text outside that section never leaks them.** **A real paper never appears as an exercise; fictional papers are never presented as real.**
- **The claim-ledger convention.** **Every paper read is reduced to a ledger of claims: `id | claim text (verbatim from the paper or the exercise paper) | type {method, empirical-comparison, theoretical, scope, mechanism} | scope (data, model, scale, metric, conditions) | evidence the paper gives {PROVED, MEASURED, ASSERTED, CITED} | what would falsify it | my confidence and why`.** **A claim without a stated scope is flagged; "improves" without a metric, a comparator, and a condition is not a claim yet.** **The evidence statuses `PROVED` / `CITED` / `EVIDENCE ONLY` / `HEURISTIC` / `JUDGEMENT` of the rest of this syllabus are the learner's *own* statuses for the claim after audit; `MEASURED` and `ASSERTED` are the *paper's*.**
- **The flaw-taxonomy and scoring convention.** The module uses a fixed, original taxonomy with ids and severity weights (1 minor, 2 material, 3 invalidating): **F1 unequal tuning budget; F2 evaluation leakage (test used for selection, or train–test overlap); F3 best-of-`k` or cherry-picked seed reporting; F4 improvement within seed noise or no interval; F5 underspecified or unstated hyperparameter that matters; F6 metric–claim mismatch; F7 baseline weaker than a standard configuration; F8 confounded ablation (two factors changed); F9 scope overreach (claim broader than evidence); F10 equation–code or equation-typo inconsistency; F11 misleading figure (truncated or dual axis, missing intervals); F12 unequal compute, data, or parameters; F13 multiple-comparison or forking-paths selection; F14 citation laundering (a result cited through a secondary source that does not contain it).** **Flaw detection is scored as weighted precision and weighted recall against the key; a reported flaw with no located evidence in the paper counts as a false positive; the scorer lives in `replab.audit` and is itself tested.**
- **The assumption-ledger convention.** **Whenever a paper (or exercise paper) leaves a detail unstated or ambiguous, the learner records an entry: `item | what the paper says (or omits) | assumption made | plausible alternatives | sensitivity run (effect under each alternative) | verdict (matters / does not matter)`.** **No silent defaults: an assumption that is not in the ledger is a defect.**
- **The pre-registration convention (inherited from Modules 11–14, specialised).** **Before any reproduction run: the claim in testable form; the primary metric; the unit of replication (seed, dataset draw); the number of seeds per arm from a stated power analysis; the equivalence margin `δ` and its justification; the decision rule (reproduced / not reproduced / inconclusive); the tuning budget for each arm; the assumption ledger's planned sensitivity grid; the stopping rule; and the hash of the pre-registration file.** **A deviation is recorded and justified; changing `δ`, the metric, or the exclusions after seeing results invalidates the verdict and is flagged.**
- **The verdict vocabulary.** **Reproduced** (the equivalence test passes: the confidence interval of the difference between the reproduced and the claimed effect lies inside `(−δ, δ)`); **Not reproduced** (the interval lies entirely outside `(−δ, δ)` on the side that contradicts the claim, with adequate power); **Inconclusive** (the interval overlaps the margin boundary — the study lacked power or precision); **Reproduced with a different scope** (the effect holds only in a narrower condition than claimed — reported with the conditions); **Not assessable** (the paper's omissions prevent a faithful attempt). **A non-significant difference is never reported as "no effect"; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence (the equivalence test is the tool for the latter).**
- **The evaluation convention (inherited from Modules 11–14).** Programmatic checks only; **no LLM judge**; **paired comparisons by seed with exact or bootstrap intervals; unit of independence stated; held-out items never used for tuning; sampling/seed/data variance sources separated (Module 12, `Bouthillier`-style decomposition); headline comparisons use ≥ 20 seeds per arm for the exercise worlds (`--quick` ≥ 8)**; **the optimism of any max over `k` choices is measured**.
- **Exact-oracle convention.** **Every component of a re-implementation (loss, metric, data pipeline, schedule, update rule) is checked against an independent oracle that never imports the function under test, in `float64` where numeric; tolerances stated and justified.** **Every statistic the module teaches (the power formula, TOST, the prediction interval, the winner's-curse bias, the expected maximum of `k` draws) is verified by simulation with a stated interval and, where one exists, an exact closed form.**
- **Random-stream convention.** Per-stream NumPy `Generator`s and `torch.Generator`s seeded from `SeedSequence` with named spawn keys (`world`, `data`, `init`, `order`, `augment`, `bootstrap`, `audit`); **never the global generator; the data, init, and order seeds are distinct variance sources; tuning and reporting seeds disjoint**.
- **Fair-comparison protocol (inherited from Module 05, with Modules 11–14 additions).** Equal parameters, data, and compute (or the stated deviation) between compared methods; **equal tuning budgets** (the unequal-tuning trap is the module's most-tested flaw, F1); **≥ 20 seeds in the exercise worlds**; effect sizes with intervals; trials reported; **no method ranking is ever stated** (link `../05-Sequence-Models-and-Tabular-Deep-Learning/README.md`, content not yet authored — mark `OPEN` until reconciled).
- **Inherited protocols.** The **5C training-run card** (all eighteen items; `../00-Stage-Overview/README.md`, not yet authored — mark `OPEN` until reconciled), **Module 02's equivalence, measurement, reproducibility, and safe-checkpoint conventions**, **Module 10's counting conventions, ledger, fitting conventions, and the paper-reading checklist for scaling claims**, **Module 11's template-cluster and loophole-audit conventions**, **Module 12's determinism levels (D0/D1) and variance decomposition**, **Module 13's equal-budget, exact-McNemar, and Simpson-reversal conventions**, and **Module 14's multimodal-claims checklist** apply unchanged and are linked, not restated at length. **Sanity gates adapted to this module:** a re-implementation with the true method and a large `n` recovers the planted effect within its interval; a null method (effect planted at 0) is "not distinguishable from 0" at the stated power and is *equivalent* to 0 within `δ` when `n` is large enough; the flaw scorer returns recall 1 and precision 1 on the key itself and recall 0 on an empty report; the winner's-curse simulation shows positive bias conditional on significance and none unconditionally.

- **L3:** **mathematics and statistics of evidence, in full:** (a) **the noise floor of a reported improvement**: **with per-seed standard deviation `σ` and `n` seeds per arm, the standard error of the difference of means is `σ√(2/n)`; a reported improvement `Δ` has `z = Δ/(σ√(2/n))`; the minimum detectable difference at two-sided `α = 0.05` and power 0.8 is `(1.96 + 0.84)·σ√(2/n) ≈ 2.8·σ√(2/n)` — derive; verified by simulation; a worked example in which a "+2.0" improvement is `z ≈ 1`**; (b) **best-of-`k` and selection**: **if a method is reported at the best of `k` i.i.d. runs and the baseline at the mean, the expected inflation is the expected maximum of `k` standard normals times `σ`: `E[max of k] = 0.564, 1.163, 1.539, 1.867` for `k = 2, 5, 10, 20` (to be *verified by numerical integration and simulation, not asserted*) and `≈ σ√(2 ln k)` as an upper-envelope approximation for large `k` — derive the exact integral `E[max] = k∫ x φ(x) Φ(x)^{k−1} dx` and evaluate it; the apparent improvement of a *null* method that simply received more tuning — a planted exercise paper whose true effect is zero**; (d) **multiple comparisons and forking paths (F13)**: **the probability of at least one nominal significant result among `m` independent tests at level `α` is `1 − (1 − α)^m` — derive;
