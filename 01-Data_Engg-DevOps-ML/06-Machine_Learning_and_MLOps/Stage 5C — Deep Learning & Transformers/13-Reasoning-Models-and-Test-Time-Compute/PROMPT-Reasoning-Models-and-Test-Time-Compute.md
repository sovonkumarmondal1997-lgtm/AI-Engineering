**Design decisions to state in Unit 0 and Unit 7 (deliberate, `JUDGEMENT`):**

1. **"Reasoning" is operationalised, not assumed.** The word is used in this module **only to mean a measured behaviour: generating intermediate tokens before the answer, which improves accuracy on tasks whose solution requires serial computation, as established by a controlled comparison against a direct answer at a stated budget on a planted task.** **Nothing is claimed about what a model "understands" or whether its written steps reflect its internal computation; the latter is *tested* (causal-intervention tests on the written steps) and the result is reported as a property of the tested model and task.**
2. **Two ground-truth instruments.** **(a) Planted tasks with a *depth* parameter** let the learner measure the accuracy-versus-depth curve with and without intermediate tokens, for models of different layer counts, and so test the hypothesis that *tokens substitute for depth* — directly, on their own models. **(b) Synthetic policies with planted per-problem success probabilities `p_x` (and a planted distractor-answer distribution)** let every aggregation and allocation formula — majority and plurality voting, pass@`n` with heterogeneous difficulty (including its power-law tail), best-of-`n` against an imperfect verifier (including its precision ceiling), sequential agreement stopping, and optimal budget allocation — be verified **exactly** against closed forms and enumeration, so that when the same quantities are measured on trained models the measurement error and the model effect are separable.
3. **Everything is compared at equal *inference* budgets, and the compute axis is defined first.** Every curve has a stated compute axis (**total inference tokens, prefill and decode counted separately, hidden reasoning tokens included; and FLOPs by the Module 10/12 formulas**), **methods are compared at equal compute (the budget-parity table is mandatory), and "accuracy per unit cost" is reported with its intervals**. **Training cost is counted when a comparison involves different trained models (the break-even analysis of Unit 4).** **No wall-clock or hardware claim is made beyond symbolic models and the learner's own CPU, at the edge, labelled `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`.**
4. **Relationship to neighbouring modules.** Module 11's RLVR on `reasongen` and Module 12's sampling/caching/speculation machinery are **reused, not re-taught**; this module is where they are **assembled into a test-time-compute study**. ## Conventions stated once, used everywhere (state them in Unit 0, apply in all units)

- **The compute-axis and token-accounting convention (signature habit).** **Every curve states its compute axis before its accuracy axis.** For a query `x` answered by a method that makes `n` model calls, the **inference cost is `T_inf(x) = Σ_calls (T_prefill + T_decode)` in tokens, with `T_prefill` the prompt tokens processed and `T_decode` the tokens generated — *including hidden or intermediate reasoning tokens, discarded samples, rejected drafts' verified tokens, verifier calls, and revision rounds*; the corresponding FLOPs use the Module 10/12 formulas (`2N` per processed token plus the attention term; a shared-prefix cache makes the prompt's prefill count once per distinct prefix — the accounting states which)**. **Costs in money-like units are `c_in·T_prefill + c_out·T_decode` with `c_in`, `c_out` symbolic (`[HYPOTHETICAL]` ratios only; never real prices).** **Latency is modelled symbolically: `T_prefill/F_pre + T_decode·t_tok` per sequential chain, parallel samples sharing wall-clock up to a stated batch limit (`[HYPOTHETICAL]` parameters); the only measured time is the learner's own CPU at the edge.** **Methods are compared at equal total inference tokens (and equal FLOPs where stated) via a mandatory budget-parity table; "accuracy per unit cost" is reported with intervals; training cost enters only through the break-even analysis (Unit 4).**
- **Task, depth, and oracle conventions.** A **planted task family** has a **serial-depth parameter `d`** (the number of dependent steps required), an **exact oracle** (the answer and the canonical step sequence), a **step checker** (is step `i` correct given the previous steps), a **final-answer checker**, and **templates** (the unit of independence, as in Module 11). **Answer extraction is a specified, tested function (a strict parser by default); its loophole audit (Module 11 Unit 6) is repeated here for every scratchpad format; the extractor is part of the evaluated system and is versioned.**
- **Synthetic-policy convention.** A **synthetic policy** returns, for a prompt `x`, a sampled answer: the correct answer with the planted probability `p_x`, otherwise a draw from a planted distractor distribution over `m` wrong answers (uniform or specified); **its own sampling seed stream and token-length model are specified**; **every aggregation, verifier, stopping, and allocation formula is verified exactly against enumeration or closed form on synthetic policies before it is measured on trained models**.
- **Aggregation conventions.** **Majority/plurality voting** over `n` samples with a **specified tie-break (uniformly random among tied answers, using a named random stream — never by position or lexical order)**; **best-of-`n`** with a specified scorer (exact checker as an oracle upper bound, a learned verifier, a likelihood or confidence score) and a **fallback when no sample is accepted**; **pass@`n`** by the Module 11 unbiased estimator; **weighted voting** states its weights. **A vote's unit is the extracted final answer, not the chain.**
- **The evaluation convention (inherited from Module 11, with test-time additions).** Prompts are scored by **programmatic checkers**; **the unit of independence is the template (cluster bootstrap over templates, `B` stated)**; **paired comparisons between methods on the same prompts use the exact McNemar test or a paired template-cluster bootstrap**; **results are reported *stratified by serial depth and by prompt difficulty* (the pooled average hides where reasoning helps and where it hurts)**; **held-out templates and held-out instances are separated**; **routers, stopping rules, and allocation policies are tuned on tuning prompts and reported on held-out prompts; the sweep optimism is measured**; **sampling seed, prompt, and training seed are separate variance sources (Module 12)**.
- **Faithfulness-test convention.** Whether written reasoning is *causally used* is tested **by interventions on the written steps** (corrupt one intermediate step and continue generation from the corrupted prefix; truncate the chain and force an answer; replace steps by filler; permute steps) and measured by **the final-answer change rate and the error-propagation rate against calibrated reference policies — a planted *faithful* policy (computes through the steps) and a planted *decorative* policy (computes the answer independently and writes steps for show) — so that the test's sensitivity and specificity are themselves measured**; the result is a property of the tested model, task, and intervention **and is never generalised to "the model's reasoning" or to other models**.
- **Fair-comparison protocol (inherited from Module 05, with Module 11/12 additions).** **Equal inference budgets; the same trained checkpoint for pure test-time methods; equal training budgets and starting checkpoints when comparing trained variants (Module 11); ≥ 5 paired sampling seeds and ≥ 3 training seeds for headline comparisons (`--quick` 3/2); effect sizes with template-cluster intervals; trials reported; no method ranking is ever stated** (link `../05-Sequence-Models-and-Tabular-Deep-Learning/README.md`, content not yet authored — mark `OPEN` until reconciled).
