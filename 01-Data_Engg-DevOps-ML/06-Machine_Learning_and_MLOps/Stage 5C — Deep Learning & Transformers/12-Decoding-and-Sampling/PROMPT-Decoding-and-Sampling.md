**Design decisions to state in Unit 0 and Unit 8 (deliberate, `JUDGEMENT`):**

1. **Decoding is studied where the exact answer is computable.** Real decoding studies are judged by eye or by noisy proxies. **In the microworld (small vocabulary, short sequences) the learner computes the exact distribution a model assigns to every sequence and the exact distribution a sampler induces (temperature, top-`k`, top-`p`, min-`p`, constraints, repetition penalties), and compares it with the planted source distribution by total-variation distance, KL divergence, validity mass, entropy, and coverage — with no Monte Carlo error.** On larger tasks the same quantities are estimated with stated sample sizes and intervals. **Conclusions hold for these models, tasks, and scales only; no "recommended" parameter values are ever stated.**
2. **Equivalence is proved by tests, not asserted.** **The KV-cached decoder equals the cache-free decoder (logits within a stated tolerance, exhaustively over small configurations, including RoPE positions, left-padded batches, sliding windows, and beam reordering); speculative decoding's output distribution equals the target model's (exactly, by enumeration in the microworld, and statistically by chi-square on larger problems); constrained decoding's distortion relative to conditioning on validity is computed exactly.** Every equivalence test has a planted-bug variant it must reject.
3. **The unit of variation is separated.** Every sampling result has three sources of variance — **the sampling seed (within a model and prompt), the prompt (and its template), and the training seed (between models)** — which the module **measures and decomposes** rather than conflates; sampling settings are **tuned on tuning prompts and reported on held-out prompts** (the best-of-`k` optimism of a settings sweep is measured).
4. **Speed claims are symbolic or counted.** Decoding speed on a CPU says little about accelerators; **the module derives FLOPs, bytes, and sequential-depth counts by formula, verifies them against counters, and reports wall-clock only for the learner's own CPU, at the edge with spread, labelled `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`; no claim about any real system's latency is made.**

**Priority:** Unit 3 (truncation samplers — the exact-distribution unit), Unit 4 (KV cache — the equivalence-and-accounting unit), Unit 5 (speculative decoding — the exactness-proof unit), and Unit 8 (the project) get the deepest treatment; Units 1, 2 must be complete and rigorous; Units 0, 6, 7 meet their stated requirements.

## Conventions stated once, used everywhere (state them in Unit 0, apply in all units)

- **Logits, processors, and the processing-order convention (a table, stated once; mixing is a defect).** The model outputs **logits `z ∈ ℝ^V`** (batched `(B, V)`); **a decoding strategy is a pipeline of *pure* logit/probability processors followed by a sampler or an argmax**; the **fixed, documented order of this module is: logit bias and constraint masks → repetition/frequency/presence penalties → temperature → top-`k` → top-`p` → min-`p` → renormalisation → sampling**; **libraries differ in the order and in the exact definitions (`[VERIFY]` against each library's documentation; the module never asserts what any library does) — the learner measures how reordering changes the induced distribution**. **The *processed distribution* `p̃(· | context)` is the distribution actually sampled from; every metric is computed on `p̃` (and on the induced *sequence* distribution), never on the raw model distribution unless stated.** Masked entries are `−∞` in logit space and `0` in probability space; **an empty support after processing is an error with a defined policy (fall back to the argmax of the pre-truncation distribution) and a test**.
- **Sequence-distribution and exact-microworld conventions.** For a model `p(y | x) = ∏_t p(y_t | x, y_{<t})` over sequences that end in an end-of-sequence token or at a maximum length, **the microworld enumerates every sequence (vocabulary ≤ 8, length ≤ 6) and stores its exact probability under the model, under the source, and under any processor chain** (the induced sequence distribution is the product of the *processed* conditionals along the sequence). **Exact quantities** — total-variation distance, KL divergence in both directions, validity mass (the mass on sequences accepted by the task's validity oracle), entropy, coverage (the source mass on sequences with non-zero generated probability), expected gold reward — **are computed without sampling; a Monte Carlo estimate of any such quantity states its sample size and interval and is checked against the exact value in the microworld.** **Probabilities are computed in `float64` in the microworld.**
- **Determinism and randomness conventions.** **Sampling uses an explicit `torch.Generator` per sequence stream (never the global generator), seeded from named spawn keys (`sample`, `prompt`, `draft`, `accept`, `bootstrap`); the sampling seed is distinct from the training seed.** The module defines **determinism levels**: **D0** — same seed, same machine, same batch composition and thread count reproduces the generation; **D1** — the *logits* of a sequence are invariant (to a stated floating-point tolerance) to batch composition, and the **greedy output is invariant except where the top-two logit margin is smaller than the numerical error (the flip rate is measured, not assumed zero)**; **bitwise reproducibility across machines, versions, thread counts, or devices is never promised.**
- **KV-cache conventions (set in Module 07; applied here).** **Cache layout `(n_layer, 2, B, n_kv, T_max, d_k)` (keys then values), per-sequence `cache_len`, absolute zero-indexed positions, and the cache-offset causal mask `allow[i, j] = (j ≤ cache_len + i)` for a query block of length `T_q`; rotary embeddings are applied to keys *before* they are cached, at their absolute positions, with the pairing convention declared in Module 07; left-padded batches carry per-sequence position ids and an attention mask; the cache is the *only* mutable state of a decoder and is owned by a single sequence or beam.** The **cache-equivalence criterion** is stated once: **for every position, the cached decoder's logits equal the cache-free decoder's logits to a stated tolerance (float64: ≤ the stated bound; float32: looser and justified)**, over an exhaustive small grid of configurations.
- **Speculative-decoding notation.** **`p` — the target model's processed distribution; `q` — the draft model's processed distribution *with the same processors applied to both*; `γ` — the number of draft tokens per verification step; `α = Σ_x min(p(x), q(x)) = 1 − TV(p, q)` — the per-position acceptance probability; `E` — the expected number of tokens produced per target forward pass; `c = t_draft / t_target` — the per-token cost ratio (symbolic).**
- **The evaluation convention.** Generated text is evaluated by **programmatic measures**: **quality** (the task's gold reward, checker pass rate, or validity mass), **fidelity to the source** (TV/KL against the exact source distribution in the microworld; `n`-gram-statistics divergence on larger tasks), **diversity** (entropy of the induced distribution — exact in the microworld, plug-in with a bias correction otherwise (Miller–Madow stated); distinct-`n`; coverage), **repetition** (the fraction of repeated `n`-grams; degenerate-loop rate), **termination** (termination rate; length distribution), **calibration** (reliability of the model's probabilities, `5B-04`), and **cost** (tokens, FLOPs, bytes, sequential depth); **"quality" is never judged by eye, and no LLM judge is used**. **The unit of independence is the prompt *template* (`5C-11`): intervals are cluster bootstraps over templates (`B` stated); the sampling seed, the prompt, and the training seed are three separate variance sources, decomposed (ANOVA-style) and reported**; **sample sizes per prompt are chosen by a stated precision target (derived), not by convenience**.

## Unit 5 — Speculative-decoding intuition — **core unit (exactness proof)**

- **L1:** **the idea** (a small, fast *draft* model guesses the next few tokens; the large *target* model checks all the guesses in *one* forward pass — scoring several positions in parallel is possible because a causal model produces next-token distributions for every prefix of a given sequence at once — and keeps the ones it agrees with; when it disagrees it corrects the first wrong guess); **why this can be faster** (the target model's sequential steps are the expensive part; if on average several tokens are accepted per target pass, fewer target passes are needed per token); **why this can be *exactly* right** (the acceptance rule is designed so that the final tokens are distributed exactly as if the target model had generated them alone — **the central claim, proved in L3 and verified exactly in the lab**); **the acceptance rule in words** (accept the draft token `x` with probability `min(1, p(x)/q(x))`, where `p` is the target's and `q` the draft's probability; on rejection, sample a replacement from the "leftover" distribution `∝ max(0, p − q)`; if all `γ` drafts are accepted, the target's last distribution yields one extra "bonus" token); **a hand-worked example** (a 3-token vocabulary, given `p` and `q`: acceptance probabilities, the residual distribution, and the exact output distribution — hand-verified to equal `p`); **greedy speculative decoding** (at `τ = 0`: accept iff the draft token equals the target's argmax); **what can go wrong** (a draft model that disagrees too often gives no speed-up; mismatched tokenisers; different sampling processors applied to the two models).
- **L2:** **implement and verify (`decodelab.spec`)**: **(a) the verification step** (given the draft tokens, their draft probabilities `q_i`, and the target's distributions `p_i` at each draft position plus the bonus position: the sequential accept/reject loop with explicit random numbers; the residual distribution; the bonus token; **the output length and the cache truncation on rejection**); **(b) the speculative decoder** (draft proposes `γ` tokens autoregressively with its own cache; - **L3:** **mathematics, in full:** (a) **the losslessness theorem**: **for one position, with draft `X ∼ q`, accept with probability `min(1, p(X)/q(X))`, and on rejection draw from `p'(x) = max(0, p(x) − q(x))/Σ_{x'} max(0, p(x') − q(x'))`, the output has distribution exactly `p` — prove in full: `P(out = x) = q(x)·min(1, p(x)/q(x)) + P(reject)·p'(x) = min(p(x), q(x)) + (Σ_{x'} max(0, p(x') − q(x')))·p'(x) = min(p(x), q(x)) + max(0, p(x) − q(x)) = p(x)`, using `P(reject) = 1 − Σ_x min(p(x), q(x)) = Σ_x max(0, p(x) − q(x))` (both sums are `1 − Σ min`)**;
