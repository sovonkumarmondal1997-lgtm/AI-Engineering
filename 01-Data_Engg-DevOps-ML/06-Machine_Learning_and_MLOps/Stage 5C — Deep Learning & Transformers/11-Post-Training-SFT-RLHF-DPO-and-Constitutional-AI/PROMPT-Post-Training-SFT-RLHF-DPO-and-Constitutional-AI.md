**Design decisions to state in Unit 0 and Unit 9 (deliberate, `JUDGEMENT`):**

1. **The learner post-trains their own base model on a planted world; no pretrained models are used.** The project is named "compare base vs instruct models", and the usual version compares two downloaded checkpoints. **The repository forbids downloaded weights, so the graded study instead builds both sides: the base model is the learner's own Module 09 model pretrained on a planted-truth "world" corpus (`worldgen`), and the instruct models are that base model post-trained by SFT, preference optimisation, and reinforcement learning on synthetic tasks (`instructgen`, `prefgen`).** **Optionally**, a learner who already holds small base/instruct checkpoints locally by their own means may evaluate them through a documented adapter interface — **reported separately, never graded, licence and provenance noted `[VERIFY]`; the module neither downloads nor requires them.**
2. **Everything is scored against ground truth the learner controls.** **`instructgen` tasks have programmatic checkers (exact answers, format constraints, a planted "forbidden-category" refusal rule, a planted sycophancy trap); `prefgen` preference pairs are generated from a planted gold reward `r*` with annotator noise (a Bradley–Terry logit noise) and *planted biases* (a length bias, a position bias, a sycophancy bias); the Bayes-optimal preference accuracy and the maximum achievable gold reward are computed exactly.** This makes **reward over-optimisation, reward hacking, judge bias, over-refusal, capability regression, and "RL expands pass@k or does not" directly measurable** — which no real-model study at laptop scale can do. **Conclusions hold for these tasks, models, and scales only; no claim about real assistants, real feedback, or real safety is made.**
3. **Constitutional/AI-feedback methods are taught as a data-flow-and-evaluation pipeline with simulated critics and judges.** Tiny models cannot critique or follow principles. **The "constitution" is a small set of written, machine-checkable rules; the "AI judge" is a programmatic labeller with controllable accuracy, noise, and planted biases (position, length, self-preference); the critique–revision step is a rule-driven revision or rejection sampling against the rules.** The learner builds and evaluates **the pipeline, its error propagation, its principle-conflict trade-offs, and its failure modes** — not the emergent capability that makes the method work at scale. **State that limitation plainly and never imply the toy version reproduces a real system's behaviour.**
4. **Relationship to neighbouring modules.** Module 07's attention/block and Module 09's model are **reused unchanged**; Module 10's compute and memory ledgers and stability instrumentation are **reused**; **sampling strategies and KV-cached generation are Module 12 (here generation is the minimal temperature-sampling loop)**; **reasoning-time scaling, chain-of-thought, and budgets are Module 13 (here reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards on planted multi-step tasks is the only reasoning content)**; **evaluation of real assistants and paper reproduction are Module 15**. ## Conventions stated once, used everywhere (state them in Unit 0, apply in all units)

- **Policy, sequence, and reward notation (a table, stated once; mixing is a defect).** A **prompt** is `x`; a **response** is `y = (y₁, …, y_L)` ending in an end-of-turn token; the **policy** `π_θ(y | x) = ∏_t π_θ(y_t | x, y_{<t})`; the **reference policy** `π_ref` is the frozen SFT (or base) model; the **sequence log-probability** `log π(y | x) = Σ_t log π(y_t | x, y_{<t})` **summed over response tokens only** (length-normalised variants are named as such); a **reward** `r(x, y)` is a scalar; **preference data** are triples `(x, y_w, y_l)`; **`KL(π ‖ π_ref)` is the sequence-level KL `E_{y ∼ π}[log π(y | x)/π_ref(y | x)]` averaged over prompts**; **KL is estimated with the stated estimator (Unit 3) and, in the exact-enumeration regime, computed exactly**. **The gold reward `r*` is the planted truth; a *proxy* reward `r̂` is anything learned or simulated; a model is never evaluated on the signal it was optimised against.**
- **Chat-template and loss-mask convention.** Conversations are rendered by a **versioned chat template** (role markers, turn delimiters, an end-of-turn token); **role markers and the end-of-turn token are special tokens that ordinary text cannot produce** (Module 08's `allowed_special` policy; **a test that user text containing a role-marker string cannot open a new turn**); **the training loss is token-weighted cross-entropy over assistant-response tokens (including the end-of-turn token) only — prompt tokens are masked (`ignore_index`)**; **the mask is constructed once, tested exhaustively on small conversations, and logged with every run**.
- **Gold-and-planted-truth convention (the module's signature habit).** Every study is run in a world with **a planted gold reward `r*`, programmatic checkers, a manifest of planted biases and noise levels, an oracle policy (the maximum achievable gold reward), a reference lower bound (the base model), the Bayes-optimal preference accuracy given the annotator noise, and — in the exact-enumeration micro-regime — exact policy distributions, rewards, and KL values**; **a proxy reward is never reported without the gold reward and the KL alongside it; a measured gold reward above the oracle's, a preference accuracy above the Bayes-optimal accuracy, or a KL below zero means a bug or a leak.**
- **The evaluation convention.** Behaviour is scored by **programmatic checkers** (exact-match answers, format constraints, a refusal rule, a sycophancy trap) **and the gold reward**; **an LLM-style "judge" appears only as a *simulated* judge with known, planted accuracy and biases**; **the unit of independence is the prompt *template* (prompts from one template are correlated): intervals are computed by a cluster bootstrap over templates (`B` stated), never over individual prompts or tokens**; **held-out evaluation uses held-out templates and held-out instances separately (generalisation across templates is the harder test)**; **SFT/preference/RL training data and evaluation data are decontaminated by template and by `n`-gram overlap (Module 10)**; **the base model is evaluated with a *pre-registered, fairly tuned* prompt set (zero-shot and `k`-shot, chosen on tuning prompts, reported on held-out prompts) — never only with a prompt it cannot follow**.
- **The cost-of-post-training convention.** Every post-trained model is reported with **what it bought** (gold reward, task accuracy, format compliance, refusal accuracy) **and what it cost** (**capability retention**: bits per byte on held-out pretraining-distribution text and accuracy on planted knowledge probes; **calibration**: expected calibration error of answer confidences (link `5B-04`); **diversity**: distinct-`n` rates and the entropy of the response distribution at fixed prompts; **over-refusal**; **sycophancy**; **response length**; **KL to the reference**; **compute and memory ledgers**).
- **The fair-comparison protocol (inherited from Module 05, with post-training additions).** Methods (SFT variants, reward-model-based RL, DPO and its variants, best-of-`n`, RL for reasoning) are compared at **equal training-compute budgets (FLOPs and tokens; the budget-parity table is mandatory), equal data budgets where stated, the same starting checkpoint, ≥ 3 paired seeds (≥ 5 for a headline comparison; `--quick` ≥ 2/3), disjoint tuning and reporting seeds, hyperparameters tuned per method on tuning prompts with an equal budgeted protocol, effect sizes with template-cluster bootstrap intervals, the number of configurations and runs (including failed and discarded RL runs) reported; and — for any preference/RL method — compared on the **reward–KL frontier, not at a single point**; no method ranking is ever stated** (link `../05-Sequence-Models-and-Tabular-Deep-Learning/README.md`, content not yet authored — mark `OPEN` until reconciled).
- **Inherited protocols.** The **5C training-run card** (all eighteen items; `../00-Stage-Overview/README.md`, not yet authored — mark `OPEN` until reconciled), **Module 03's `Trainer`, logging, resume-equivalence, and tuning protocol**, **Module 02's equivalence, measurement, reproducibility, and safe-checkpoint conventions**, **Module 07's sanity gates and masks**, **Module 08's special-token and injection conventions**, **Module 09's data-hygiene and memorisation conventions**, **Module 10's counting conventions (`N_total`/`N_ne`/`D`/`C`), compute/memory/energy ledger, fitting conventions, and stability instrumentation**, and **5B-13's RL conventions** (the `terminated` versus `truncated` distinction; evaluation under separate seeds; the planted-optimum check) apply unchanged and are linked, not restated at length. **Sanity gates adapted to post-training:** the response-only loss mask is exact; initial SFT loss on response tokens `≈ ln V` for the untrained head or the base model's measured value on the response distribution; **overfit one SFT batch**; **the reference-policy log-probabilities are reproducible (same value from two code paths)**; **the DPO and RM losses at initialisation equal `ln 2` when the policy equals the reference / the reward is constant**; **the KL of the policy to itself is zero**; **an oracle policy attains the maximum gold reward; a random policy attains the planted minimum**.
