# 4. TEACHING MODEL — FOUR LEVELS APPLIED TO A CAPSTONE (UNITS 1–4)

A capstone is learned by **building it four times at increasing rigour**, not by reading about it. Each of Units 1–4 progresses through **exactly these four levels** with identical headings.

| Level | Name | Learner outcome for the capstone |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Guided build — Foundations | Reproduces a small reference solution end to end on the planted data: frame the decision, generate data, build the baseline, produce a first honest evaluation, and fill the first version of the decision memo. |
| **L2** | Full pipeline — Working Practice | Builds the complete pipeline unaided: all required components, tests, packaging, tracking, and a model card; meets every **must** in the rubric. |
| **L3** | Rigour — Engineering Rigor | Hardens the capstone: ablations, sensitivity and stress scenarios, repeated seeds, uncertainty on every claim, a written failure analysis, and a reproducibility audit; meets every **should** in the rubric. |
| **L4** | Review and mastery | Reviews a peer's (or a deliberately flawed reference) capstone against the rubric, finds planted flaws, writes the review, defends their own capstone in a mock review, and extends it to a production plan (monitoring, drift, rollback). |

## Unit 1 — Capstone 1: rare-event model with calibration and conformal uncertainty

- **Brief:** a rare-event decision (e.g. a review-queue triage problem) with a fixed **alert capacity** and asymmetric error costs; deliverable: a calibrated risk model, a justified operating threshold under the capacity constraint, and **conformal uncertainty** with an honest statement of what coverage guarantees do and do not mean here.
- **Home modules (link, never re-teach):** `../03-…` (framing, cost-sensitive learning, resampling inside folds, expected-cost thresholds, alert capacity); `../04-…` (calibration vs discrimination, reliability diagrams, Brier, Platt/isotonic, calibration-data leakage prevention); `../02-…` (split conformal prediction, abstention and human review); `../01-…` (nested/deployment-matched validation, comparing models); `../07-…` (explanations and their limits) and `../06-…` (anomaly baselines) as optional components.
- **Planted data:** a generator with a known base rate (e.g. 0.5–2%, parameterised), a known label mechanism with a **calibration distortion**, a **temporal shift** between development and deployment, a duplicated-entity trap across splits, a leaky feature, and a **cost matrix**; the hidden truth includes the true risk for each case so calibration and ranking can be scored against truth.
- **Required components:** (1) problem framing and cost model; (2) strong simple baseline (e.g. regularised logistic regression with class weights; a rule); (3) a gradient-boosted candidate; (4) resampling/weighting done **inside** folds only; (5) calibration (Platt and isotonic) with a **separate calibration set**, reliability diagram, ECE with its caveats, Brier decomposition; (6) **split conformal** prediction sets (or conformal risk control for the alert decision), with the calibration/conformal set disjoint from the training and the test sets, and **coverage checked on a temporally shifted test set** (showing where exchangeability breaks); (7) threshold selection by **expected cost under the alert capacity** (and the sensitivity of the threshold to cost-ratio and base-rate uncertainty); (8) abstention/human-review policy with measured review burden; (9) failure and slice analysis; (10) model card and decision memo.
- **Stress scenarios (≥ 4):** base-rate shift; calibration drift over time; shifted cost ratio; capacity reduced by half; label delay.
- **Negative results to show:** accuracy and ROC-AUC looking good while precision at the alert budget is poor; calibration on the training set hiding miscalibration; conformal coverage dropping under shift; SMOTE outside the fold inflating validation.
- **Adversarial situations (L4):** threshold tuned on the test set; calibrator fit on the same set it is evaluated on; resampling before the split; conformal set reused for threshold tuning without disclosure; ECE with unstable binning used as the headline; a "guarantee" claimed under shift.

## Unit 2 — Capstone 2: time-series forecasting and backtesting

- **Brief:** a multi-series demand/volume forecasting decision with a stated **horizon**, a **cost of under/over-forecasting**, and a planning cadence; deliverable: a validated forecasting pipeline, **rolling-origin backtest**, a prediction-interval method with measured coverage, and a decision-relevant error analysis.
- **Home modules:** `../08-…` (all: decomposition, baselines, lag/rolling features, recursive vs direct, ARIMA/ETS, ML forecasting, global/hierarchical models, probabilistic forecasting, **rolling-origin backtesting and metrics**, missing timestamps/intermittent demand/drift); `../01-…` (validation, comparison across series); `../02-…` (conformalized intervals) ; `../04-…` (calibration of probabilistic forecasts, link).
- **Planted data:** a generator of many series with known trend, multiple seasonalities, holiday effects, **intermittent-demand series**, missing timestamps, a level shift, a drift point, a hierarchy (store/region/total), and known noise; hidden truth = the true generating process and the true future; **traps**: a future-leaking lag/rolling feature, a promotion calendar known only after the fact, duplicated timestamps.
- **Required components:** (1) framing, horizon(s), cost model; (2) **naive and seasonal-naive baselines** (always reported); (3) at least one statistical model (ETS/ARIMA-family via `statsmodels` if installed, or from scratch for a simple case) and one **global gradient-boosted model** with leakage-safe lag/rolling features (as-of correctness tested); (4) recursive vs direct horizon strategy compared; (5) **rolling-origin backtest** with ≥ 8 origins, expanding vs sliding window compared, a **gap** where the pipeline needs one; (6) metrics chosen for the data (MAE/RMSE, MASE, sMAPE caveats, **a scaled metric for intermittent series**, bias/ME, **pinball/quantile loss and interval coverage** and width); (7) **prediction intervals** (quantile models and/or conformalised) with coverage measured **per horizon and across origins**; (8) hierarchical coherence check (reconciliation named, simple bottom-up/top-down shown); (9) drift/level-shift handling and detection; (10) error analysis by series type, horizon, and calendar slice; model card and decision memo.
- **Stress scenarios (≥ 4):** level shift; missing timestamps; new series (cold start); holiday not in training; changed noise level.
- **Negative results:** a random-split "backtest" that overstates accuracy (measured); a leaky lag feature that makes the model look excellent; MAPE failure on intermittent data; the seasonal-naive baseline beating a fancy model on some series; interval under-coverage at long horizons.
- **Adversarial situations (L4):** features computed with the full series; hyperparameters tuned across all origins including the evaluation origins; a metric aggregated in a way that lets large series dominate; an interval method reported without per-horizon coverage; a forecast that cannot be reproduced because the series order changed.

## Unit 3 — Capstone 3: recommender system with ranking metrics

- **Brief:** a top-*k* recommendation decision with a stated **slate size**, a cold-start population, and a business trade-off (relevance vs diversity vs exposure); deliverable: a baseline-to-candidate-generation-to-ranking pipeline with **honest offline evaluation** and an explicit analysis of its limits.
- **Home modules:** `../09-…` (all: baselines, feedback types, CF, MF, content/hybrid, two-tower retrieval (concept level, NumPy-scale), cold start, pointwise/pairwise/listwise ranking, **P/R@k, MAP, NDCG**, offline vs online evaluation, popularity bias/diversity/feedback loops); `../01-…` (splits and comparison); `../10-…` (selection and exposure bias, off-policy ideas); `../11-…` (implicit-feedback noise, link); `../04-…` (calibration of ranking scores, link).
- **Planted data:** a generator with known user and item latent factors, **implicit feedback produced through an exposure process** (so observed clicks ≠ preference), popularity skew, cold-start users/items, temporal drift in taste, and optionally a logging policy with known propensities; hidden truth = true preferences for **all** user–item pairs, so offline metrics can be compared with **ground-truth** quality; **traps**: a random split that leaks the future, users in both splits' candidate sets with duplicated interactions, a popularity artefact.
- **Required components:** (1) framing, slate size, metrics tied to the decision; (2) baselines: **popularity**, recency, and item–item co-occurrence (always reported); (3) collaborative filtering and/or matrix factorisation implemented from scratch (ALS or SGD) with agreement checks on tiny data; (4) a candidate-generation stage and a **re-ranking** stage (a pointwise or pairwise model with features), with the retrieval recall measured; (5) **time-based split** with per-user holdout and a **leave-out protocol that is leakage-safe** (tested); (6) metrics: **P@k, R@k, MAP@k, NDCG@k**, coverage, diversity, novelty/popularity bias, with per-user bootstrap intervals and paired comparisons; (7) **cold-start** performance reported separately; (8) the **exposure-bias analysis**: compare offline rankings with ground-truth preference ranking and show how metric ranking of models changes; (9) a simple **feedback-loop simulation** (popularity amplification under retraining) with a mitigation; (10) model card and decision memo (including what an online test would need to confirm — link `../10-…`).
- **Stress scenarios (≥ 4):** more cold-start users; stronger popularity skew; sparser interactions; taste drift; exposure policy change.
- **Negative results:** a popularity baseline beating a learned model on offline NDCG; offline metric winners that lose on ground-truth quality; evaluation on already-seen items inflating NDCG; MF collapsing for cold-start users.
- **Adversarial situations (L4):** NDCG computed after filtering to interacted items; item IDs leaking through the split; negatives sampled differently for models being compared; metric reported at one *k*; a claimed "lift" within the bootstrap interval; a diversity gain obtained by degrading relevance, unreported.
