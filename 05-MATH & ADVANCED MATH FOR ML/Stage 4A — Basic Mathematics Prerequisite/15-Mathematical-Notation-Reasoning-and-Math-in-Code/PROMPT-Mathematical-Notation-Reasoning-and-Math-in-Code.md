## Unit 5 — Basic logic: if–then, counterexamples, and necessary vs sufficient

## Unit 6 — Problem-solving strategy and verifying answers
- **L1:** **a routine: (1) understand — restate the problem, list the knowns, unknowns, and conditions, draw a picture; (2) plan — pick a method; (3) carry out — one justified step at a time; (4) look back — check the answer** (Pólya's four phases, **cited by name as a teaching framework, `[VERIFY]`**; **no claim that it improves outcomes beyond what is cited**); **estimate first (M01 Unit 7) and compare the exact answer to the estimate**; **units and dimensions as a check (M01 Unit 8)**; **verification by substitution: plug a candidate back into the original statement, not into the last line of your own work (M04 Unit 15)**; **the first verification habit in code: `assert` a hand-computed value**; Python: `assert`, a tiny `pytest` file, a seeded `random.Random` for test data.
- **L2:** **strategy catalogue: restate in your own words; solve a smaller or special case; draw a diagram; make a table; work backwards; look for a pattern and then *test* it (a pattern is a hypothesis); symmetry; extreme or boundary cases (limits, `0`, `1`, `n = 0`); dimensional analysis; reduce to a known problem**; **verification catalogue: (a) substitution into the original; (b) an independent second method; (c) a special-case test; (d) a boundary test; (e) a property check (e.g., a result that must be nonnegative, symmetric, monotone, or scale correctly); (f) an estimate**; **extraneous results: squaring or multiplying by an expression that may be zero can add solutions, so *every* candidate must be checked in the original (M04 Unit 15)**; **checking versus completeness: a check shows a candidate *is* a solution, not that it is the *only* one; completeness needs a reversible derivation or a counting argument (e.g., a degree argument)**; **writing up a solution: given / to find / plan / steps with justifications / check / conclusion with status**; **error taxonomy: slip vs misconception vs gap (S3-20 idea, linked, not rebuilt) and the learner's error log**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **verifying a hand-derived gradient or formula against a finite check on a stated tiny case (the finite-difference idea is M13; Stage 4B develops it) — the *verification attitude* only; unit-testing a translated formula against a hand-computed example; a metric implementation compared with an exact hand count on a toy confusion table**; **timeboxing: when to stop and ask (`[JUDGEMENT]`, with `[ASSUMPTION]` minutes)**.
