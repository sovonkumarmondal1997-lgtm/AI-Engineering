## Unit 2 — Arithmetic sequences
- **L1:** **an arithmetic sequence has a constant difference `d`: `aₙ₊₁ − aₙ = d`**; **the explicit formula `aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d` (1-based; `a₀ + n·d` in 0-based form)**; **recognizing one from a list or a table (constant differences)**; **finding a missing term**; **the graph of the terms is a set of points on a line with slope `d` (M07 Unit 6 link)**; **the sum of the first `n` terms `Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2`**; Python: `range(a, b, d)` vs the formula.
- **L2:** **finding `a₁` and `d` from two terms (a 2×2 linear system, M04 Units 11–12; a single linear equation when `d` is known)**; **counting terms: the number of terms from `a₁` to `aₙ` is `(aₙ − a₁)/d + 1`, an integer only if `d` divides the gap — the fencepost problem**; **arithmetic means (the middle term of three consecutive terms is the average of its neighbors)**; **sums of consecutive integers and of arithmetic runs**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **a linear learning-rate warm-up `ηₙ = η₀ + n·δ` and the total of the first `N` learning rates**; **nested-loop counts as arithmetic sums: the inner loop runs `1, 2, …, n − 1` times, a total of `n(n − 1)/2` iterations (S3-01 link; the quadratic cost, named)**; **floating-point accumulation: adding a decimal increment `N` times vs the formula `a₁ + N·d` (measured on the learner's machine)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the explicit formula by induction**; (b) **characterization: a sequence has constant differences iff its general term is a linear function of `n` (`aₙ = pn + q`, with `d = p`)**; (c) **the sum formula two ways — by pairing the first and last terms (`2Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)`, writing the sum forwards and backwards) and by induction**; (d) **the equivalent form `Sₙ = n(2a₁ + (n − 1)d)/2`**; (e) **the number-of-terms formula and the divisibility condition**; (f) **the arithmetic-mean property `aₙ = (aₙ₋₁ + aₙ₊₁)/2`**; (g) **optional: two arithmetic progressions with differences `d₁` and `d₂` that share a term share infinitely many, forming a progression of difference `lcm(d₁, d₂)` (M01 Unit 6)**; **exhaustive verification**: **the sum formula against a brute-force loop for all integers `a₁, d ∈ [−5, 5]` and `n ∈ [1, 20]`, exactly**; the characterization over all integer sequences of length 5 with entries in `[0, 6]` (`7⁵ = 16,807`) against a brute-force constant-difference test; the divisibility condition over a stated grid.
- **L4:** **fencepost and off-by-one errors in counting terms and in `range` bounds (a catalogue with exact instances)**; **exact vs float accumulation (the measured error of `N` additions of `0.1`)**; **very large `n` and exact `int` growth**; **an arithmetic tool** `arith` (Tool card: fit `a₁, d` from two terms or from a table with exactness checks; explicit term; exact sum; term count with the divisibility check; intersection of two progressions; classification of a table as arithmetic/not (as `exact computation` on the given finite table); caps; stable errors); hostile inputs (`d = 0` handled as constant; non-integer gaps; huge `n` — caps); **testing**: the brute-force sum and constant-difference oracles; **mutation lab** (`n` vs `n − 1` in the explicit formula; the sum formula divided by `n`; term count without the `+ 1`; a float accumulation used as the oracle) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 3 — Geometric sequences
- **L1:** **a geometric sequence has a constant ratio `r`: `aₙ₊₁ = r·aₙ` (`aₙ ≠ 0`)**; **the explicit formula `aₙ = a₁·rⁿ⁻¹` (1-based; `a₀·rⁿ` in 0-based form)**; **recognizing one from a list (constant ratios)**; **growth (`r > 1`), decay (`0 < r < 1`), alternating (`r < 0`), constant (`r = 1`)**; **percent rates as ratios (M02 Unit 8, M05 Unit 6)**; **the sum of the first `n` terms `Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)` for `r ≠ 1`, and `n·a₁` for `r = 1`**; **the finite doubling sum `1 + 2 + ⋯ + 2^{k−1} = 2ᵏ − 1` as the case `a₁ = 1`, `r = 2` (M05 Unit 8 link)**; Python: `itertools.accumulate` with multiplication.
- **L2:** **finding `a₁` and `r` from two terms (`r^{m−n} = aₘ/aₙ` — a root equation: unique when the exponent is odd or the ratio positive, up to sign when even — M05 Unit 4)**; **geometric means (the middle term of three consecutive terms satisfies `aₙ² = aₙ₋₁aₙ₊₁`, so `|aₙ| = √(aₙ₋₁aₙ₊₁)` — M05 Unit 4 AM–GM link)**; **compound growth and decay as geometric sequences (M05 Unit 6)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **exponential learning-rate decay `ηₙ = η₀γⁿ` (the time unit of `n` stated: per step or per epoch), the total learning rate accumulated over `N` steps `Σ ηₙ`, dataset sizes that double each round**; **exponential growth overflowing floats and decay underflowing (measured)**; **the exact error term of the partial sum: `Sₙ = a₁/(1 − r) − a₁rⁿ/(1 − r)` for `r ≠ 1` (a preview of Unit 8)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the explicit formula by induction**; (b) **characterization: a sequence of non-zero terms has constant ratios iff its general term is `a₁rⁿ⁻¹`**; (c) **the sum formula two ways — multiply `Sₙ` by `r` and subtract (`(1 − r)Sₙ = a₁ − a₁rⁿ`), and by induction**; (d) **`r = 1` and `r = 0` degenerate cases**; (e) **the geometric-mean property `aₙ² = aₙ₋₁aₙ₊₁`**; (f) **for `0 < r < 1`, `Sₙ < a₁/(1 − r)` and the gap is exactly `a₁rⁿ/(1 − r)`; for `r > 1`, `Sₙ < aₙ·r/(r − 1)` (the last term dominates the sum — used in amortized analysis; S3-01 link)**; (g) **the sign pattern of `rⁿ` for negative `r`**; **exhaustive verification**: **the sum formula and the exact-gap identity against brute-force sums for `r ∈ {±1/3, ±1/2, ±2, ±3}`, integer `a₁ ∈ [−3, 3]`, and `n ∈ [1, 15]`, exactly**; the constant-ratio characterization over all integer sequences of length 5 with entries in `[1, 6]` (`6⁵ = 7,776`) against a brute-force ratio test; the domination bound for `r > 1`.
- **L4:** **overflow and underflow of `rⁿ` in floats (`OverflowError` vs `inf`; the measured thresholds on the learner's machine — M05 Unit 1)**; **exact growth of numerators and denominators with `Fraction` (caps)**; **ratios from measured data (`HEURISTIC` tolerance; exact for exact data)**; **a geometric tool** `geom` (Tool card: fit `a₁, r` from two terms (exact when the required root is rational, otherwise the tool reports `irrational ratio` and gives a `numerical evidence` value); explicit term; exact sum; exact gap `a₁rⁿ/(1 − r)`; classification of a table; caps; stable errors); hostile inputs (`r = 1`, `r = 0`, zero terms in the ratio test, huge `n` — caps); **testing**: the brute-force sum and ratio oracles; **mutation lab** (`rⁿ` vs `rⁿ⁻¹`; `1 − r` sign error in the denominator; `r = 1` divided by zero; exponent shifted when the start index changes) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 4 — Recursive definitions and recurrence relations
- **L1:** **a recursive definition gives the first term(s) and a rule for the next: `a₁ = c`, `aₙ₊₁ = f(aₙ)`**; **examples: arithmetic `aₙ₊₁ = aₙ + d`, geometric `aₙ₊₁ = r·aₙ`, factorial `n! = n·(n − 1)!` with `0! = 1`, Fibonacci `Fₙ₊₁ = Fₙ + Fₙ₋₁` (the convention for the first two terms differs between sources — `[VERIFY]`; the module states `F₁ = F₂ = 1`)**; **initial conditions are part of the definition**; **explicit vs recursive descriptions of the same sequence**; **computing terms by iteration (a loop) and by recursion (a function that calls itself — S3-05 link)**; Python: loops, recursion, `functools.lru_cache`.
- **L2:** **first-order linear recurrences `aₙ₊₁ = r·aₙ + b`**: **the fixed point `c = b/(1 − r)` for `r ≠ 1` (solve `c = rc + b`, M04 Unit 2) and the closed form `aₙ = c + rⁿ⁻¹(a₁ − c)`; for `r = 1` the sequence is arithmetic**; **unrolling a recurrence by repeated substitution**; **second-order linear recurrences and the characteristic equation `x² = px + q` for `aₙ₊₂ = p·aₙ₊₁ + q·aₙ` (M04 Units 6–7)**; **Fibonacci's closed form (Binet) with `φ = (1 + √5)/2` and `ψ = (1 − √5)/2`: `Fₙ = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5`, evaluated *exactly* with single-radical values (`QuadSurd`, M04 Unit 6)**; **the cost of naive recursion: the number of calls `C(n)` of the naive recursive Fibonacci is `2Fₙ − 1` when the recursion stops at the base cases `F₁ = F₂ = 1` (and `2Fₙ₊₁ − 1` when it stops at `F₀ = 0, F₁ = 1` — **the base cases are stated and the matching formula is derived, never quoted**), exponential in `n`; memoization or iteration makes it linear (S3-09 link; counted work, S3-01)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the exponential moving average `mₜ₊₁ = β·mₜ + (1 − β)·xₜ` as a first-order recurrence, and its unrolled form `mₜ = βᵗm₀ + (1 − β)Σ_{k=0}^{t−1} βᵗ⁻¹⁻ᵏ xₖ` — a weighted sum with geometrically decaying weights (M02 Unit 10 link; the bias toward `m₀` is visible in the unrolled form)**; **a learning-rate schedule defined recursively; momentum-style updates and recurrent hidden-state updates named only (forward references)**; **dynamic-programming recurrences named (S3-09; not re-taught)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the closed form of `aₙ₊₁ = r·aₙ + b` (induction; the fixed-point substitution `eₙ = aₙ − c` turns it into a geometric sequence `eₙ₊₁ = r·eₙ`)**; (b) **the EMA unrolling by induction, and that its weights `(1 − β)βᵏ` for `k = 0, …, t − 1` sum to `1 − βᵗ` (finite geometric sum, Unit 3), so the total weight on the initial value is `βᵗ`**; (c) **the naive Fibonacci call count by induction, for each of the two stated base-case conventions (`C(n) = 1 + C(n − 1) + C(n − 2)` with `C = 1` at the base cases)**; (d) **Binet's formula: both `φⁿ` and `ψⁿ` satisfy the recurrence (they are roots of `x² = x + 1`), so does their difference over `√5`, and it matches `F₁, F₂` — induction**; (e) **for `aₙ₊₁ = r·aₙ + b`, the sequence converges to the fixed point `c` iff `|r| < 1` (from the closed form and Unit 7's `rⁿ → 0` — forward-referenced and proved there)**; **exhaustive verification**: **the closed form vs iteration for all `r ∈ {±1/2, ±1/3, 2, −2, 3}`, `b ∈ [−3, 3]`, `a₁ ∈ [−3, 3]`, and `n ≤ 20`, exactly**; **Binet in exact `QuadSurd` arithmetic against the integer Fibonacci numbers for `n ≤ 40`**; **the call-count formula against an instrumented recursion for `n ≤ 25`**; **the first `n` at which the float evaluation of Binet's formula no longer rounds to the correct integer, found by a stated search and reported as the learner's run (`numerical evidence`)**; EMA unrolling exactly in `Fraction` arithmetic.
- **L4:** **recursion limits and iteration (`sys.getrecursionlimit` is documented; hitting it is a `RecursionError` — test, do not assume the value)**; **initial-condition and off-by-one errors in unrolling (a catalogue)**; **numerical error accumulation in float recurrences**; **a recurrence tool** `recur` (Tool card: strict parser for first- and second-order linear recurrences with constant coefficients and stated initial terms; exact iteration with caps; the fixed-point closed form for first order; for second order the characteristic roots when rational (closed form in `Fraction`) or a single square-free radical (closed form in `QuadSurd`), otherwise **refusal with a clear message**; verification of every closed form by substitution against iteration; instrumented call counters for the naive-vs-memoized demonstration; stable errors); hostile inputs (`r = 1`, zero leading coefficient, huge `n` — caps; non-linear recurrences refused); **testing**: closed-form-vs-iteration oracles; **mutation lab** (initial term off by one; wrong fixed point; EMA without the `(1 − β)`; Binet with `+ψⁿ`) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 5 — Summation and product notation
- **L1:** **sigma notation `Σ_{i=m}^{n} aᵢ`: the index `i`, the lower and upper bounds, the summand `aᵢ`**; **expanding a sum by writing out its terms**; **the index is a dummy variable (renaming it changes nothing)**; **the number of terms in `Σ_{i=m}^{n}` is `n − m + 1` (the fencepost count)**; **the empty sum is `0` (when `n < m`) and the empty product is `1`**; **pi notation `Π_{i=m}^{n} aᵢ`**; **writing a sum in Python: `sum(a(i) for i in range(m, n + 1))` — the half-open `range` needs `n + 1`**; **1-based mathematics vs 0-based Python made explicit (M15 Unit 3 link)**; **`math.prod`**.
- **L2:** **the rules of summation: linearity `Σ(aᵢ + bᵢ) = Σaᵢ + Σbᵢ`, `Σ c·aᵢ = c·Σaᵢ`, `Σ_{i=m}^{n} c = (n − m + 1)c`**; **splitting a sum `Σ_{i=m}^{n} = Σ_{i=m}^{k} + Σ_{i=k+1}^{n}`**; **index shifts `Σ_{i=m}^{n} aᵢ = Σ_{j=m+s}^{n+s} aⱼ₋ₛ`** and **reversing the order of summation**; **double sums and swapping the order — with dependent bounds the bounds must be rewritten (`Σ_{i=1}^{n} Σ_{j=1}^{i}` is not `Σ_{j=1}^{n} Σ_{i=1}^{j}`; the correct swap is `Σ_{j=1}^{n} Σ_{i=j}^{n}`)**; **what the rules do *not* say: `Σ aᵢbᵢ ≠ (Σaᵢ)(Σbᵢ)` and `(Σaᵢ)² ≠ Σaᵢ²` (exact counterexamples) — the correct expansion is `(Σaᵢ)² = Σaᵢ² + 2Σ_{i<j} aᵢaⱼ`**; **products and logarithms: `ln Π aᵢ = Σ ln aᵢ` for positive `aᵢ` (M06 Unit 3)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **an average loss `(1/N) Σ_{i=1}^{N} ℓᵢ`; a dot product `Σ wᵢxᵢ` (M09 Units 3–4); a mean and a variance written with Σ (M12 link; M03 Unit 4's identity); nested loops as double sums and their iteration counts (S3-01 link)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **linearity and the constant-sum rule (induction on the number of terms, using associativity and distributivity — M03 Unit 3)**; (b) **splitting, index shift, and order reversal**; (c) **swapping the order of a finite double sum with constant bounds (both sums count the same set of index pairs), and with dependent bounds (the set `{(i, j): 1 ≤ j ≤ i ≤ n}` described two ways)**; (d) **`(Σaᵢ)² = Σaᵢ² + 2Σ_{i<j} aᵢaⱼ`**; (e) **why the empty sum is `0` and the empty product is `1`: they are the unique values that keep the splitting rule valid when `k = m − 1` or `k = n`**; (f) **`ln Π aᵢ = Σ ln aᵢ` by induction from the product rule (M06 Unit 3)**; **exhaustive verification**: **every rule over all sequences of length ≤ 5 with entries in `[−2, 2]` (`5⁵ = 3,125` for the longest), exactly in `Fraction` arithmetic**; the dependent-bound swap over all `n ≤ 12` by explicit enumeration of the index pairs; fencepost counts `n − m + 1` over a stated grid of `(m, n)`; Python `sum`/`math.prod` vs explicit loops.
- **L4:** **fencepost and bound errors in translating Σ to code (a catalogue with exact instances)**; **summing very different magnitudes in floats (M10 Unit 2; `math.fsum`)**; **a summation-notation tool** `sumlab` (Tool card: a strict parser for `Σ`/`Π` expressions over integer bounds with a body in the module's small expression grammar of one index variable (the M03 grammar); exact evaluation with `Fraction`; the transformations **split, index shift, reverse, and dependent-bound swap**, each **verified by evaluation against the original**; a **readable Python snippet shown as text and never executed**; the empty-sum/empty-product conventions; caps on the number of terms and on nesting depth; stable errors); hostile inputs (upper bound below lower bound; huge ranges; nesting depth — caps); **testing**: exhaustive rule oracles and explicit-loop oracles; **mutation lab** (`range(m, n)` instead of `range(m, n + 1)`; empty product returned as `0`; a dependent-bound swap without rewriting the bounds; a shifted index with the summand not shifted) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 6 — Finite sums and series formulas
- **L1:** **the sum of the first `n` positive integers `Σ_{i=1}^{n} i = n(n + 1)/2` (Unit 2's arithmetic sum, now in Σ-notation)**; **the sum of the first `n` squares `Σ i² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6` and cubes `Σ i³ = (n(n + 1)/2)²`**; **the geometric sum `Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} rⁱ = (1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)` (Unit 3)**; **the sum of the first `n` odd numbers is `n²`**; **a series is a sum of the terms of a sequence; its partial sums form a new sequence `Sₙ`**; **checking a formula on small `n`**; Python: `sum(i for i in range(1, n + 1))` vs the formula.
- **L2:** **telescoping sums: `Σ_{i=1}^{n} 1/(i(i + 1)) = 1 − 1/(n + 1)` (partial fractions `1/(i(i + 1)) = 1/i − 1/(i + 1)`)**; **the method of differences: if `aᵢ = bᵢ₊₁ − bᵢ` then `Σ_{i=m}^{n} aᵢ = bₙ₊₁ − bₘ`**; **deriving `Σ i²` from `(i + 1)³ − i³ = 3i² + 3i + 1`**; **sums of a polynomial in `i` (linearity plus the formulas above)**; **counting formulas: pairs `Σ_{i=1}^{n−1} i = n(n − 1)/2 = C(n, 2)` (M11 forward link)**; **the number of subsets `Σ_{i=0}^{n} C(n, i) = 2ⁿ` (M05 Unit 8; M11 forward link — stated, with the binomial theorem `CITED [VERIFY]`)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **loop costs: `Σ i` gives the `O(n²)` of a double loop and `Σ i²` of a triple loop (S3-01 link; counted work)**; **a Riemann-style sum `(1/n³) Σ_{i=1}^{n} i² = (n + 1)(2n + 1)/(6n²)` written exactly — its limit is `1/3` (limits are Units 7–8 and Module 13; integrals are `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/06-Integral-Calculus/05-numerical-integration-riemann-sums-and-the-trapezoid-rule.md`)**.

## Unit 7 — Infinite sequences and the idea of convergence
- **L1:** **the idea: the terms of `(aₙ)` get and stay as close as we like to a number `L`**; **examples: `1/n → 0`, `n/(n + 1) → 1`, `(1/2)ⁿ → 0`, `(1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e` (M05 Unit 7)**; **non-examples: `(−1)ⁿ` (oscillates), `n²` (grows without bound)**; **the precise definition: `aₙ → L` if for every `ε > 0` there is an `N` such that `|aₙ − L| < ε` for every `n ≥ N`**; **reading the definition as a game (an adversary picks `ε`, we respond with `N`)**; **convergent vs divergent**; **a sequence can be graphed as points approaching a horizontal line, with an `ε`-band around it (M07 Unit 1 plot; M13 Unit 5 link)**; Python: terms of a sequence and the first index at which the distance to a claimed limit stays below `ε`.
- **L2:** **proving a limit from the definition: `1/n → 0` (`N = ⌈1/ε⌉ + 1` works)**; **`rⁿ → 0` for `0 < |r| < 1` (using Bernoulli, M05 Unit 6, to bound `rⁿ ≤ 1/(1 + n(1/|r| − 1))`, then choosing `N`)**; **limit laws for sequences: sums, constant multiples, products, and quotients (non-zero limit of the denominator)** — **the sum and constant-multiple laws are proved; the product and quotient laws are stated, used, and verified on exact examples (`CITED [VERIFY]` or proved if the author includes the proof)**; **the squeeze theorem**; **the limit is unique**; **bounded monotone sequences converge (`CITED [VERIFY]`, the completeness property; used for `e` in M05)**; **divergence: `(−1)ⁿ` (proved from the definition with `ε = 1`), `n²`**; **the harmonic numbers `Hₙ = Σ_{k=1}^{n} 1/k`: successive differences `1/n → 0` but `Hₙ` is unbounded (grouping terms: `H_{2ᵏ} ≥ 1 + k/2`)** — **so "successive terms differ by less than a tolerance" is not a convergence test**; **`Σ 1/k²` has bounded partial sums (`1/k² ≤ 1/(k − 1) − 1/k` telescopes to `< 2`), hence converges (by the monotone-bounded property, cited)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **a loss sequence and a stopping rule "stop when `|Lₙ₊₁ − Lₙ| < tol`": what it does and does not establish (a *heuristic*; the harmonic example shows it can stop on a divergent sequence)**; **learning-rate sequences `ηₙ = 1/n`: `Σηₙ` diverges (harmonic) while `Ση²ₙ` converges — the pair of conditions named in the stochastic-approximation literature `[VERIFY]` and linked forward to `../../Stage 4C — Advanced Mathematics for Machine Learning — Deep Learning/03-Optimization-Theory-and-Training-Algorithms/05-convergence-rates-of-gradient-descent.md`; no claim is made about training outcomes**; **the "stalling" of floating-point sums: adding a tiny term to a large partial sum can change nothing, so a divergent series can *look* convergent — demonstrated in `Decimal` with a small context precision (measured, not assumed)**.

## Unit 8 — Infinite geometric series
- **L1:** **an infinite series is the limit of its partial sums: `Σ_{k=0}^{∞} a·rᵏ = lim Sₙ` if the limit exists**; **the partial sums of a geometric series (Unit 3): `Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)`**; **it converges iff `|r| < 1`, and then the sum is `a/(1 − r)`**; **examples: `1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ⋯ = 2`, `1 − 1/2 + 1/4 − ⋯ = 2/3`**; **divergence for `|r| ≥ 1` (the terms do not tend to `0`)**; **the picture: a staircase of areas converging to a total**; **repeating decimals as geometric series: `0.(3) = (3/10)/(1 − 1/10) = 1/3` and `0.(9) = (9/10)/(1 − 1/10) = 1` — the second argument for `0.(9) = 1` that M01 Unit 4 promised (the first was algebraic)**; Python: exact `Fraction` partial sums and the closed form.
- **L2:** **the exact remainder: `S − Sₙ = a·rⁿ/(1 − r)`, so the error after `n` terms is known exactly — a rigorous stopping rule, unlike the "difference below a tolerance" heuristic of Unit 7**; **how many terms are needed for an error below `ε`: the smallest `n` with `|a·rⁿ/(1 − r)| < ε` — found by exact search**; **the divergence test: if the terms do not tend to `0`, the series diverges (the converse is false: the harmonic series, Unit 7)**; **repeating decimals with longer periods: `0.(d₁…d_p) = (d₁…d_p)/(10ᵖ − 1)` (derived by summing the series; M01 Unit 1 link)**; **bounded geometric partial sums and amortization: `1 + 2 + 4 + ⋯ + 2^{k−1} < 2·2^{k−1}` (the last term dominates; S3-01 link)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the exponential moving average's weights `(1 − β)βᵏ` sum to `1` in the limit (and to `1 − βᵗ` after `t` terms — Unit 4), so the bias-correction factor `1/(1 − βᵗ)` that some optimizers apply to a moving average is exactly the normalization of a finite geometric sum (formula only; `[VERIFY]` for any statement about a particular optimizer; no claim of benefit)**; **a discounted sum `Σ γᵗ = 1/(1 − γ)` and its "effective horizon" `1/(1 − γ)` (a discount factor `γ` named as used in sequential decision problems; formula only; forward reference)**; **slow convergence when `r` is close to `1` (the exact number of terms for a stated tolerance)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the sum of a convergent geometric series: `Sₙ → a/(1 − r)` for `|r| < 1` (Unit 3's partial-sum formula and Unit 7's `rⁿ → 0`)**; (b) **divergence for `|r| ≥ 1` (the terms do not tend to `0`; for `r = −1` the partial sums oscillate)**; (c) **the divergence test: if `Σaₖ` converges then `aₖ → 0` (`aₖ = Sₖ − Sₖ₋₁`)**; (d) **the exact remainder identity**; (e) **`0.(9) = 1` and the general repeating-decimal formula via the series**; (f) **the EMA weights sum: `Σ_{k=0}^{t−1}(1 − β)βᵏ = 1 − βᵗ → 1`**; (g) **the amortized bound `Σ_{i=0}^{k−1} 2ⁱ = 2ᵏ − 1 < 2·2^{k−1}`**; **exhaustive verification**: **the remainder identity exactly for `r ∈ {±1/2, ±1/3, ±2/3, ±9/10}`, integer `a ∈ [−3, 3]`, `n ≤ 30`**; **the repeating-decimal formula for every digit block of length 1 to 4 (`10 + 100 + 1,000 + 10,000 = 11,110` blocks, including blocks with leading zeros — state the domain) against the exact series sum computed from partial sums plus the exact remainder bound**; the EMA weight sums for stated `β`; the stopping-term search vs the closed-form bound.
- **L4:** **why a rigorous remainder beats a tolerance guess (the contrast with Unit 7's harmonic example)**; **series that converge slowly and the cost of exact arithmetic (numerator and denominator growth; caps)**; **geometric series in floats: loss of the remainder when `rⁿ` underflows; the exact bound is unaffected in `Fraction`**; **other series tests (ratio, comparison, integral) are *named and deferred* — only the geometric series, the harmonic series, and `Σ 1/k²` are treated here**; **an infinite-series tool** `geoseries` (Tool card: exact partial sums; exact remainder; the smallest `n` for a tolerance; convergence classification for `a·rᵏ`; repeating-decimal ↔ fraction conversion both ways; EMA weights and bias-correction factors; float-vs-exact comparison reports; caps; stable errors); hostile inputs (`|r| = 1`; `r` extremely close to `1`; huge `n` — caps); **testing**: the exact-remainder and repeating-decimal oracles; **mutation lab** (`|r| = 1` treated as convergent; the sum taken from `k = 1` instead of `k = 0`; the remainder with the wrong sign; the repeating-decimal denominator `10ᵖ` instead of `10ᵖ − 1`) with no surviving mutants.
