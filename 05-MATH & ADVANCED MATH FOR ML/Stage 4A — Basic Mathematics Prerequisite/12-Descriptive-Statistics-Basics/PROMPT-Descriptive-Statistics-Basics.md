## Unit 4 — Quartiles, percentiles, IQR, and outliers
- **L1:** **sorted data and order statistics `x₍₁₎ ≤ x₍₂₎ ≤ ⋯ ≤ x₍ₙ₎`**; **the median as the 50th percentile; the quartiles `Q₁`, `Q₂`, `Q₃` (the 25th, 50th, 75th percentiles)**; **percentiles as "the value below which about `p`% of the data falls"**; **the interquartile range `IQR = Q₃ − Q₁`**; **the outlier idea (a point far from the rest) and the conventional fences `Q₁ − 1.5·IQR` and `Q₃ + 1.5·IQR` (Tukey; a *convention*, `CITED [VERIFY]`, `HEURISTIC`)**; **an outlier is a prompt to investigate, not an instruction to delete**; **computing quartiles by hand for tiny datasets and with `statistics.quantiles`**; **percentile rank**.
- **L2:** **there is no single definition of a percentile: the same data give different answers under different definitions (Hyndman and Fan catalogue nine; `CITED [VERIFY]`)**; **three definitions made precise: (1) the inverse-ECDF definition `Q(p) = x₍⌈np⌉₎` (the smallest data value with at least a fraction `p` of the data at or below it); (2) the interpolated "exclusive" definition used by `statistics.quantiles(…, method="exclusive")` (the position `(n + 1)p`, interpolating between neighbors); (3) the interpolated "inclusive" definition (`method="inclusive"`, the position `(n − 1)p + 1`) — the two `statistics` methods are *documented*, `[VERIFY]`; NumPy's and pandas' defaults are *awareness only* and the learner tests whether they match**; **the empirical distribution function `Fₙ(x) = #{i : xᵢ ≤ x}/n` (a right-continuous step function, M07 Unit 5; nondecreasing, M07 Unit 13) and the quantile as its generalized inverse (M07 Unit 12: `Fₙ` is not one-to-one, so the inverse needs a definition)**; **ties and repeated values**; **the five-number summary**; **robust scaling `(x − median)/IQR` (an affine map, M07 Unit 15)**; **winsorizing and trimming (definitions; `HEURISTIC`)**; **equal-frequency binning by quantiles**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers (all synthetic)**: **percentile summaries (p50, p95, p99) of a synthetic latency-like list — a *description* with the definition stated; the sensitivity of a high percentile to a few extreme values; outlier flagging as a data-cleaning *candidate list***; **running and streaming quantiles are Stage 3 (S3-07 and S3-17 — link only; approximate sketches are `../../Stage 3 — Data Structures & Algorithms/17-Probabilistic-and-Advanced-Data-Structures`)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **`Fₙ` is nondecreasing, right-continuous, `0` below the minimum and `1` at and above the maximum, and its jumps are multiples of `1/n`**; (b) **the inverse-ECDF quantile is well defined, always one of the data values, and nondecreasing in `p`**; (c) **the inverse-ECDF quantile commutes with strictly increasing maps (`Q_{f(x)}(p) = f(Q_x(p))`), so it is meaningful for ordinal data (Unit 1) — interpolated definitions are not**; (d) **all three definitions agree on the median of an odd-size dataset but can disagree otherwise — an explicit small dataset on which the three quartiles differ is found by a stated search and reported**; (e) **the interpolated definitions are convex combinations of two neighboring order statistics (so they lie between them)**; (f) **the quartiles, the IQR, and the Tukey fences are affine-equivariant: for `a > 0`, `Q(ax + b) = aQ(x) + b`, `IQR(ax + b) = a·IQR(x)`, and the fences transform the same way (for `a < 0` the roles of `Q₁` and `Q₃` swap)**; (g) **the median's breakdown (Unit 2) generalizes: changing fewer than `⌈np⌉`-many extreme points cannot move the inverse-ECDF quantile outside the range of the remaining data (stated precisely and proved for the median)**; **exhaustive verification**: **the inverse-ECDF quantile vs a brute-force definition over all datasets of size `≤ 6` with integer values in `[0, 5]` (`Σₙ 6ⁿ = 55,986` datasets) for `p ∈ {k/8}`; the learner's exclusive and inclusive implementations vs `statistics.quantiles` (the documented external reference) over the same domain for `n = 4`, with every disagreement investigated and reported; monotonicity in `p`; affine equivariance over a stated grid (state each domain; the stdlib agreement is `finite-check evidence`)**.

## Unit 6 — Z-scores and standardization
