## Unit 1 — Average rate of change and secant lines
- **L1:** **the average rate of change of `f` over `[a, b]`: `(f(b) − f(a))/(b − a)` (M02 Unit 6; units are output units per input unit)**; **the secant line through `(a, f(a))` and `(b, f(b))` and its slope (M07 Unit 6)**; **a constant rate for a linear function; a varying rate for others**; **the difference quotient `(f(a + h) − f(a))/h` as the same quantity with `b = a + h`**; **examples: average speed, average loss decrease per epoch (the difference of consecutive terms of a sequence — M10 Unit 1)**; **a table of average rates over successive intervals**; Python: exact `Fraction` difference quotients.
- **L2:** **simplifying the difference quotient algebraically (so that `h` can be cancelled): for `f(x) = x²` it is `2x + h`; for `x³` it is `3x² + 3xh + h²`; for `1/x` it is `−1/(x(x + h))` (algebraic fractions, M03 Unit 8); for `√x` it is `1/(√(x + h) + √x)` (rationalizing, M05 Unit 4)**; **the central (symmetric) difference quotient `(f(x + h) − f(x − h))/(2h)` and when it is exact (for a quadratic it equals the exact slope of the secant of the quadratic *for every `h`*)**; **average rates over adjacent intervals combine as a *weighted* average with weights equal to the interval lengths (M02 Unit 10 — "mean of rates" vs "rate of totals")**; **monotonicity from the sign of secant slopes (M07 Unit 13)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the average change of a loss per epoch over a window; throughput over windows of unequal length (rate of totals, M02 Unit 6); the average rate of change of a learning-rate schedule**; **units and the "per what?" question**; **floating-point difference quotients: the cancellation `f(x + h) − f(x)` for small `h` (previewed here, developed in Unit 2)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the average rate of change of `f(x) = mx + b` over every interval is `m`**; (b) **the secant slope equals the slope of the line through the two points (M07 Unit 6) and is symmetric in `a` and `b`**; (c) **the difference quotient of `xⁿ` (natural `n`) is `Σ_{k=1}^{n} C(n, k)xⁿ⁻ᵏhᵏ⁻¹` (the binomial theorem, M11 Unit 4)**; (d) **the quotients for `1/x` and `√x` by the algebra above**; (e) **adjacent-interval rates combine as the length-weighted average: `(f(c) − f(a))/(c − a) = ((b − a)·r₁ + (c − b)·r₂)/(c − a)`**; (f) **for a quadratic `f(x) = px² + qx + r`, the secant slope over `[a, b]` is `p(a + b) + q`, which equals the slope `2px + q` of the quotient's limiting line at the *midpoint* `x = (a + b)/2` — an exact preview of the mean value theorem (named, `CITED [VERIFY]`; Stage 4B)**; (g) **the central quotient of a quadratic equals `2px + q` exactly**; **exhaustive verification**: **all the quotient simplifications as exact `Fraction` identities for integer `x ∈ [1, 8]` and `h ∈ {±1, ±2, ±3}` with `x + h` in the domain; the binomial form for `n ≤ 12`; (f) and (g) for all integer `p, q, r ∈ [−3, 3]` and `a < b ∈ [−4, 4]` (state each domain size)**.
- **L4:** **why the algebraic form matters: the simplified quotient is evaluable at `h = 0` (the original `0/0` is not) — the idea Unit 2 turns into a limit**; **floats: `f(x + h) − f(x)` loses digits when `h` is small (measured)**; **a rate tool** `rate` (Tool card: exact secant slopes and difference-quotient tables in `Fraction` for rational functions; algebraic simplification of the quotient for polynomials, `1/x`, and `√x` (within a small strict grammar, verified by exact evaluation on a grid); central quotients; length-weighted combination of adjacent rates; a float comparison clearly labelled `numerical evidence`; stable errors); hostile inputs (`h = 0`; `b = a`; points outside the domain; `nan` — refused); **testing**: exact-`Fraction` oracles over the stated domains; **mutation lab** (`b − a` vs `a − b` in the denominator; the central quotient divided by `h` instead of `2h`; rates averaged without weights; `1/x`'s sign dropped) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 3 — Limits of functions, numerically and graphically
- **L1:** **`lim_{x→a} f(x) = L` in words: as `x` gets close to `a` (but `x ≠ a`), `f(x)` gets close to `L`**; **the value `f(a)` is irrelevant (it may be undefined: `(x² − 1)/(x − 1)` at `x = 1`, whose limit is `2` while `f(1)` is undefined)**; **numerical tables from both sides with exact `Fraction`s**; **reading a limit from a graph (open and closed circles)**; **one-sided limits `x → a⁻` and `x → a⁺`**; **when a limit does not exist: a jump (`|x|/x` at `0`), unbounded growth (`1/x²`), or oscillation (`sin(1/x)`)**; **the formal definition (stated): for every `ε > 0` there is a `δ > 0` such that `0 < |x − a| < δ` implies `|f(x) − L| < ε`; the picture is a box around the point `(a, L)`; the module's discrete preview is the ε–N definition of M10 Unit 7**; Python tables and a first `ε`-box SVG.

## Unit 4 — Limit laws and continuity intuition
- **L1:** **the limit laws (stated): if `lim f = L` and `lim g = M` as `x → a`, then `lim(f + g) = L + M`, `lim(f − g) = L − M`, `lim(c·f) = c·L`, `lim(f·g) = L·M`, and `lim(f/g) = L/M` when `M ≠ 0`**; **direct substitution for polynomials (`lim_{x→a} p(x) = p(a)`) and for rational functions where the denominator is non-zero**; **the `0/0` form and algebraic rescue (Unit 3)**; **continuity at a point: `f(a)` is defined, `lim_{x→a} f(x)` exists, and they are equal — "you can draw it without lifting the pen"**; **types of discontinuity: removable (a hole), jump, infinite, oscillating**; **continuity of piecewise functions at their boundary points (M07 Unit 5)**; Python: exact limits of polynomials and rational functions by substitution and simplification.

## Unit 5 — Limits at infinity and asymptotes
- **L1:** **`lim_{x→∞} f(x) = L` in words: as `x` grows without bound, `f(x)` gets close to `L`; the same for `x → −∞`**; **the formal definition (stated): for every `ε > 0` there is an `X` such that `x > X` implies `|f(x) − L| < ε` (the function analogue of M10 Unit 7's ε–N)**; **a horizontal asymptote `y = L`**; **`1/x → 0`; `(3x² + 1)/(x² − 2) → 3`**; **vertical asymptotes: `lim_{x→a⁺} f(x) = +∞` (or `−∞`) means `f` exceeds every bound near `a`; `1/(x − a)` has a vertical asymptote at `x = a`**; **holes vs vertical asymptotes in rational functions (M03 Unit 8)**; **polynomial end behavior stated as limits `±∞` (M07 Unit 9)**; **sigmoid and tanh limits `0`, `1`, `−1` (M07 Unit 14)**; Python: exact values of rational functions at large `x` as `finite-check evidence`.
- **L2:** **rational functions: compare the degrees of numerator and denominator — numerator lower: the limit is `0`; equal degrees: the ratio of leading coefficients; numerator higher: `±∞`, and when it is exactly one higher, an *oblique* (slant) asymptote found by polynomial long division (M03 Unit 9)**; **a graph can cross a horizontal asymptote (`x/(x² + 1)` crosses `y = 0` at `x = 0` and tends to `0` at infinity)**; **growth comparison at infinity: exponentials beat polynomials, polynomials beat logarithms — stated as a ranking (`CITED [VERIFY]` in general; M05 Units 1 and 6 prove instances), with exact integer tables (`2ˣ` vs `x³`)**; **limits of sequences as limits at infinity restricted to integers: if `f(x) → L` then `f(n) → L` (and not conversely: `sin(πx)` at integers)**; **Big-O and limits: if `lim f(n)/g(n)` is finite then `f = O(g)`; if it is `0` then `f = o(g)` (S3-01 Unit 3 link; the definitions are Stage 3's, not re-taught)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **saturation of a sigmoid (the output approaches but never equals `1`, while in floats it can equal `1.0` exactly — measured; the exact bound `1 − σ(x) < e^{−x}`, M07 Unit 14)**; **a loss that levels off: "the plateau" is a *claim about the limit*, not about the plot**; **learning-rate sequences tending to zero (M10 Unit 7)**; **overflow/underflow at large `|x|` in floats: `e^{−x}` underflows to `0.0` at a machine-dependent point while its true value is positive (measured)**.

## Unit 6 — Accumulation and area under a curve: intuition
- **L1:** **accumulation: total change = rate × time when the rate is constant (M02 Unit 6; M01 Unit 8 units); when the rate varies, the total is the *area under the rate graph***; **approximating the area by rectangles: partition `[a, b]` into `n` equal pieces of width `Δx = (b − a)/n`; the left, right, and midpoint Riemann sums `Σ f(xᵢ*)Δx`**; **more rectangles, better approximation (a table with exact `Fraction`s)**; **signed area: area below the axis counts negative**; **the trapezoid rule as the average of the left and right sums**; **units: rate unit × time unit = amount unit**; Python: exact Riemann sums with `Fraction`.
