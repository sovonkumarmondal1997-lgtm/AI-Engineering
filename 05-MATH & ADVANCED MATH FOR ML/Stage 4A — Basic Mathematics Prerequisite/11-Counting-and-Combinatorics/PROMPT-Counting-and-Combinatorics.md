## Unit 1 — The counting principle
- **L1:** **the addition principle: if a task can be done in one of several *mutually exclusive* ways, the number of ways is the sum**; **the multiplication principle: if a task is done in stages and stage `i` can be done in `nᵢ` ways *whatever the earlier stages chose* (the same number of ways for every earlier choice), the total is `n₁·n₂⋯n_k`**; **tree diagrams**; **strings of length `k` over an alphabet of `n` symbols: `nᵏ` (M05 Unit 1)**; **subsets of an `n`-element set: `2ⁿ` (each element is in or out — M01 Unit 2, M05 Unit 8)**; **sample spaces and "number of outcomes" (named; probability is `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/09-Probability-Fundamentals/01-sample-spaces-events-and-probability-axioms.md`)**; Python: `itertools.product`, `len`, and `math.prod`.
- **L2:** **complement counting: the number of objects with a property is the total minus the number without it ("at least one" = all − none)**; **when the number of options at a later stage depends on the earlier choice, sum over the earlier choices (`Σ` — M10 Unit 5): e.g., choose `x ∈ {1, …, 4}` and then `y ∈ {1, …, x}` gives `1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10`, not `4·4`**; **ordered vs unordered, with vs without repetition (a four-way table, previewed here and developed in Units 2–3)**; **counting functions: `|B|^{|A|}` (M07 Unit 2 link)**; **overcounting and undercounting — the two classic failures (examples)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the size of a hyperparameter grid `∏ nᵢ`; the number of binary feature-flag configurations `2^d`; the number of possible label assignments `k^N` for `N` examples and `k` classes**; **reading a count as information: `log₂` of the number of configurations is the number of bits needed to index them (M06 Unit 2; `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/13-Information-Theory-for-ML/01-information-and-surprise.md` as the forward link; formula only)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the addition principle for finite disjoint sets as the definition of the size of a union**; (b) **the multiplication principle: `|A × B| = |A|·|B|` (the Cartesian product splits into `|A|` blocks `{a} × B`, each of size `|B|`, by the addition principle), extended to `k` factors by induction**; (c) **the generalized principle with dependent stage counts: the total is `Σ` over first-stage choices of the number of completions**; (d) **the bijection principle: two finite sets with a bijection between them have the same size**; (e) **`|B^A| = |B|^{|A|}` (a function is a choice of an image for each element of `A`) and `|P(A)| = 2^{|A|}` (bijection with binary strings, or with functions `A → {0, 1}`)**; (f) **complement counting**; **exhaustive verification**: **for every instance in a stated family — alphabets of size `n ≤ 5` and lengths `k ≤ 6`; all subsets of sets of size `≤ 12`; the dependent two-stage example for ranges up to `n ≤ 12`; all function counts for `|A|, |B| ≤ 4` — the formula equals `len` of the `itertools` enumeration (state each domain size; `exact computation` for each instance, `finite-check evidence` for the general formula; the proofs are the proofs)**.
- **L4:** **when the multiplication principle does not apply (dependent stage counts) and the signs of a misuse (a count that is too large)**; **counting vs enumerating: compute `|X|` without constructing `X`; enumeration under a hard cap only**; **combinatorial blow-up shown in exact numbers (`2⁵⁰`, `10¹⁰`, `30!` as exact integers)**; **a counting-principles tool** `count` (Tool card: finite-set objects defined by a product/sum/function/subset description in a small strict grammar; the **size computed from the description** with the principle named; an **enumeration cross-check under a cap** (default `10⁶` elements; refusal above); status labelling (`exact computation` for the instance); stable errors; SVG tree diagrams by the stdlib writer); hostile inputs (cap exceeded; negative sizes; empty alphabets — `0ᵏ` and `n⁰` conventions stated); **testing**: enumeration oracles; **mutation lab** (product used when stage counts depend on earlier choices; sum used for non-disjoint cases; `nᵏ` with `n` and `k` swapped; a complement count missing the "none" case) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 2 — Factorials and permutations
- **L1:** **the factorial `n! = n·(n − 1)⋯2·1`, with `0! = 1` (justified two ways: the empty product is `1`, M10 Unit 5; and `n! = n·(n − 1)!` must hold at `n = 1`)**; **permutations of `n` distinct objects: `n!` (an ordering is a sequence of choices from `n`, `n − 1`, … remaining)**; **`k`-permutations: ordered selections of `k` from `n` distinct objects, `P(n, k) = n!/(n − k)! = n(n − 1)⋯(n − k + 1)` (the falling factorial)**; **how fast `n!` grows (a table of exact values)**; Python `math.factorial`, `math.perm`, `itertools.permutations`; **printing a large factorial and the integer-to-string conversion limit in recent CPython versions (`[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`; test it)**.
- **L2:** **permutations with repeated objects: `n!/(n₁!n₂!⋯n_k!)` (divide out the orderings of identical items)**; **circular arrangements: `(n − 1)!` when rotations are considered the same (a stated convention; reflections are *not* identified here)**; **arrangements with a stated item fixed in place**; **derangements named (permutations with no fixed point; the exact count is Unit 5)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the number of orderings of a dataset of `N` examples is `N!`; a shuffle driven by a generator with a `b`-bit state can reach at most `2ᵇ` distinct orderings, so for `N! > 2ᵇ` *not all orderings are reachable* — a pigeonhole argument (proved in L3; the actual state size of any real generator is *not* asserted — `[VERIFY]` against that generator's documentation)**; **the number of ways to order `d` features, `d` layers' hyperparameters, or `k` experiment runs**; **`log n! = Σ log k` (M10 Unit 5; M06 Unit 3) and why logarithms tame factorials (`math.lgamma(n + 1) = ln n!` is documented — `numerical evidence`)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **`n!` counts permutations (induction on `n`, or by the multiplication principle)**; (b) **`P(n, k) = n!/(n − k)!` and the recurrence `P(n, k) = n·P(n − 1, k − 1)`**; (c) **the multiset-permutation formula (each arrangement of the labelled objects collapses to the same arrangement of unlabelled ones in exactly `n₁!⋯n_k!` ways)**; (d) **circular arrangements: each circular arrangement corresponds to exactly `n` linear ones (its rotations), so the count is `n!/n = (n − 1)!`**; (e) **bounds: `2ⁿ⁻¹ ≤ n! ≤ nⁿ` for `n ≥ 1`, and `n! > (n/e)ⁿ` (from the series `eⁿ = Σ nᵏ/k! > nⁿ/n!`, M05 Unit 7)**; (f) **the pigeonhole bound: a generator with `b` bits of state produces at most `2ᵇ` distinct output sequences, so when `n! > 2ᵇ` some permutation is unreachable**; (g) **`2ⁿ < n!` for `n ≥ 4` (induction)**; **exhaustive verification**: **`itertools.permutations` counts vs `n!` for `n ≤ 8`; `P(n, k)` vs enumeration for `n ≤ 8`, all `k`; multiset permutations vs the size of `set(itertools.permutations(...))` for all multisets of size `≤ 8` over `≤ 3` symbols; circular counts vs enumeration after canonical rotation for `n ≤ 8`; the bounds in (e) and (g) exactly for `n ≤ 100` (state each domain)**.
- **L4:** **numerical hazards: factorials in floats overflow (`OverflowError` or `inf`) while `int` is exact; `n!` with `n` in the thousands is exact but slow to print; fixed-width integers (NumPy) overflow silently — shown as a hazard with an exact `int` comparison, never as the oracle; **Stirling's approximation is `CITED [VERIFY]` and is used only for estimates, with the exact value computed beside it**; **a permutation tool** `perm` (Tool card: exact `n!`, `P(n, k)`, multiset permutations, circular counts, derangements via the Unit 5 formula; **enumeration cross-check under a cap**; log-factorial by exact summation of `log` terms as `numerical evidence` beside `math.lgamma`; the pigeonhole check for a stated bit width; stable errors; caps on `n`); hostile inputs (`k > n`; negative arguments; huge `n` — caps; a float with an integral value — refused with a message); **testing**: enumeration oracles; **mutation lab** (`0!` taken as `0`; `P(n, k)` with `k > n` returning a nonsense value instead of `0`; multiset division omitted; circular count `n!`) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 3 — Combinations and binomial coefficients
- **L1:** **a combination is an *unordered* selection of `k` objects from `n` distinct objects; the count is the binomial coefficient `C(n, k) = n!/(k!(n − k)!)`**; **why: `P(n, k)` counts ordered selections and each unordered selection appears `k!` times, so `C(n, k) = P(n, k)/k!`**; **`C(n, 0) = C(n, n) = 1`; `C(n, k) = C(n, n − k)` (choosing the objects to take is the same as choosing the objects to leave)**; **`C(n, k) = 0` when `k > n` (a convention made explicit)**; **the sum over all `k` is `2ⁿ` (subsets sorted by size)**; Python `math.comb`, `itertools.combinations`.
- **L2:** **multisets: the number of ways to choose `k` objects from `n` types with repetition allowed is `C(n + k − 1, k)` (stars and bars)**; **non-negative integer solutions of `x₁ + ⋯ + x_m = n`: `C(n + m − 1, m − 1)`**; **lattice paths: `C(a + b, a)` shortest paths on an `a × b` grid**; **multinomial coefficients: the number of ways to split `n` distinct objects into labelled groups of sizes `k₁, …, k_m` is `n!/(k₁!⋯k_m!)`**; **the multiplicative formula `C(n, k) = ∏_{i=1}^{k} (n − k + i)/i` (every partial product is itself a binomial coefficient, hence an integer)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the number of ways to choose a test set of `m` examples from `N`: `C(N, m)`; the number of feature subsets of size `k` from `d` features: `C(d, k)`; the number of ways to assign `N` examples to `k` equal folds — ordered folds `N!/((N/k)!)ᵏ` and, if the folds are unlabelled, that count divided by `k!` (`k ∣ N`)**; **the difference between "a sequence of folds" and "a partition into folds" — a classic double-counting error**; **naive recursion for `C(n, k)` is exponential; Pascal's recurrence (Unit 4) with memoization or the multiplicative formula is not (S3-09 and S3-16 link; counted work, S3-01)**; **the exactness of `int` vs the overflow of floats and of fixed-width integers**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **`C(n, k) = P(n, k)/k!` (each `k`-subset corresponds to exactly `k!` ordered selections)**; (b) **symmetry via the complement bijection**; (c) **`Σ_{k=0}^{n} C(n, k) = 2ⁿ` (partition the subsets by size)**; (d) **stars and bars: a multiset of size `k` over `n` types corresponds to a string of `k` stars and `n − 1` bars, i.e., a choice of `k` of the `n + k − 1` positions for the stars**; (e) **lattice paths: a path is a sequence of `a` rights and `b` ups, i.e., a choice of `a` of `a + b` steps**; (f) **the multinomial coefficient by successive binomial choices (`C(n, k₁)·C(n − k₁, k₂)⋯`) and by dividing out orderings**; (g) **the multiplicative formula and the integrality of every partial product (the partial product after `i` steps is `C(n − k + i, i)`)**; (h) **Vandermonde's identity `C(m + n, k) = Σᵢ C(m, i)C(n, k − i)` by splitting a `k`-subset of a union of two disjoint sets by how many elements come from each (optional L4)**; **exhaustive verification**: **`C(n, k)` vs `len(list(itertools.combinations(...)))` for all `n ≤ 12` and all `k`; multisets vs `combinations_with_replacement` for `n, k ≤ 7`; non-negative solutions of `x₁ + ⋯ + x_m = n` vs brute-force enumeration for `m ≤ 4`, `n ≤ 12`; lattice paths vs a dynamic-programming count for `a, b ≤ 10`; multinomial vs enumeration of group assignments for `n ≤ 8` with `≤ 3` groups; partial-product integrality for `n ≤ 60`, all `k`; Vandermonde for `m, n ≤ 12` (state each domain)**.
- **L4:** **off-by-one hazards in stars and bars (`n + k − 1` vs `n + k`; `m − 1` vs `m`) with exact instances**; **large binomials: exact values with `math.comb`; ratios of huge binomials as `Fraction`s; `math.lgamma`-based logs as `numerical evidence`**; **`itertools.combinations` on large inputs (never run without a cap)**; **a combinations tool** `comb` (Tool card: exact `C(n, k)`, multisets, non-negative solutions, lattice paths, multinomials, fold-assignment counts (ordered and unordered); the multiplicative algorithm with a **per-step integrality assertion**; **enumeration cross-check under a cap**; a float-overflow demonstration beside the exact value; stable errors; caps); hostile inputs (`k > n`; `n < 0`; huge arguments — caps; zero types in stars and bars — convention stated); **testing**: enumeration and DP oracles; **mutation lab** (`C(n, k)` divided by `(n − k)!` only; stars and bars with `n + k`; ordered folds reported as unordered; the `k > n` case returning `1`) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 4 — Pascal's triangle and the binomial theorem
- **L1:** **Pascal's triangle: row `n` lists `C(n, 0), …, C(n, n)`; each entry is the sum of the two above it (Pascal's rule `C(n, k) = C(n − 1, k − 1) + C(n − 1, k)`)**; **building the triangle by hand and in Python with exact integers**; **row sums `2ⁿ`**; **symmetry**; **the binomial theorem: `(x + y)ⁿ = Σ_{k=0}^{n} C(n, k) xⁿ⁻ᵏ yᵏ`**; **the cases `n = 2, 3` already seen as the identities `(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²` and `(a + b)³ = a³ + 3a²b + 3ab² + b³` (M03 Unit 4)**; **expanding `(x + 1)ⁿ` and `(2x − 3)ⁿ` for small `n`**; Python `math.comb` and a polynomial-coefficient list.
- **L2:** **why the theorem holds (choose, in each of the `n` brackets, either `x` or `y`; the coefficient of `xⁿ⁻ᵏyᵏ` counts the ways to choose `y` from `k` brackets)**; **special values: `x = y = 1` gives `2ⁿ`; `x = 1, y = −1` gives `0` for `n ≥ 1` (the alternating sum: subsets of even and odd size are equally many)**; **`3ⁿ = Σ C(n, k)2ᵏ`**; **the hockey-stick identity `Σ_{i=r}^{n} C(i, r) = C(n + 1, r + 1)`**; **`k·C(n, k) = n·C(n − 1, k − 1)`**; **the binomial theorem used elsewhere in the course: Bernoulli's inequality for `x ≥ 0` (M05 Unit 1), the expansion `(1 + 1/m)ᵐ = Σ C(m, k)/mᵏ` behind the definition of `e` (M05 Unit 7 — this lesson supplies the proof those lessons cited), and the finite sums of M10 Unit 6**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers (formulas only)**: **the binomial probabilities `C(n, k)pᵏ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ` sum to `1` by the binomial theorem with `x = p`, `y = 1 − p` (the distribution itself is `../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/10-Common-Distributions/01-bernoulli-and-binomial.md`); the number of terms and the coefficients of a polynomial-feature expansion (M03 Unit 9)**; **the multinomial theorem named (`../../Stage 4B — Mathematics for Machine Learning/10-Common-Distributions/02-categorical-multinomial-and-softmax.md`)**; **parity of entries and Pascal's triangle modulo 2 (named; Unit 4 L4)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **Pascal's rule, combinatorially (a `k`-subset of `{1, …, n}` either contains `n` or does not) and algebraically (from the factorial formula)**; (b) **the binomial theorem by induction on `n` using Pascal's rule**; (c) **the binomial theorem by the choice-in-each-bracket argument**; (d) **`Σ C(n, k) = 2ⁿ` and `Σ (−1)ᵏ C(n, k) = 0` for `n ≥ 1`**; (e) **the hockey-stick identity (induction on `n`, or by counting `(r + 1)`-subsets by their largest element)**; (f) **`k·C(n, k) = n·C(n − 1, k − 1)` (choose a committee and then a leader, two ways)**; (g) **`Σ_{k} C(n, k)pᵏ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ = 1`**; (h) **the multinomial theorem's coefficients are the multinomial coefficients (Unit 3)**; **exhaustive verification**: **Pascal's rule vs `math.comb` for all `n ≤ 200`, all `k`; the binomial theorem vs a direct polynomial expansion (exact, M03-style) for `n ≤ 12` and several coefficient pairs; the alternating-sum and hockey-stick identities for `n ≤ 100`; `k·C(n, k) = n·C(n − 1, k − 1)` for `n ≤ 100`; the probability-sum identity with `Fraction` values of `p` on a stated grid for `n ≤ 20` (state each domain)**.
- **L4:** **the parity of `C(n, k)`: `C(n, k)` is odd iff the binary digits of `k` are a subset of those of `n` (equivalently `k & (n − k) == 0`) — Lucas's/Kummer's theorem, `CITED [VERIFY]`, stated, and verified exhaustively for `n ≤ 256`, all `k`, as `finite-check evidence` (S3-10 bit-manipulation link)**; **Pascal's triangle modulo 2 as a self-similar pattern (named; no proof)**; **efficient generation of a row by the multiplicative update `C(n, k + 1) = C(n, k)·(n − k)/(k + 1)` with exact integers (division is exact at every step — Unit 3)**; **a Pascal-and-binomial tool** `pascal` (Tool card: exact rows and the triangle up to a cap; binomial expansion of `(a·x + b·y)ⁿ` with exact coefficients; the identity-verification engine (each identity labelled with its proof status and checked over a stated range as `finite-check evidence`); exact binomial-probability sums with `Fraction`; the parity test with its cited status; **a text/pattern rendering of the triangle that does not rely on color**; stable errors; caps on `n`); hostile inputs (`n < 0`; huge `n` — caps; non-integer coefficients — exact `Fraction` accepted, floats flagged); **testing**: `math.comb` and polynomial-expansion oracles; **mutation lab** (Pascal's rule with `C(n − 1, k)` repeated; the binomial theorem with `xᵏyⁿ⁻ᵏ` mis-paired; the alternating sum without the sign; the hockey-stick bounds off by one) with no surviving mutants.

## Unit 5 — Inclusion–exclusion
- **L1:** **two sets: `|A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|` (M01 Unit 2 link)**; **three sets: `|A ∪ B ∪ C| = |A| + |B| + |C| − |A ∩ B| − |A ∩ C| − |B ∩ C| + |A ∩ B ∩ C|`**; **why simply adding the sizes overcounts, and why the correction alternates**; **Venn diagrams with region counts**; **the complement form: the number of elements in none of the sets is `|U| − |A ∪ B ∪ C|`**; **counting integers up to `N` divisible by `a`, `b`, or `c` (`⌊N/a⌋`, with `⌊N/lcm(a, b)⌋` for the overlap — M01 Unit 6)**; Python `set` union and `len`.
- **L2:** **the general formula `|A₁ ∪ ⋯ ∪ Aₙ| = Σ_{∅ ≠ S ⊆ {1,…,n}} (−1)^{|S|+1} |⋂_{i∈S} Aᵢ|`**; **derangements: the number of permutations of `n` objects with no fixed point is `!n = Σ_{k=0}^{n} (−1)ᵏ n!/k!` (the complement of the union of the "item `i` is fixed" sets)**; **the first values and the ratio `!n/n!` approaching `Σ (−1)ᵏ/k!`, the series of `e⁻¹` (M05 Unit 7, M10 Unit 8) — with the exact error bound `|!n/n! − Σ_{k≤n}(−1)ᵏ/k!| = 0` and `|!n/n! − e⁻¹| < 1/(n + 1)!` derived in L3**; **Euler's totient: the number of integers in `[1, n]` coprime to `n` via inclusion–exclusion over the prime divisors (M01 Unit 6) — `φ(n) = n·∏(1 − 1/p)`**; **counting with an upper bound on each part: the number of solutions of `x₁ + ⋯ + x_m = n` with `0 ≤ xᵢ ≤ c` is `Σ_j (−1)ʲ C(m, j) C(n − j(c + 1) + m − 1, m − 1)` (inclusion–exclusion over the parts that exceed the bound, then stars and bars)**; **ML-flavored use with stated numbers**: **the size of the union of the example sets flagged by several data-quality filters, given the sizes of the filters and of their overlaps; the number of distinct examples after merging overlapping data sources (M01 Unit 2's leakage link); the size of a union of feature or label vocabularies**; **the exponential cost of the general formula: `n` sets give `2ⁿ − 1` terms (so the formula is for small `n`, while a direct `set` union is the practical route when the sets are available)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the two-set formula (Module 01 recalled) and the three-set formula**; (b) **the general formula by element counting: an element lying in exactly `m ≥ 1` of the sets is counted `Σ_{j=1}^{m} (−1)ʲ⁺¹ C(m, j) = 1` times (by the alternating binomial sum of Unit 4, `Σ_{j=0}^{m}(−1)ʲ C(m, j) = 0`), and an element in none is counted `0` times**; (c) **the complement form**; (d) **the derangement formula (apply (c) to the sets "`i` is a fixed point", whose `j`-fold intersections have size `(n − j)!`)**; (e) **the bound `|!n/n! − e⁻¹| < 1/(n + 1)!` (the tail of an alternating series with decreasing terms is smaller than its first omitted term — stated and used; the series' convergence to `e⁻¹` is `CITED [VERIFY]` from M05 Unit 7)**; (f) **the totient formula (optional L4)**; (g) **Bonferroni's inequalities: truncating the formula after an odd number of terms over-estimates the union and after an even number under-estimates it (optional L4; the same element-counting argument with partial alternating sums of binomials)**; **exhaustive verification**: **the general formula vs a direct union over every family of 4 subsets of a 4-element universe (`16⁴ = 65,536` ordered families) and every family of 3 subsets of a 5-element universe (`32³ = 32,768`)**; **derangement counts vs enumeration (`itertools.permutations` without fixed points) for `n ≤ 8`**; the exact bound in (e) with `Fraction` partial sums for `n ≤ 12`; the totient formula vs a direct `gcd` count (M01 Unit 6) for `n ≤ 500`; the bounded-parts formula vs enumeration for `m ≤ 4`, `n ≤ 12`, `c ≤ 4` (state each domain).

## Unit 6 — Counting with restrictions and combinatorial explosion
