## Unit 1 — Linear regression: design matrix and residuals

- **L1:** **the model: predict a number as a weighted sum of features plus an intercept, `ŷᵢ = b + w·xᵢ`; stacking all samples gives `ŷ = Xβ` with the design matrix `X` of shape `(n, p)`, rows = samples, a leading column of ones for the intercept**; **worked by hand on a tiny integer dataset (e.g., five points, one feature)**: build `X`, write `ŷ = Xβ`, compute residuals `r = y − Xβ` and the residual sum of squares `RSS = ‖r‖²` for two candidate `β`; **the loss `J(β) = RSS/(2n)` (the module's pinned convention) and why squaring (differentiable, penalises large errors, the Gaussian-noise reading previewed)**; **the picture: data points, the fitted line, vertical residuals; the geometric picture: `y` as a vector in `ℝⁿ`, the columns of `X` spanning a subspace, `ŷ` the point of that subspace closest to `y` (S4B-01/02 projection — previewed here, derived in Unit 2)**; **`R² = 1 − RSS/TSS` with `TSS = Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)²` (definition, the baseline of predicting the mean, the range, and what it does not mean)**; **interpretation of a coefficient (the change in `ŷ` per unit change in a feature *holding the others fixed*; units; why a coefficient is not "importance" without scaling)**; ≥ 3 worked examples with exact rational checks.
- **L2:** **building design matrices correctly**: multiple features; the intercept column and the alternative of centring; **categorical features by one-hot encoding with a reference level, and the dummy-variable trap (a full one-hot set plus an intercept is exactly collinear: the columns sum to the intercept column — shown by a rank computation, S4B-02)**; polynomial and interaction features as *linear-in-parameters* models (the model is linear in `β`, not in `x`; a worked quadratic fit; a plot of the same `X` machinery); feature scaling and the **scaling rule** (standardise on the training split only; the coefficient conversion between standardised and original units, derived); **residual diagnostics as engineering tools**: residuals vs fitted (a pattern signals missing structure — a seeded synthetic curve fitted by a line), the residual histogram and a normal-quantile comparison (a diagnostic, never proof), heteroscedasticity as a fan shape, outliers and their pull on a least-squares fit (a seeded example: one outlier moves the slope by an exactly computed amount; MAE/Huber named, S4B-08), **leverage**; **train/validation/test discipline for a regression (S4B-09 Unit 4): the training `R²` always rises when a feature is added, even a random one — a seeded demonstration with the exact monotone property proved in L3; held-out error is the honest measure**; **collinearity as a symptom: two near-duplicate features make individual coefficients unstable while predictions barely change (a seeded perturbation experiment with measured coefficient spread vs `κ(X)` from S4B-03)**; **the model's units and baselines: predicting the training mean is the `R² = 0` baseline**; **extrapolation warning (a linear fit beyond the data range; no claim about any real system)**.
- **L3:** **proofs and derivations, written in full:** (a) **the gradient of the loss: `∇J(β) = −(1/n)Xᵀ(y − Xβ)` — derived from `J = (1/2n)(y − Xβ)ᵀ(y − Xβ)` by S4B-07 matrix calculus, cross-checked by finite differences**; (b) **the Hessian `∇²J = XᵀX/n` is constant and PSD (`vᵀXᵀXv = ‖Xv‖² ≥ 0`), so `J` is convex; it is positive definite iff `X` has full column rank (`Xv = 0 ⇒ v = 0`) — hence a unique minimiser exactly in the full-rank case (the exact link between rank, S4B-02, and uniqueness)**; (c) **with an intercept, the residuals sum to zero and `ȳ = b + w·x̄` (the fitted hyperplane passes through the centroid) — from the intercept's normal equation (stated here; proved in Unit 2)**; (d) **adding a column to `X` never increases the minimal `RSS` (the model class grows), so training `R²` is non-decreasing in the number of features — full**; (e) **simple regression closed forms by hand: `ŵ = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)/Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²` and `b̂ = ȳ − ŵx̄`, and `R² = ρ²` (the squared correlation, S4B-09) — derived, with an exact rational check**; (f) **centring and scaling: how the coefficients and intercept transform under `x → (x − μ)/s` — derived exactly**; (g) **the effect of an affine reparametrisation of the features on the fitted *predictions* (invariant when the new design spans the same column space — proved by the projection characterisation previewed here) versus on the *coefficients* (not invariant) and on *penalised* fits (not invariant — the reason for the scaling rule, Unit 5)**; (h) **the rank of `X` vs the dummy trap: `rank` of a full one-hot-plus-intercept design is `p − 1` — shown by the explicit null vector**; (i) **counterexamples**: **training `R²` rising from a pure-noise feature (an exact tiny instance); a perfect fit (`R² = 1`) with `n = p` and meaningless predictions (the exactly determined system); a coefficient changing sign when a correlated feature is added (an exact two-feature instance — Simpson-style, S4B-09 Unit 3)**; **numerical analysis**: `TSS`/`RSS` accumulation with `math.fsum`; the `R²` loss of meaning when the baseline is wrong (no intercept: the uncentred `R²` convention — named and tested); condition number of `X` (S4B-03) as the collinearity detector; float32 vs float64 differences in `RSS`; **tool card** for `linmodels.design` (design-matrix builder with intercept, one-hot with a reference level, polynomial/interaction terms, standardiser fitted on the training split, residual/`R²`/leverage diagnostics, rank and condition checks; verified against exact rational computation and `numpy` cross-checks on stated domains).
- **L4:** **design failures, each reproduced and diagnosed with a test**: **the dummy trap (a rank-deficient design and the solver's different silent behaviours); a constant or duplicated column; the scaler fitted on all data (leakage — the exact optimism measured on a seeded split); an intercept forgotten (a biased fit forced through the origin — an exact example); a categorical feature encoded as an integer code (an imposed ordering — a seeded example); a target-derived feature (leakage, S4B-09); a prediction-time feature unavailable at training (named); an outlier-driven slope; extrapolation; heteroscedastic residuals misread as noise; a train/test split by row when rows are clustered (S4B-12 cluster discipline — a seeded demonstration of the optimism); coefficients read as importance or as causal effects (`JUDGEMENT`; no causal claim; confounding by the S4B-09 example)**; hostile inputs (`n < p`, `n = 0`, a constant target, NaN/Inf, a string column, mismatched lengths, an all-zero column); **exhaustive verification** of the gradient, the PSD Hessian, the residual-sum and centroid properties, the `R²` monotonicity, and the simple-regression closed forms over all small integer designs (domain named and sized; exact rational arithmetic; `EVIDENCE ONLY`; the theorems are proved above); mutation lab (the intercept column dropped; `1/n` omitted; residual sign flipped; `R²` baseline wrong; one-hot reference level not dropped; scaler fitted on the full data; `TSS` uncentred; leverage diagonal wrong) with no surviving mutants; case study: **a model whose validation error improved after "adding features" only because the scaler and a target-encoded column had been fitted on all rows** (original synthetic reproduction with the leakage-free pipeline as the fix; no claim about any real system).

## Unit 2 — The normal equation and closed-form solution

- **L1:** **the minimum of the loss is where the gradient is zero; setting `∇J = 0` gives the *normal equations* `XᵀXβ = Xᵀy`, and when `XᵀX` is invertible, `β̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy`** — derived in two lines from Unit 1; **why "normal": the residual is *normal* (orthogonal) to every column of `X` (`Xᵀ(y − Xβ̂) = 0`)**; **the geometric picture: `ŷ = Xβ̂` is the orthogonal projection of `y` onto the column space (S4B-02), and the residual is the perpendicular**; **worked by hand on a 3×2 integer system with exact rational arithmetic (`Fraction`): form `XᵀX` and `Xᵀy`, solve the 2×2 system, check orthogonality exactly**; **the hat matrix `H = X(XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀ` (symmetric, idempotent, `ŷ = Hy`) and what its diagonal (leverage) means**; **when it fails: `XᵀX` singular (collinear columns, `p > n`) — infinitely many solutions; the picture of a flat valley instead of a bowl**; ≥ 3 worked examples with exact rational checks.
- **L2:** **solving least squares correctly in code (the module's central numerical lesson)**: **why not `inv(XᵀX)` — the condition number of `XᵀX` is the *square* of that of `X` (proved in L3), so a design with `κ(X) = 10⁸` has `κ(XᵀX) = 10¹⁶`, beyond float64 resolution; a measured example on a seeded near-collinear design where the normal-equation solution loses all digits while QR and SVD retain most**; **three solvers compared on the same designs: (i) the normal equation via a Cholesky/`solve` call on `XᵀX`, (ii) QR factorisation `X = QR` then `Rβ = Qᵀy` (back-substitution), (iii) the SVD / pseudo-inverse `β̂ = X⁺y = VΣ⁺Uᵀy` (S4B-03) with a rank cutoff; a table of cost, accuracy, and failure behaviour; a decision rule (`JUDGEMENT`)**; **`np.linalg.lstsq` and its rank-cutoff convention (`rcond`, `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`/`[VERIFY]` — and tested: the learner must observe what it returns on a rank-deficient design)**; **rank-deficient and wide designs: the set of minimisers is an affine subspace; the pseudo-inverse picks the *minimum-norm* solution (proved in L3); a seeded demonstration with a planted sparse `β` and `p > n`**; **the probabilistic reading (closing the S4B-08/S4B-10 correspondence)**: with `y = Xβ + ε`, `ε ∼ N(0, σ²I)`, the log-likelihood is `−(n/2)ln(2πσ²) − RSS/(2σ²)`, so *least squares is maximum likelihood*, and the MLE of `σ²` is `RSS/n` (the unbiased version divides by `n − p`; derived in L3)**; **the sampling distribution of `β̂` (closing the S4B-11/12 link)**: `β̂ = β + (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀε` is unbiased with covariance `σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹`; **standard errors `SE(β̂ⱼ) = σ̂√[(XᵀX)⁻¹]ⱼⱼ`, a confidence interval by the S4B-12 recipe (normal/t quantile, `[VERIFY]` the degrees of freedom `n − p`), and the **bootstrap interval for a coefficient (pairs bootstrap vs residual bootstrap — S4B-12 Unit 2 applied; both seeded; compared with the formula under the planted model)**; **the variance inflation from collinearity: `Var(β̂ⱼ) = σ²/[(1 − Rⱼ²)·Σ(xᵢⱼ − x̄ⱼ)²]` with `Rⱼ²` the `R²` of regressing column `j` on the others (derived in L3) — the *variance inflation factor* `VIFⱼ = 1/(1 − Rⱼ²)`; a measured table**; **leverage and influence (named: Cook's distance as a definition, no claim)**; **adding and removing rows: the rank-one update idea named (`CITED [VERIFY]`)**; **weighted least squares: `min Σ wᵢ rᵢ²` gives `(XᵀWX)β = XᵀWy` (derived; the connection to heteroscedastic noise and to importance weights, S4B-11)**; **cost arithmetic: forming `XᵀX` costs `O(np²)`, solving `O(p³)`, QR `O(np²)`, SVD `O(np·min(n, p))` — stated as asymptotic counts, `[ASSUMPTION]` on constants, never as measured speed claims**.
- **L3:** **proofs and derivations, written in full:** (a) **the normal equations from `∇J = 0` and the sufficiency of a stationary point for a convex `J` (Unit 1 (b)); uniqueness iff `X` has full column rank — full**; (b) **the projection characterisation: `ŷ` is the unique point of `col(X)` minimising `‖y − z‖`; proof by the Pythagorean identity `‖y − z‖² = ‖y − ŷ‖² + ‖ŷ − z‖²` when `y − ŷ ⊥ col(X)` — full; hence `H` is the orthogonal projector (symmetric, idempotent, `trace(H) = rank(X) = p`)**; (c) **the residual properties: `Xᵀr = 0`; with an intercept `Σrᵢ = 0`; the decomposition `TSS = ESS + RSS` (with an intercept) — full**; (d) **the SVD solution: with `X = UΣVᵀ` (thin SVD, full column rank), `β̂ = VΣ⁻¹Uᵀy`; in the rank-deficient case `β̂ = X⁺y = VΣ⁺Uᵀy` is the minimum-norm minimiser: any minimiser is `β̂ + z` with `z ∈ null(X)` and `β̂ ∈ row(X) ⊥ null(X)` so `‖β̂ + z‖² = ‖β̂‖² + ‖z‖²` — full**; (e) **the condition number: `XᵀX = VΣ²Vᵀ`, so `κ(XᵀX) = σ_max²/σ_min² = κ(X)²` — full; the consequence for the forward error of the normal-equation solution (the standard perturbation bound, `CITED [VERIFY]` with a measured demonstration)**; (f) **QR: `X = QR` with orthonormal `Q` gives `‖y − Xβ‖² = ‖Qᵀy − Rβ‖² + ‖(I − QQᵀ)y‖²`, minimised by `Rβ = Qᵀy` — full; the conditioning of the QR route is `κ(X)`, not `κ(X)²` (the stability statement `CITED [VERIFY]`)**; (g) **Gauss–Markov: under `E[ε] = 0`, `Cov(ε) = σ²I`, and full-rank `X`, `β̂` is unbiased with `Cov(β̂) = σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹` and, among all linear unbiased estimators `Cy`, `Cov(Cy) − σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹` is PSD — a complete proof (write `C = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀ + D`, unbiasedness forces `DX = 0`, the cross term vanishes) and the statement that *the theorem is about linear unbiased estimators only* (ridge, Unit 5, is biased and can have smaller error — Unit 5)**; (h) **the Gaussian MLE: the likelihood derivation, `β̂_MLE = β̂_OLS`, and `σ̂²_MLE = RSS/n`; the unbiasedness of `RSS/(n − p)` — `E[RSS] = σ²(n − p)` from `RSS = εᵀ(I − H)ε` and `trace(I − H) = n − p` — full**; (i) **the variance of a single coefficient and the VIF identity via the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem: the coefficient on `xⱼ` equals the coefficient from regressing `y` on the residual of `xⱼ` after projecting out the other columns — full (a projection argument); hence `Var(β̂ⱼ) = σ²/‖x̃ⱼ‖²` with `‖x̃ⱼ‖² = (1 − Rⱼ²)‖xⱼ − x̄ⱼ‖²`**; (j) **weighted least squares and generalised least squares by the change of variables `ỹ = W^{1/2}y`, `X̃ = W^{1/2}X` — derived**; (k) **leverage: `hᵢᵢ = ‖row i of Q‖²`, `0 ≤ hᵢᵢ ≤ 1`, `Σ hᵢᵢ = p` — full; the leave-one-out residual identity `rᵢ/(1 − hᵢᵢ)` named `CITED [VERIFY]` with an exact rational check**; (l) **counterexamples**: **a rank-deficient `X` with infinitely many minimisers (an exact instance); a near-singular `XᵀX` whose float64 inverse has no correct digits while QR is accurate (a measured, seeded instance); Gauss–Markov's optimality failing outside the linear-unbiased class (named, exact comparison in Unit 5); the OLS covariance formula failing under heteroscedasticity (a seeded coverage measurement of the naive interval)**; **numerical analysis**: Cholesky vs QR vs SVD error on a family of designs with prescribed `κ` (built from a chosen SVD, S4B-03), the rank-cutoff effect, float32 vs float64; **iterative refinement named**; **tool card** for `linmodels.lstsq` (normal-equation, QR, and SVD solvers behind one interface with a stated rank cutoff, exact rational solve for integer data, condition-number and rank report, hat matrix/leverage, standard errors and VIF, weighted least squares, coefficient intervals by formula and bootstrap; - **L4:** **solver failures, each reproduced and diagnosed with a test**: **`inv(XᵀX)` on a near-collinear design (the silent garbage; the condition-number alarm that should have fired); a rank-deficient design where different solvers return different "solutions" (all minimisers, same predictions — the test that checks predictions agree and coefficients do not); ## Unit 3 — Gradient descent versus closed form for linear regression

- **L1:** **when `p` is large or the data stream, we do not solve; we *iterate*: start at `β₀` and repeat `β ← β − η∇J(β)` with `∇J = −(1/n)Xᵀ(y − Xβ)` (Unit 1)**; **a first run by hand: one feature, three points, five steps of GD with an exact rational step size, converging toward the exact normal-equation answer (Unit 2) — a table of `β`, `J`, `‖∇J‖` per step**; **the picture: the bowl-shaped loss, contours, the path of GD; a step size that is too small (slow), just right, too large (divergence — shown by hand)**; **the three algorithms side by side: full-batch GD, stochastic GD (one sample), mini-batch GD — the gradient estimated from a subset (S4B-08, S4B-11 LLN: an unbiased estimate of the full gradient); what changes (noise, cost per step) and what does not (the minimiser, in expectation)**; **why scale features: elongated contours and zig-zagging (a picture, quantified in L2/L3)**; ≥ 3 worked examples with exact checks.
- **L2:** **gradient descent for least squares in code**: a complete vectorised fitter with shape checks; stopping rules (gradient-norm tolerance, relative loss decrease, max iterations — each stated and justified; a failing example for each); the **convergence rate in practice: a seeded experiment measuring iterations-to-tolerance vs the condition number `κ(XᵀX/n)` and vs the step size, against the exact predicted rate (L3)**; **feature scaling's effect: the same data standardised vs raw, measured `κ` and iteration counts (closing the S4B-08 preview), and the effect of centring on the intercept's coupling**; **learning-rate selection: the exact safe range `0 < η < 2/L` with `L = λ_max(XᵀX/n)` (computed by the S4B-03 eigenvalue tools); the exact optimal-parameter result stated in L3)**; **SGD and mini-batches: the gradient-noise variance and its dependence on the batch size (derived in L3; S4B-11 standard error), a constant vs decaying step size (the noise floor vs convergence — a seeded demonstration), the epoch/shuffle convention; a learning-rate schedule named**; **GD vs closed form — the decision table** (`n`, `p`, conditioning, streaming, regularisation, exactness needs, memory; `JUDGEMENT` flagged; cost counts as asymptotic arithmetic, never measured-speed claims): closed form costs `O(np² + p³)` and is exact up to conditioning; each GD step costs `O(np)` (full batch) and needs `O(κ ln(1/ε))` steps; for `p` in the thousands or streaming data iteration wins, for small well-conditioned problems the direct solve wins; **early stopping as regularisation (the Unit 5 link): the GD iterates from `β₀ = 0` move along the singular directions in order of singular value — the exact filter factor `1 − (1 − ησᵢ²/n)ᵗ` (derived in L3; compared with ridge's `σᵢ²/(σᵢ² + nλ)` in Unit 5)**; **the minimum-norm implicit bias (L3): GD from `β₀ = 0` on an underdetermined problem converges to the *same minimum-norm solution* as the pseudo-inverse — verified numerically against Unit 2**; **monitoring a fit: loss curve, gradient norm, validation loss; a divergence alarm (`NaN`/growing loss) and its root causes (step too large, unscaled features, a bug in the gradient — caught by the S4B-07 gradient check)**; **gradient checking as a mandatory test (S4B-07) applied to every gradient in the module**.
- **L3:** **proofs and derivations, written in full:** (a) **the GD update as a linear recursion: with `A = XᵀX/n`, `b = Xᵀy/n`, and `β* = A⁻¹b`, the error `eₜ = βₜ − β*` obeys `eₜ₊₁ = (I − ηA)eₜ`, so `eₜ = (I − ηA)ᵗe₀` — full (the exact solution of the iteration)**; (b) **in the eigenbasis `A = QΛQᵀ`, each component contracts by `(1 − ηλᵢ)ᵗ`; convergence for all `e₀` iff `|1 − ηλᵢ| < 1` for all `i`, i.e., `0 < η < 2/λ_max` — full; the rate `max(|1 − ηλ_min|, |1 − ηλ_max|)`; the optimal `η* = 2/(λ_min + λ_max)` with rate `(κ − 1)/(κ + 1)` — derived; the canonical `η = 1/L` giving rate `1 − 1/κ`; the iteration count `t ≥ κ ln(‖e₀‖/ε)` for accuracy `ε` — derived from `(1 − 1/κ)ᵗ ≤ e^{−t/κ}`**; (c) **the loss decreases monotonically for `η ≤ 1/L` (the descent lemma for a quadratic: `J(β − η∇J) = J(β) − η(1 − ηL/2)‖∇J‖²`-type identity — derived exactly for the quadratic)**; (d) **momentum: the heavy-ball recursion on the quadratic as a 2×2 system per eigen-direction; the optimal parameters give rate `(√κ − 1)/(√κ + 1)` — derived (the characteristic-polynomial argument) or `CITED [VERIFY]` with the numerical verification — say which**; (e) **the SGD gradient estimate is unbiased: `E[g_B] = ∇J` for a uniformly sampled batch `B` (S4B-09 Unit 7 / S4B-11), with variance `σ_g²/|B|` for with-replacement sampling (and the finite-population correction without replacement, `CITED [VERIFY]` or derived) — full; the noise-floor result: with a constant step the iterates fluctuate in a neighbourhood whose size grows with `η` and shrinks with `|B|` — shown by the exact stationary variance of the linear recursion in one dimension**; (f) **the implicit minimum-norm bias: from `β₀ = 0`, every GD iterate lies in `span(rows of X) = row(X)` (since each gradient is `Xᵀ(·)`), and a convergent limit that minimises `J` and lies in `row(X)` is the minimum-norm minimiser (Unit 2 (d)) — full**; (g) **early stopping filter factors: with `X = UΣVᵀ`, `β_t = Σᵢ [1 − (1 − ησᵢ²/n)ᵗ](uᵢᵀy/σᵢ)vᵢ` — derived from (a) with `β₀ = 0`**; (h) **scaling and `κ`: standardising columns can change `κ(XᵀX/n)`; the exact `κ` for a two-feature design as a function of the feature correlation `ρ` is `(1 + |ρ|)/(1 − |ρ|)` for equal-variance standardised features — derived (eigenvalues `1 ± ρ`) — so correlated features slow GD even after scaling**;
