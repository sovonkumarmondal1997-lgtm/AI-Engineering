## Unit 3 — High-dimensional geometry and the curse of dimensionality

- **L1:** what "the curse of dimensionality" refers to (a family of phenomena, **not one theorem**; Bellman's phrase `CITED [VERIFY]`); **three first facts, each computed by hand and in NumPy**: (1) **volume**: the fraction of a cube's volume within distance `ε` of its boundary is `1 − (1 − 2ε)ᵈ` for the unit cube (exact formula; evaluate for `d = 2, 10, 100, 1000`) — **"almost all of the volume is near the surface"**; (2) **the ball inside the cube**: the unit ball's volume as a fraction of the enclosing cube `[−1, 1]ᵈ` shrinks toward `0` (exact values for `d = 2, 3`; a seeded Monte Carlo estimate for larger `d`, with its sample size and standard error); (3) **sparsity**: covering `[0, 1]ᵈ` with a grid of `10` points per axis needs `10ᵈ` points (exact; a table); the **picture** (2D intuition does not extend); **NumPy computation of pairwise distances for random points** in `d = 2, 10, 100, 1000` and the first look at **the spread of distances shrinking relative to their size**; **common misconceptions** ("high-dimensional data is always hopeless"; "distance becomes meaningless" — **both stated as false in general and refined in L2–L4**); ≥ 3 worked examples.
