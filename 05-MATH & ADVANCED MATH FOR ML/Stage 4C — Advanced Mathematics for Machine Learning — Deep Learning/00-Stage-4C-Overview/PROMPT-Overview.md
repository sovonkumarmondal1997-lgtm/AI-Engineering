## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, interviews, or how much mathematics a role needs is exactly one of: (a) cited research tagged `[VERIFY]` with its limits; (b) practitioner opinion tagged `[JUDGEMENT]`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the repository tree; or (d) omitted. **No success rates, no outcome guarantees, no statements about what any named company asks or requires, no invented quotes or anecdotes, no "N% of ML work uses X".** All durations are `[ASSUMPTION]` templates the learner replaces with measured values.
- **Mathematical correctness policy.** Every worked example and every key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation shown line by line in the text, and (2) an independent computation in Python (exact `Fraction`/`sympy` where possible; otherwise NumPy/SciPy/finite differences with a **stated tolerance and reason**; never float `==`). A third check (an autograd oracle if installed, a different library, or Monte Carlo with a stated error bar) is encouraged. **Domains, assumptions, and conventions are stated before use** (differentiability, smoothness, convexity, `log` argument > 0, `0·log 0 := 0`, sample vs population `ddof`, natural log vs log₂ and nats vs bits, row- vs column-vector convention, numerator- vs denominator-layout for Jacobians). **Numerical agreement on examples is evidence, not proof of a general statement; say so.**
- **Derivation discipline (the signature habit of this stage; taught in Unit 1, enforced everywhere).** Every derivation follows: **(i) state the claim and its assumptions; (ii) annotate shapes on every line (`X: (n, d)`, `∂L/∂W: (d, k)`); (iii) derive in index notation first, then convert to matrix form; (iv) shape-check the result; (v) check a limiting/special case; (vi) verify numerically against finite differences and an independent oracle; (vii) record the failure modes (when the identity does not hold).** The **derivation card** records these seven items.
- **Gradient-check policy.** Central differences `(f(x+h e_i) − f(x−h e_i)) / 2h` with a stated `h` justified by truncation-versus-round-off error (derive the trade-off); relative error `|a − n| / max(|a|, |n|, ε)` with stated thresholds (`HEURISTIC`); check in **float64**; test at several random points and on a **non-smooth point** (ReLU at 0, max-pool ties) to show what the check can and cannot tell you; an autograd oracle shares *your* mathematical assumptions, not your bugs — state both facts.
- **Numerical-correctness policy (ML-specific).** float16/bfloat16/float32/float64 and machine epsilon (`np.finfo`); `np.isclose`/`np.allclose` defaults (`rtol=1e-05`, `atol=1e-08`) `[VERIFY]` and why `atol` matters near zero; catastrophic cancellation; stable softmax and log-sum-exp (derive the shift identity); `log1p`/`expm1`; clipping vs epsilon in cross-entropy and their different biases; avoiding `np.linalg.inv` (use `solve`/`lstsq`/`cholesky`); condition number as an error amplifier; summation order and `math.fsum`; determinism (`np.random.default_rng`, BLAS thread effects) — **a result that cannot be reproduced is not a result**.
- **Index and notation convention.** Sums and indices are written **1-based** in text with the **0-based Python translation side by side**; every symbol defined at first use; plain-text math with Unicode (`Σ`, `∇`, `∂`, `∈`, `≈`, `⊙`, `⊗`) — **avoid LaTeX that may not render**; one stated convention for vectors (column), data matrices (rows = samples), and Jacobian layout (`J_ij = ∂f_i/∂x_j`, i.e. numerator layout) used everywhere; where a lesson or library differs, say so.
- **Original-content policy.** All problems, data, and scenarios are original or synthetic. Named results (chain rule, spectral theorem, Eckart–Young, Cramér–Rao, Jensen, Bayes) are referred to **by name and own-words statement only**, never by reproducing a textbook's wording, examples, figures, or exercise sets. No downloaded or scraped content.
- **Randomness and time policy.** Every randomized component takes an explicit `np.random.Generator` or seed; tools never read the system clock inside logic; every Monte Carlo statement carries its sample size and an error estimate.
- **Data and security policy.** All data local, human-readable (JSON/CSV/`.py`/`.ipynb`-with-outputs-stripped), validated, written atomically (`os.replace`); no PII, no telemetry, no network; never `pickle`/`marshal`/`eval`/`exec` on untrusted text; notebooks executed only from the learner's own project directory with the stated warning that opening a notebook runs code.
- **Integrity and ethics policy.** Derivations and notebooks are the learner's own work; AI assistance is allowed for explanation and review, **must be disclosed in the evidence log**, and AI-produced derivations or code are **untrusted hypotheses verified against an oracle**. Honest self-representation; accessibility (text fallbacks for plots); proof anxiety acknowledged as a real experience (awareness only, `[JUDGEMENT]`, no clinical claims).
- **Result status vocabulary:** `PROVED`, `CITED`, `EVIDENCE ONLY`, `HEURISTIC`, `JUDGEMENT` (markers: `[ASSUMPTION]`, `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`, `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`). **Exhaustive or seeded testing never upgrades a status to `PROVED`.**
- **Stage gate convention (inherits the repository's):** ≥ 80% proceed; 60–79% remediate named modules and retake; < 60% repeat prerequisites. Thresholds and weights are `HEURISTIC`/`JUDGEMENT`; **no certificate or credential is implied.**
