## Conventions

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, interviews, performance, library internals, information-theoretic behaviour of real models, or role requirements is exactly one of: (a) cited research/documentation tagged `[VERIFY]` with limits; (b) practitioner opinion tagged `[JUDGEMENT]`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the tree; or (d) omitted. **No success rates, no employer claims, no invented quotes or anecdotes, no benchmark/speed/accuracy figures as facts, and no claim about the information content, compression, or "information flow" of any named model.** Durations and illustrative sizes are `[ASSUMPTION]`/`[ILLUSTRATIVE]`; operation counts are **derived**.
- **Mathematical correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation — **exact (the dyadic-exactness policy below, `Fraction`/`sympy`) wherever possible**, otherwise an **arbitrary-precision (`mpmath`) or independent-library oracle with a stated tolerance and reason** (never float `==`), or **simulation with a stated sample size, seed, and standard error and a pass criterion in standard errors**. **A library function is never the sole oracle for a claim about that function.** Agreement on examples is evidence, not proof; say so.
- **Dyadic-exactness policy (this module's exact oracle).** **When every probability is a power of two (a "dyadic" distribution), entropies, cross-entropies, KL divergences, and mutual informations measured in *bits* are exactly rational (`log₂ 2⁻ᵏ = −k`), so identities (chain rules, DPI, non-negativity cases, equality conditions) can be verified *exactly* with `Fraction`.** For non-dyadic distributions use `mpmath` at ≥ 50 digits, or symbolic `sympy` logs for closed-form cases, with the tolerance stated. **Exhaustive verification in this module is run over named finite families of dyadic distributions; the domain and size are always stated.**
- **Proof policy.** Where Section 6 says "proof", write it **in full**: statement, **hypotheses (finite alphabet or densities; support/absolute-continuity conditions; finite entropy)**, every step justified, and a closing "where each hypothesis was used". **Proofs are given for finite alphabets; the extension to general measures is named (`CITED [VERIFY]`) where it matters (Unit 5), never developed.** Where a result is `CITED [VERIFY]`, say which and give a numerical sanity check. A counterexample is a disproof; agreement on examples is not a proof.

**Required components:**
