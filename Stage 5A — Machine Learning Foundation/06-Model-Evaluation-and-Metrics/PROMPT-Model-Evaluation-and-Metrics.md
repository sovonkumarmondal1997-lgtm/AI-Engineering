## Conventions stated once, used everywhere

- **Claims policy.** Every claim about learning efficacy, study method, hiring, role requirements, the "best" metric, or a cutoff for "good" is exactly one of: (a) cited research/documentation tagged `[VERIFY]` with limits; (b) practitioner opinion/rule of thumb tagged `[JUDGEMENT]` or `HEURISTIC`; (c) a verifiable statement about mathematics, code, or the tree; or (d) omitted. **No general ranking of metrics; no "good AUC / good F1 / good ECE" thresholds presented as laws — every metric value is read against its baseline, its interval, and the decision it serves.** Durations `[ASSUMPTION]`; sizes `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`.
- **Correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation — **exact (`Fraction`/`sympy`/enumeration) on tiny confusion tables and score sets wherever possible**, otherwise `numpy`/`scipy`/scikit-learn with a **stated tolerance and reason**, or simulation with stated `n`, seed, replicates, interval. **Every metric in the module is implemented as a short spec-literal reference that does not import the function under test, and is cross-checked against `sklearn.metrics` (the agreement test, including all edge cases, is part of the lesson).** A library function is never the sole oracle for a claim about that function.
