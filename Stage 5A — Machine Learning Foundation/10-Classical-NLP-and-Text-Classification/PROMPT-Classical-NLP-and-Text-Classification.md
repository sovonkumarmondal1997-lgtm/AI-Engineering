## Unit 4 — Bag-of-words and TF-IDF

- **L1:** **bag-of-words (BoW): represent a document as a vector of token counts over the vocabulary, ignoring order; the document–term matrix; binary vs count vs frequency (count divided by document length)**; **TF-IDF: weight a term by how often it appears in the document (term frequency, TF) and how rare it is across documents (inverse document frequency, IDF): common words get small weights, distinctive words large ones**; **definitions by hand on the 6-document corpus: `tf(t, d) = c(t, d)`; `df(t)` = number of documents containing `t`; the classical `idf(t) = ln(n/df(t))` and the scikit-learn smoothed form `idf(t) = ln((1 + n)/(1 + df(t))) + 1` (the default `smooth_idf=True`: `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`: tested against the installed version); the weight `tf·idf`; **L2 normalisation of each row (so every document vector has unit length) and the resulting cosine similarity as a dot product**; **a first worked example by hand: the 6×|V| count matrix, `df`, both IDFs, the TF-IDF matrix, the normalised rows, and one cosine similarity**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) TF variants (raw count, binary, log-scaled `1 + ln c` (`sublinear_tf`), augmented frequency, length-normalised) and IDF variants (classical, smoothed, probabilistic, none): defined and compared on the tiny corpus;
