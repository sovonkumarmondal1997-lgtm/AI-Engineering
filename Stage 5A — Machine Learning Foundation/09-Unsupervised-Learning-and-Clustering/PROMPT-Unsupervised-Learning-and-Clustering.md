## Unit 2 — k-means: initialisation and cluster count

- **L1:** **k-means partitions `n` rows into `k` clusters by minimising the within-cluster sum of squares `WSS = Σ_cΣ_{i∈C_c}‖x_i − μ_c‖²`, where `μ_c` is the mean of cluster `c`**; **Lloyd's algorithm (the standard procedure): (1) choose `k` initial centroids; (2) *assign* each row to its nearest centroid; (3) *update* each centroid to the mean of its assigned rows; (4) repeat (2)–(3) until the assignments stop changing (or a tolerance/iteration cap is reached)**; **a first worked example by hand: 8 points in 2-D, `k = 2`, a stated initialisation: the assignment table, the new centroids, `WSS` after each iteration (monotone decreasing), convergence in 2–3 iterations; the same in `KMeans(n_clusters=2, init=array, n_init=1)` and in your reference (`inertia_` equals `WSS`: tested; `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`)**; **the centroid is the mean (derived in L3: it minimises the sum of squared distances)**; **the geometry: the clusters are the Voronoi cells of the centroids, so boundaries are straight (perpendicular bisectors) and clusters are convex**; **local minima and initialisation (a worked example where a bad initialisation converges to a worse partition with higher `WSS`)**; **k-means++ (`CITED [VERIFY]` Arthur & Vassilvitskii 2007): choose the first centroid uniformly at random; then choose each next centroid among the rows with probability proportional to the squared distance to the nearest already-chosen centroid (`D²` sampling); spreads the starts out; the default `init` in scikit-learn (`[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`)**; **multiple restarts (`n_init`: run several initialisations and keep the lowest `WSS`; the default's value and meaning changed across versions: `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`: tested)**; **scaling (k-means uses Euclidean distance: unscaled features dominate: standardise or robust-scale inside the pipeline; 5A-03 Unit 6)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **k-means in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) choosing `k` (the module's honest treatment): `WSS` always decreases as `k` grows (to 0 at `k = n`), so minimising it cannot choose `k`; common devices: the **elbow** (plot `WSS` vs `k` and look for a bend; subjective and often absent: a seeded demonstration that uniform noise also shows a smooth "elbow"-looking curve), **silhouette**, **Calinski–Harabasz**, **Davies–Bouldin**, the **gap statistic** (Unit 6), **BIC under a mixture model** (Unit 5), **stability** (Unit 6), and **domain constraints** (the number of segments an organisation can act on, minimum segment size); the module requires several criteria, a null comparison, stability, and the business constraint; disagreement among criteria is reported, not hidden; (2) the variance decomposition `TSS = WSS + BSS` and the "variance explained" `1 − WSS/TSS` (derived in L3; recall 5A-08 Unit 2's between-group identity); (3) restarts and the probability of finding the best partition (if a single initialisation reaches the best-known partition with probability `q`, `r` independent restarts succeed with probability `1 − (1 − q)^r`; the learner estimates `q` by simulation on tiny datasets where the global optimum is known by exhaustive search: Unit 1's partition enumeration; k-means++'s effect on `q` measured with intervals — evidence about these generators); (4) cost per iteration `O(nkp)` distance evaluations (counted); iterations to convergence vary; `algorithm="lloyd"/"elkan"` (triangle-inequality acceleration: same result, fewer distance computations: counted; `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`); `MiniBatchKMeans` (updates from random mini-batches: faster, noisier, slightly worse `WSS`: measured with intervals; use for large `n`); (5) empty clusters (a centroid with no assigned rows: a handling rule (reinitialise at the farthest point) and the library's behaviour `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`: tested); (6) assumptions and failure modes (seeded demonstrations: unequal cluster sizes pull centroids toward large clusters; unequal densities and variances give wrong boundaries (k-means splits a big diffuse cluster and merges small tight ones); elongated and non-convex clusters (rings, moons) are cut incorrectly; outliers drag centroids and may take their own clusters; high dimension with noise features washes out structure); (7) k-means as the hard-assignment, equal-spherical-covariance limit of a Gaussian mixture (derived in Unit 5); (8) robust variants named (k-medians/k-medoids (PAM): `CITED [VERIFY]`; `kmodes` for categoricals: named; optional libraries never required); (9) k-means for compression (vector quantisation: replace each row by its centroid; the distortion is `WSS/n`; the choice of `k` is a rate–distortion decision: link 4C-06; no claims); (10) feature engineering (distances to centroids or cluster IDs as supervised features: only inside folds: Unit 7)**; **assigning new rows (the stored scaler and centroids: nearest centroid; a distance-to-nearest-centroid monitor; a threshold above which a row is flagged "does not fit any segment": 5A-02 Unit 8; no claim of anomaly detection: 5B `06`)**; **reproducibility (seeds for the initial draw and for restarts; results independent of `n_jobs`; label permutation across runs: Unit 7)**; **PCA before k-means (a reduction that can denoise or distort: the module's recommendation is to evaluate with and without, reporting explained variance and stability: 4C-01; 5B `05`)**; **choosing a distance (Euclidean implied by the mean update; other metrics need other centre definitions (medoids))**; **initialisation by domain (seeding centroids with prototypes: a warm start; the risk of confirmation)**.
- **L3:** **proofs, written in full:** (a) **the mean minimises the sum of squared distances: for any point `c`, `Σ_i‖x_i − c‖² = Σ_i‖x_i − x̄‖² + n‖x̄ − c‖²`, so `c = x̄` is the unique minimiser** — complete (add and subtract `x̄`; (c) **`TSS = WSS + BSS` with `BSS = Σ_cn_c‖μ_c − μ‖²`; for `k = 2`, `BSS = (n_1n_2/n)‖μ_1 − μ_2‖²` (recalled from 5A-08 Unit 2's variance-reduction identity, now in `p` dimensions)** — complete; (d) **the optimal `WSS` as a function of `k` is non-increasing: from an optimal partition with `k` clusters, splitting any cluster with ≥ 2 distinct rows into two (any split) weakly decreases `WSS` by (a)-type argument; ## Unit 3 — Hierarchical clustering and dendrograms

- **L1:** **agglomerative (bottom-up) clustering builds a tree of merges instead of a single partition: start with each row as its own cluster; repeatedly merge the two closest clusters until one cluster remains; the sequence of merges is the hierarchy; cutting the tree at a height (or at a number of clusters) yields a partition**; **the dendrogram: a tree whose leaves are rows and whose merge heights are the distances at which clusters joined; reading it by hand (which rows merge first; large vertical gaps suggest well-separated groupings, as a heuristic)**; **linkage — how to measure the distance between two clusters `A, B` (each defined by its formula and computed by hand)**: **single (`min d(a, b)`), complete (`max d(a, b)`), average (UPGMA: mean of all pairwise distances), and Ward (merge the pair that gives the smallest increase in total within-cluster sum of squares)**; **a first worked example by hand: 6 points on a line; the distance matrix; the merge sequence and heights under single, complete, average, and Ward linkage; the four dendrograms as nested tuples; the same with `scipy.cluster.hierarchy.linkage` (the `Z` matrix format: each row `[idx_a, idx_b, height, size]`; new clusters get indices `n, n + 1, …`: `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`, `[VERIFY]`: tested) and `AgglomerativeClustering`**; **cutting (`fcluster` with `maxclust` or `distance` criteria; `distance_threshold` and `n_clusters` in `AgglomerativeClustering` are mutually exclusive: `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`)**; **cost (the distance matrix is `n(n − 1)/2` numbers; the naive algorithm is `O(n³)` time and `O(n²)` memory: counted; practical for thousands of rows, not millions)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **hierarchical clustering in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) linkage behaviours (seeded demonstrations on 2-D point sets, reported as numbers): single linkage *chains* (a thin bridge of points joins clusters; good for elongated non-convex shapes, bad with noise), complete linkage favours compact clusters of similar diameter and is sensitive to outliers, average linkage is a compromise, Ward's method favours compact, similarly sized, spherical clusters and is tied to Euclidean distance (derived: it minimises the SSE increase) — evidence about these generators, no general ranking; (2) **Ward's merge cost** `Δ(A, B) = (n_An_B/(n_A + n_B))‖μ_A − μ_B‖²` (derived in L3: exactly the between-group SS of two clusters: the identity of 5A-08 Unit 2 in `p` dimensions); (3) the **Lance–Williams update** (derived for single, complete, average, and Ward in L3): after merging `A` and `B`, the distance from the new cluster to any other cluster `C` is a function of `d(A, C)`, `d(B, C)`, `d(A, B)` and sizes, so the full distance matrix need not be recomputed (`O(n²)` per merge naively, `O(n²)`-ish total with nearest-neighbour chains for reducible linkages: named, `CITED [VERIFY]`); ## Unit 5 — Gaussian mixture models and EM intuition

- **L1:** **a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) says the data came from a mixture of `k` Gaussian components: pick a component `c` with probability `π_c` (the mixing weights), then draw `x ~ N(μ_c, Σ_c)`; the density is `p(x) = Σ_cπ_cN(x | μ_c, Σ_c)` (recall 4C-04 Unit 4 and 5A-01 Unit 6)**; **soft clustering: each row gets *responsibilities* `γ_{ic} = π_cN(x_i | μ_c, Σ_c)/Σ_jπ_jN(x_i | μ_j, Σ_j)` — the posterior probability that component `c` generated row `i` (Bayes' rule); a hard assignment takes the largest responsibility; the entropy of a row's responsibilities measures its ambiguity**; **fitting by the EM algorithm (the intuition; derived in L3; recall 4C-05 Unit 10): the component memberships are unobserved ("latent"); alternate: **E-step**: compute responsibilities with the current parameters; **M-step**: re-estimate each component's weight, mean, and covariance as *responsibility-weighted* averages; repeat until the log-likelihood stops improving**; **a first worked example by hand: 6 one-dimensional points, `k = 2`, fixed initial parameters (a stated variance): the responsibilities table, the updated means/variances/weights after one iteration, and the log-likelihood before and after (non-decreasing); the same in `GaussianMixture(n_components=2, covariance_type=…, random_state)` (`weights_`, `means_`, `covariances_`, `predict_proba`, `score_samples`, `converged_`, `n_iter_`; `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`)**; **covariance types (each defined): `full` (each component its own full covariance: ellipsoids with arbitrary orientation), `tied` (one covariance shared by all components), `diag` (axis-aligned ellipsoids), `spherical` (a single variance per component); the parameter count of each (derived in L3)**; **relationship to k-means (hard assignments and equal spherical covariances: derived in L3)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **GMMs in depth (derived/measured)**: **(1) initialisation and local maxima (EM converges to a local maximum of the likelihood; initialise from k-means (the library's default `init_params="kmeans"`: `[VERSION-SENSITIVE]`), random responsibilities, or random means;
