## Unit 2 — Variational inference and the ELBO

- **L1:** recall (one line, linked): KL divergence and its non-negativity (4C-06 Unit 3), Jensen's inequality (4A/4B), the evidence (Unit 1), EM via the ELBO (4C-05 Unit 10); **the idea**: **we cannot compute the posterior `p(z | x)`, so we choose a simple family `q_φ(z)` and make it as close as possible to the posterior; closeness is KL; the quantity we can compute and optimise is the evidence lower bound**; **the ELBO**: **`ℒ(q) = E_q[log p(x, z) − log q(z)] = E_q[log p(x | z)] − KL(q(z)‖p(z))`** — **reconstruction term minus regulariser (stated; both forms proved in L3)**; **the central identity (stated; proved in L3): `log p(x) = ℒ(q) + KL(q(z)‖p(z | x))`, so `ℒ ≤ log p(x)` with equality iff `q = p(· | x)`**; **a worked exact example by hand**: **a two-class discrete latent model with a chosen `q`: compute `log p(x)`, `ℒ(q)`, and `KL(q‖posterior)` and verify the identity digit for digit**; **mean-field**: **`q(z) = ∏_jq_j(z_j)` (stated; consequences in L2)**; **amortised inference**: **instead of one `q` per data point, one network `q_φ(z | x)` shared across data points (stated; the amortisation gap defined in L2)**; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **three equivalent forms of the ELBO (each derived)**: **(i) `E_q[log p(x, z)] + H(q)` (energy + entropy: the free-energy form, link 4C-05 Unit 10 and 4C-06); (ii) `log p(x) − KL(q‖posterior)`; (iii) `E_q[log p(x | z)] − KL(q‖prior)`** — **what each form is good for (computation, interpretation, gradient estimation); which terms are analytic (Gaussian KL) and which need sampling**; **why a *lower* bound and why maximise it: maximising `ℒ` over `θ` raises a bound on the evidence; maximising over `φ` shrinks the KL gap — the two roles (derived)**; **coordinate ascent variational inference (CAVI) for mean-field (derived for a Gaussian-mixture-style model and for a two-variable correlated Gaussian)**: **`log q_j*(z_j) = E_{q_{−j}}[log p(x, z)] + const` (derived: optimise `ℒ` over one factor holding the others fixed); each update does not decrease `ℒ` (monotone ascent); the mean-field fit to a correlated 2-D Gaussian recovers the correct mean but **underestimates the marginal variances** (derived exactly: the optimal factor precisions are the diagonal of the precision matrix, `1/Λ_jj` variance — `CITED [VERIFY]` Bishop, *PRML*, ch. 10; verified numerically)**; **the direction of the KL matters (derived; link 4C-06 Unit 3)**: **minimising `KL(q‖p)` is mode-seeking/zero-forcing (`q` avoids regions where `p` is small); `KL(p‖q)` is mass-covering — a worked bimodal target fitted by a single Gaussian under each direction (computed on a grid; the optimal Gaussian under `KL(p‖q)` matches the moments of `p` — derived)**; **amortisation**: **the amortisation gap `log p(x) − ℒ(q_φ)` splits into the approximation gap (best `q` in the family vs the posterior) and the amortisation gap (best per-`x` `q` vs the shared network's `q`) — defined and measured on the linear-Gaussian model where the exact posterior is available (`CITED [VERIFY]` Cremer, Li & Duvenaud 2018)**; **the importance-weighted bound (IWAE-type)**: **`ℒ_K = E_{z_{1..K} ~ q}[log (1/K)Σ_kw_k]`, `w_k = p(x, z_k)/q(z_k)`; `ℒ_1 = ℒ`; `ℒ_K ≤ log p(x)`; `ℒ_K` is non-decreasing in `K` and converges to `log p(x)` under conditions (stated; proved/cited in L3) — `CITED [VERIFY]` Burda, Grosse & Salakhutdinov 2016; its Monte Carlo estimate is the standard held-out evidence estimator (with the Jensen caveat)**; **the sandwich: for held-out evaluation, report a lower bound (ELBO or `ℒ_K`) and, where possible, note that upper bounds exist (named, `CITED [VERIFY]`; no development) — a single number from a bound is not "the likelihood"**; **the gradient of the ELBO (derived structure)**: **w.r.t. `θ`: `E_q[∇_θ log p_θ(x, z)]` (no problem: `q` does not depend on `θ`); w.r.t. `φ`: the expectation is over a distribution that depends on `φ` — the central difficulty resolved in Unit 3 by reparameterisation (or by the score-function estimator); the exact ELBO gradient on the linear-Gaussian model as the oracle**; **stochastic variational inference and minibatches (the ELBO is a sum over data; an unbiased minibatch estimate scales by `N/B` — derived; link 4C-03 Unit 10)**; **the reduction convention (Section 6): the per-example ELBO summed over data dimensions and latent dimensions, averaged over the batch; the conversion to bits per dimension (`−ℒ/(D ln 2)`) — worked with `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` numbers**; **the ELBO and EM (recalled from 4C-05 Unit 10): EM sets `q = p(z | x)` exactly (E-step) and maximises over `θ` (M-step);
