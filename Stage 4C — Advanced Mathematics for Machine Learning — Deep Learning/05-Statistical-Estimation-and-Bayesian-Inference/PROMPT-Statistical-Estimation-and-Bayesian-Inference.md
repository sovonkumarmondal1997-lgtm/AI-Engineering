- **Mathematical correctness policy.** Every worked example and key is verified **two independent ways**: (1) a hand derivation in the text, and (2) an independent computation — **exact (`Fraction`/`sympy`, full enumeration of a finite sample space) wherever possible**, otherwise **simulation with a stated sample size, seed, and Monte Carlo standard error and a stated pass criterion in standard errors**, or an independent library oracle with a stated tolerance and reason (never float `==`). **A simulation is never the sole oracle for a claim about the same simulation code.** Agreement on examples is evidence, not proof; say so.
- **Proof policy.** Where Section 6 says "proof", write it **in full**: statement, **hypotheses**, every step justified, and a closing "where each hypothesis was used". **Regularity conditions are named once (Section 6, "Regularity conditions") and cited by label wherever a proof uses them.** Where a result is `CITED [VERIFY]`, say which and give a numerical sanity check. **Measure-theoretic technicalities are named, not developed** (as in 4C-04). A counterexample is a disproof; agreement on examples is not a proof.
- **Regularity conditions (stated once, labelled R1–R5, referenced by label).** **(R1)** the model `{p(x | θ)}` is identifiable (`θ ≠ θ′ ⇒ p(· | θ) ≠ p(· | θ′)`); **(R2)** the support of `p(x | θ)` does not depend on `θ`; **(R3)** `θ₀` is an interior point of the parameter space; **(R4)** `log p(x | θ)` is three times continuously differentiable in `θ` with derivatives dominated by an integrable function (so that differentiation and integration may be exchanged); **(R5)** the Fisher information `I(θ₀)` exists and is positive definite. **Each statement that needs them says which; each counterexample says which one it violates** (e.g., `Uniform(0, θ)` violates R2; an over-parameterised model violates R1 and R5).
- **Frequentist–Bayesian policy.** **Both are mathematical tools with different interpretations; the syllabus states each interpretation exactly (a 95% confidence interval is a procedure with 95% long-run coverage — not "a 95% probability that this interval contains `θ`"; a 95% credible interval is a posterior probability statement conditional on the model, the prior, and the data) and verifies frequentist coverage of Bayesian procedures by simulation where relevant. No "which is right" debate: opinions are `JUDGEMENT`.**
- **Statistical-honesty policy.** **Every simulation reports `n`, seeds, replicates, SEs; coverage studies use ≥ 2000 replicates with binomial SEs; never the best of many seeds; priors, link functions, bin counts, and regularisation strengths are reported; hyperparameters are tuned on validation data and never on the test data; a calibration plot or an interval-coverage claim always states the number of samples behind it with an interval.**
- **Notation and conventions.** Parameters `θ ∈ ℝᵖ`; data `x₁, …, xₙ` i.i.d. from `p(x | θ₀)` unless stated; **`ℓ(θ) = Σᵢ log p(xᵢ | θ)` is the log-likelihood (the symbol `ℓ` is never used for a loss)**; **`NLL(θ) = −ℓ(θ)` (sum) and `NLL̄ = −ℓ/n` (per-sample mean)** — the syllabus states which is meant; **loss functions are written `Loss(ŷ, y)`**; score `s(θ) = ∇_θ log p(x | θ)` (a **column**); `I(θ) = Cov_θ(s) ∈ ℝ^{p×p}` is the **per-observation** Fisher information and `Iₙ = nI`; **posterior `p(θ | D)`**; **data matrices `X` are `(n, d)` with rows as samples**; `log` is the natural log (nats; bits only if stated); `Φ`/`φ` the standard normal cdf/pdf; **hats denote estimators/estimates and the text says which** (the random variable `θ̂(X)` vs a realised value); **"bias" is always the statistical `E[θ̂] − θ` — the term is never used for the ML "inductive bias" without a label**; **calibration: `p̂` is a predicted probability of the positive class (binary) or the top-label confidence (multiclass), stated; ECE uses `M` equal-width bins with `M` reported (default `M = 10`, `HEURISTIC`)**; indices 1-based in mathematics, 0-based in Python, shown side by side; plain-text math with Unicode (`θ`, `∇`, `Σ`, `∫`, `≈`, `∝`, `⊥`, `ᵀ`, `‖·‖`), **no LaTeX**; every array carries a shape annotation.
- **Numerical policy.** float32 vs float64 and `np.finfo`; `np.isclose`/`np.allclose` defaults and why `atol` matters near zero `[VERIFY]`; **all likelihood work in log space (`logsumexp`, `log1p`, `gammaln`, `expit`/`log_expit`-style stable forms)**; **never `log(prod(...))`; never `log(det)` (use `slogdet`/Cholesky); never `inv` (use `solve`)**; **clipping probabilities by `ε` biases the loss — prefer logit-space formulations (link `../07-…/12`)**; **Poisson and Gamma likelihoods use `gammaln` for factorial terms**; **IRLS weights underflow as fitted probabilities approach 0/1 — detect separation, do not silently clip**; **EM responsibilities are computed in log space**; determinism via `np.random.default_rng(seed)` only (`SeedSequence.spawn` for parallel replicates — link 4C-04); **numerical optima are verified by a certificate (gradient norm, Hessian sign with tolerance, comparison with a closed form or an independent optimiser)**.
- **Degenerate-case policy.** Non-existent MLE (separable data), non-identifiable or singular Fisher information, improper posteriors, zero-probability observations under the model, empty classes, collapsed mixture components, non-PSD covariance, NaN/Inf, and `n < p` where a method needs `n ≥ p` **raise `ValueError` (or a documented subclass) with a stable message** `E-<TOOL>-<NNN>: <text>` or return a documented status object (`converged`, `max_iter`, `no_mle`, `singular_fisher`, `improper_posterior`, `collapsed_component`, `not_identifiable`); no tool returns a silent wrong answer; every iterative method **reports iterations, log-likelihood trace, gradient norm/parameter change, and a termination reason**.
- **Original-content policy.** All problems, models, and data are original/synthetic. Named results (the Cramér–Rao bound, the Rao–Blackwell theorem, Fisher–Neyman factorisation, Wilks' theorem, the information-matrix equality, Bernstein–von Mises, the EM monotonicity theorem, Jeffreys' prior, conformal coverage) are referred to **by name and your own statement/proof only**; never reproduce a textbook's wording, examples, figures, or exercise sets.
- **Item format (all practice items and self-checks):** `ID`, `Lesson`, `Level` (L1–L4), `Can-do ID`, `Stem`, `Answer format` (MCQ-4 / numeric-exact / numeric-tolerance / symbolic-expression / model-identification / short-proof / simulation-with-criterion / code), `Key` (exact; `Fraction` strings; distributions in a canonical parameter form), `Hand solution`, `Oracle` (**the name of a registered pure Python function in a fixed registry — never an `eval`ed string**), `Distractor rationale` (**each MCQ distractor produced by an error-model function for a named misconception — e.g., likelihood read as a density in `θ`; `1/n` vs `1/(n − 1)`; Fisher information using `E[s²]` vs `−E[s]`-type errors; `I` vs `1/I` in the CRB; Wald interval invariance assumed; MAP vs posterior mean confused; prior variance vs precision confused in ridge `λ`; ridge `λ = σ²/τ²` inverted; credible vs confidence interpretation; posterior predictive variance missing the noise term; ECE bin-count dependence ignored; CV used for both tuning and reporting; EM responsibilities not normalised; link function inverted — not typed by hand; exactly one key; no distractor equals the key; no duplicate options**), `Time estimate [ASSUMPTION]`, `Difficulty` (Easy/Medium/Hard by explicit criteria), `Tags`.
- **Data/security policy.** All data local, human-readable (JSON/CSV/`.py`), validated, written atomically (`os.replace`); no PII, no telemetry, no network; never `pickle`/`marshal`/`eval`/`exec` on untrusted text; `np.load(..., allow_pickle=False)`; **model specifications (distribution, link, prior) are registered Python callables or parsed by an allow-list parser, never `eval`**; **fitted-model artifacts (parameters, priors, scalers, calibration maps) are strict versioned JSON**; strict loaders reject unknown fields and enforce size caps; sizes validated before allocation.

## Unit 2 — Maximum likelihood estimation

- **L1:** recall (one line, linked): the likelihood and score (Unit 1), setting a derivative to zero and checking curvature (4B-05, 4C-03 Unit 2); **the maximum likelihood estimator** `θ̂ = argmax_θ ℓ(θ)` — "the parameter that makes the data most probable"; **closed-form MLEs derived by hand (each: write `ℓ`, differentiate, solve, check the sign of the second derivative)**: **Bernoulli `p̂ = k/n`**; **Gaussian `μ̂ = x̄` and `σ̂² = (1/n)Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²` — note the `1/n` (the MLE of the variance is *biased*; preview of Unit 3)**; **Poisson `λ̂ = x̄`**; **exponential `λ̂ = 1/x̄`**; **categorical (multinomial) `p̂ₖ = nₖ/n` — derived with a Lagrange multiplier for `Σpₖ = 1` (link 4C-03 Unit 3) and by Gibbs' inequality (link 4B-13 lesson `04`)**; **MLE and least squares**: with Gaussian noise of fixed variance, maximising the likelihood of a linear model is minimising the sum of squares (**derived; this is why least squares is "the" regression loss under a Gaussian-noise assumption — link 4B-14 and 4C-01 Unit 4**); **MLE and cross-entropy**: logistic regression's MLE minimises binary cross-entropy (**derived; link 4B-14 lesson `04`**); **numerical MLE when there is no closed form**: gradient ascent on `ℓ` / descent on `NLL̄` (link 4C-03 Units 5, 11) on a Gamma-shape or logistic example; ≥ 3 worked examples.
- **L2:** **numerical MLE in practice**: **Newton's method and Fisher scoring** (`θ ← θ + I(θ)⁻¹s(θ)` — the expected-information version; **derived from the quadratic model of `ℓ`; for the canonical exponential family it equals IRLS — derived in Unit 9; **verification by a certificate**: gradient norm, Hessian negative-definite check (with the 4C-01 Unit 7 tolerance policy), and comparison with a closed form or an independent optimiser — **the "MLE is a solution of `s(θ) = 0`, but not every root is a maximum" caution**; **when the MLE does not exist or is not unique**: **separable logistic regression has no MLE (the likelihood increases as `‖w‖ → ∞` — a measured divergence; link 4C-03 Unit 2; the remedies: regularisation (Unit 5), Firth's penalty named `CITED [VERIFY]`, or more data)**;
